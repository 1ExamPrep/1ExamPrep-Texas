### Exam Details

The Texas Master Electrician examination consists of **two separate portions**: the NEC Knowledge Portion and the Calculations Portion. Both portions are multiple choice, and candidates must pass each portion with a minimum score of 70%.

**NEC Knowledge Portion:**

- **Time Allowed:** 150 minutes
- **Passing Score:** 70%
- **Format:** Multiple choice

### Open Book Test

The Texas Master Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The approved technical reference is the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own compliant NEC book to the testing center.

Any soft-bound edition of the National Electrical Code may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as an examination reference.

The NEC may be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes completed before the examination session. Permanent index tabs manufactured or provided by the NEC publisher are permitted. Homemade, DIY, or non-NEC tabs are not allowed.

Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or otherwise mark the NEC during the examination session. Additional loose, taped, adhered, or stapled paper is prohibited.

Open-book testing still requires strong preparation. Candidates should know which NEC chapter or article is likely to contain the answer before searching. Master-level questions frequently combine several provisions, so simply looking for keywords may not be enough.

### Licensing Steps

1. **Hold a Texas Journeyman Electrician license.** The Master Electrician pathway builds on the Journeyman Electrician credential.
2. **Maintain the Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years.** Texas requires the applicant to have held the Journeyman Electrician license for the required period before applying for Master Electrician licensure.
3. **Complete the required electrical experience.** Applicants must document at least 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas.
4. **Obtain experience verification.** Each supervising Master Electrician must verify the qualifying experience used for the application.
5. **Submit the Master Electrician license application.** The completed application and required documentation are submitted to the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.
6. **Receive examination approval.** TDLR reviews the application and notifies approved applicants that they may take the licensing examination.
7. **Schedule through PSI.** Eligible candidates schedule the Master Electrician examination with the state testing provider.
8. **Prepare for both examination portions.** Study the NEC Knowledge Portion separately from the Calculations Portion while also practicing combined code application.
9. **Use the 2026 NEC during preparation.** Become familiar with articles, tables, calculations, definitions, and common cross-references.
10. **Pass both examination portions.** A minimum score of 70% is required on each portion.
11. **Complete all remaining licensing requirements.** Satisfy the applicable TDLR requirements for issuance of the Master Electrician license.

### State Requirements

A Texas Master Electrician is licensed to install, maintain, or extend electrical wiring systems and related apparatus, equipment, and service entrance conductors within the scope established by Texas law.

Applicants must have held a **Texas Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years** before applying for the Master Electrician license.

Applicants must also document at least **12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician**.

Journeyman Industrial Electrician experience may not be used to satisfy the experience requirement for the Texas Master Electrician license.

The qualifying experience must be documented and signed by each supervising Master Electrician. TDLR may verify the information submitted with the application.

The Texas Master Electrician license is an individual license. It is distinct from a Texas Electrical Contractor business license. An electrical contracting business must satisfy the separate requirements applicable to Electrical Contractors, including the required Master Electrician of Record structure.

Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and are subject to renewal requirements.

The **2026 National Electrical Code** became the state electrical code effective September 1, 2026, and the current Master Electrician examination is referenced to this edition.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Theory, and Plans:** Review Article 100 definitions, electrical theory, plan interpretation, voltage, current, resistance, power, and the relationships used in electrical calculations.

**Services:** Services are an important area on both examination portions. Study service calculations, service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, available fault current, and separately derived systems.

**Feeders:** Review feeder ampacity, feeder load calculations, overcurrent protection, grounded conductors, grounding and bonding, and conductor sizing.

**Branch Circuits:** Branch Circuit Conductors account for 11 items on the NEC Knowledge Portion. Review conductor sizing, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, voltage considerations, grounding, and specialized branch-circuit requirements.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 is essential for Master Electrician preparation. Study services, feeders, separately derived systems, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, and fault-current paths.

**Wiring Methods:** Review raceways, cables, boxes, enclosures, conductor fill, physical protection, wet locations, underground installations, and Chapter 3 requirements.

**Electrical Equipment:** Study equipment ratings, receptacles, switches, appliances, HVAC equipment, luminaires, working space, disconnect requirements, and general installation provisions.

**Motors and Generators:** Motors and generators are especially important in the Calculations Portion, where they account for six items. Review motor full-load currents, conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, generators, and related calculations.

**Control Devices and Disconnecting Means:** Study controllers, switches, contactors, disconnect ratings, locations, accessibility, grouping, and lockability.

**Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions:** Review hazardous locations, healthcare-related installations, pools, marinas, emergency systems, standby systems, data and communications equipment, and other specialized NEC topics relevant to the examination outline.

**Renewable Energy Technologies:** Review photovoltaic systems, energy-storage-related code provisions where applicable, and electric vehicle charging equipment.

**Service Calculations:** These calculations deserve repeated practice. Candidates should understand demand factors, general lighting loads, receptacle loads, appliances, heating and cooling, motors, and other loads used to size services.

**Feeder Calculations:** Practice feeder load calculations and conductor sizing while applying the appropriate demand factors and NEC requirements.

**Motor Calculations:** Be comfortable using NEC motor tables rather than relying only on equipment nameplate values when the code requires table values. Practice conductor, overload, short-circuit protection, and feeder calculations involving multiple motors.

**Raceway and Box Calculations:** Review conductor fill, raceway fill, box fill, pull-box sizing, and conductor volume allowances.

**Conductor Ampacity:** Practice navigating Article 310 and applying temperature limitations, adjustment factors, correction factors, terminal temperature requirements, and conductor ampacity tables.

**Code Navigation:** Use the 2026 NEC while completing practice-oriented preparation. Train yourself to recognize the subject of a question before opening the code. This can reduce search time and improve consistency.

**Separate Study for Each Portion:** Treat NEC Knowledge and Calculations as distinct exam-preparation goals. The Knowledge Portion rewards broad familiarity with code requirements, while the Calculations Portion demands more time per question and stronger numerical application.

**Time Management:** The NEC Knowledge Portion provides 150 minutes for 75 total items, while the Calculations Portion provides 170 minutes for 33 total items. Candidates should practice under timed conditions so calculations do not consume excessive testing time.
