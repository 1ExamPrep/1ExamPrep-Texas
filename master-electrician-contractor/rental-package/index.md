### State Requirements

A Texas Master Electrician is licensed to install, maintain, or extend electrical wiring systems and the related apparatus, equipment, and service entrance conductors within the scope established by Texas law.

To apply for a Texas Master Electrician license, an applicant must have held a **Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years**.

The applicant must also document at least **12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas**.

Qualifying experience must be documented and verified. Texas does not allow Journeyman Industrial Electrician experience to be used to qualify for the Master Electrician credential.

The Master Electrician license is an individual credential. A Texas Electrical Contractor license is a separate business license. An electrical contractor must employ a licensed Master Electrician who serves as the Master Electrician of Record. The Master Electrician may also own the contracting business.

A Master Electrician may generally be assigned to only one Electrical Contractor unless the Master Electrician owns more than 50% of the electrical contracting business.

Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and are subject to annual renewal requirements.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Services:** Services are a major topic on both portions of the Master Electrician examination. Review service calculations, conductors, equipment, disconnects, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, fault current, and separately derived systems.

**Feeders:** Study feeder conductor sizing, load calculations, grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, overcurrent protection, and applicable demand factors.

**Branch Circuits:** Review conductor sizing, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, circuit requirements, grounding, and branch-circuit calculations.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 should receive substantial attention. Candidates should understand grounding electrode systems, bonding jumpers, equipment grounding conductors, services, feeders, and separately derived systems.

**Wiring Methods:** Review raceways, cables, conductors, boxes, enclosures, conductor fill, box fill, physical protection, wet locations, and Chapter 3 installation requirements.

**Electrical Equipment:** Study receptacles, switches, luminaires, appliances, HVAC equipment, working clearances, equipment ratings, disconnect requirements, and general installation rules.

**Motors and Generators:** Motors and generators are especially important on the Calculations Portion. Review full-load current tables, motor conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, feeders supplying multiple motors, and generator requirements.

**Special Occupancies and Equipment:** Review hazardous locations, pools, emergency systems, standby systems, specialized equipment, and other NEC subjects identified on the examination outline.

**Renewable Energy Technologies:** Study photovoltaic systems and electric vehicle charging equipment along with related conductor, disconnect, overcurrent, and equipment requirements.

**Service and Feeder Calculations:** Practice load calculations repeatedly. Candidates should understand demand factors, general lighting loads, appliances, motors, heating, cooling, and other loads used to size services and feeders.

**Motor Calculations:** Practice finding the correct NEC motor-table values and applying them to conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit protection, and feeder calculations.

**Code Navigation:** Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC during course study. Practice identifying the subject first, finding the correct article or table, and confirming the rule before selecting an answer.

**Timed Preparation:** The NEC Knowledge Portion and Calculations Portion have different pacing requirements. Candidates should practice them separately and become comfortable completing code lookups and calculations within realistic time limits.
