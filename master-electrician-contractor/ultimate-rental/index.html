Exam Details

The Texas Master Electrician examination is divided into two separate portions: the NEC Knowledge Portion and the Calculations Portion. Candidates must pass both portions.

NEC Knowledge Portion:

  • Total Items: 75, including 5 non-scored items
  • Time Allowed: 150 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%

The NEC Knowledge Portion includes:

  • Definitions, Theory, and Plans: 7 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 11 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 7 items
  • Branch Circuit Conductors: 11 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 7 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 7 items
  • Motors and Generators: 7 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 3 items
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: 7 items
  • Renewable Energy Technologies: 3 items

Calculations Portion:

  • Total Items: 33, including 3 non-scored items
  • Time Allowed: 170 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%

The Calculations Portion includes:

  • Calculations and Theory: 2 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 8 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 3 items
  • Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: 4 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 2 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 2 items
  • Motors and Generators: 6 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 1 item
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: 1 item
  • Renewable Energy Technologies: 1 item

Time remaining on one exam portion does not transfer to the other. Candidates should therefore practice the NEC Knowledge and Calculations portions independently and become comfortable managing time for each section.

Open Book Test

The Texas Master Electrician examination is an open book test.

The technical reference for the current examination is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing an acceptable NEC reference to the testing center.

Soft-bound NEC editions may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before the examination, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the testing session.

The included rental NEC is highlighted and tabbed so candidates can practice with an organized reference throughout their study period. The extended 1 year of course access provides additional time to develop familiarity with major code articles, tables, exceptions, definitions, and calculation procedures.

Because the Master Electrician exam includes a dedicated Calculations Portion, successful preparation requires more than simply finding code sections. Candidates should understand the underlying calculation process and know which NEC tables, demand factors, conductor rules, and equipment provisions apply.

Licensing Steps

  1. Hold a Texas Journeyman Electrician license. The Master Electrician credential builds on the Journeyman Electrician license.
  2. Meet the required license-duration period. Applicants must have held the Texas Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years.
  3. Complete the required electrical experience. Applicants must document at least 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.
  4. Obtain experience verification. Qualifying experience must be documented by the Master Electrician or Master Electricians who supervised the applicant.
  5. Prepare the licensing application. Applicants submit the required information and supporting documentation to the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.
  6. Use the included Application Service. Application Service is included with this Ultimate package to support the application process.
  7. Receive examination authorization. After TDLR reviews and approves the application, the applicant becomes eligible to test.
  8. Schedule through PSI. Approved candidates schedule the Texas Master Electrician examination through PSI.
  9. Prepare for both examination portions. Study NEC knowledge separately from electrical calculations while practicing how the two areas connect.
  10. Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC. Practice reference navigation regularly during the course-access period.
  11. Use the full year of online preparation. The package includes 1 year of course access for structured review and practice.
  12. Pass both examination portions. Candidates must achieve at least 70% on each portion.
  13. Complete the remaining state licensing requirements. Follow the applicable TDLR process for issuance of the Master Electrician license.

State Requirements

A Texas Master Electrician is licensed to perform electrical work within the scope established by Texas law. This credential represents an advanced level of individual electrician licensing and carries responsibilities beyond those of the Journeyman Electrician.

An applicant must have held a Texas Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years.

The applicant must also document at least 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.

Qualifying experience must be verified as part of the state application process. Candidates should gather experience documentation carefully so that required hours can be properly supported.

Journeyman Industrial Electrician experience does not count toward the experience requirement for the Texas Master Electrician credential.

The Texas Master Electrician license is an individual credential. A Texas Electrical Contractor license is a separate business credential. An electrical contracting business must satisfy the requirements applicable to the business license and must have a licensed Master Electrician serving as the Master Electrician of Record.

A Master Electrician may also own the electrical contracting business. The Master Electrician of Record carries an important role in the contractor licensing structure.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to annual renewal and applicable continuing education requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

Services and Separately Derived Systems: Services are heavily represented on both examination portions. Study service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, service sizing, and separately derived systems.

Feeders: Review feeder conductor sizing, grounded conductor requirements, demand factors, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, and load calculations.

Branch Circuits: Study conductor ampacity, circuit ratings, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, required circuits, equipment loads, and branch-circuit calculations.

Grounding and Bonding: Article 250 is a major study area. Review service bonding, grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, separately derived systems, and effective ground-fault current paths.

Wiring Methods: Study raceways, cable systems, conductors, boxes, enclosures, fittings, physical protection, wet locations, and Chapter 3 installation requirements.

Electrical Equipment: Review equipment ratings, panelboards, switchboards, disconnects, receptacles, switches, appliances, HVAC equipment, working space, and general installation rules.

Motors and Generators: Motors represent a significant portion of the Calculations exam. Practice using NEC motor tables and applying conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit protection, controllers, disconnects, and feeder requirements.

Special Occupancies and Equipment: Review code requirements for specialized locations and equipment that may require exceptions or rules beyond the general chapters of the NEC.

Renewable Energy Technologies: Study photovoltaic systems and electric vehicle charging equipment, including conductors, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, equipment, and grounding requirements.

Service Calculations: Practice general lighting loads, demand factors, appliances, motors, heating and cooling, and other loads used to determine service size.

Feeder Calculations: Work through feeder-load calculations and conductor sizing while applying the correct demand factors and overcurrent-protection rules.

Motor Calculations: Practice identifying full-load current values from the NEC and applying the rules for branch-circuit conductors, overload protection, short-circuit protection, and multi-motor feeders.

Code Navigation: Use the highlighted and tabbed NEC during every stage of preparation. Practice identifying the topic before searching so the correct article or table can be located more efficiently.

Timed Practice: The NEC Knowledge Portion allows 150 minutes, while the Calculations Portion allows 170 minutes. Practice each portion separately under realistic time limits.

Long-Term Study Structure: With 1 year of course access, candidates can divide preparation into phases. Early study can emphasize general NEC familiarity, followed by focused subject review, calculation practice, timed testing, and final reinforcement of weaker topics.
