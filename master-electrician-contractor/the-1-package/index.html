Exam Details

The Texas Master Electrician examination consists of two separate portions: the NEC Knowledge Portion and the Calculations Portion. Candidates must achieve the required passing score on both portions.

NEC Knowledge Portion:

  • Total Items: 75, including 5 non-scored items
  • Time Allowed: 150 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%

The NEC Knowledge Portion includes:

  • Definitions, Theory, and Plans: 7 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 11 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 7 items
  • Branch Circuit Conductors: 11 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 7 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 7 items
  • Motors and Generators: 7 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 3 items
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: 7 items
  • Renewable Energy Technologies: 3 items

Calculations Portion:

  • Total Items: 33, including 3 non-scored items
  • Time Allowed: 170 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%

The Calculations Portion includes:

  • Calculations and Theory: 2 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 8 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 3 items
  • Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: 4 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 2 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 2 items
  • Motors and Generators: 6 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 1 item
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: 1 item
  • Renewable Energy Technologies: 1 item

Time remaining on one examination portion does not transfer to the other. Candidates should therefore develop separate pacing strategies for NEC Knowledge and Calculations.

Open Book Test

The Texas Master Electrician examination is an open book test.

The technical reference for the current examination is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing an acceptable NEC reference to the examination site.

Acceptable soft-bound NEC editions may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as the testing reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before testing, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. New markings may not be added during the examination session.

The rental NEC included with The 1 Package is highlighted and tabbed for organized preparation. Regular use throughout the 1 year of course access can help candidates develop familiarity with major articles, tables, definitions, and exceptions.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need for strong NEC knowledge. Candidates should understand the subject behind a question before searching for the answer. Efficient navigation is particularly important on the calculation portion, where time must be divided between finding applicable code information and completing the calculation correctly.

Licensing Steps

  1. Hold a Texas Journeyman Electrician license. The Master Electrician credential builds on the Journeyman Electrician license.

  3. Complete the required electrical experience. Applicants must document at least 12,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.
  4. Obtain experience verification. Qualifying experience must be documented by the appropriate supervising Master Electrician or Master Electricians.
  5. Prepare the Master Electrician application. The application and supporting documentation are submitted to the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

State Requirements

A Texas Master Electrician is an individually licensed electrical professional. Applicants must have held a Texas Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years and must document at least 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.

Qualifying experience must be verified as part of the state application process. Journeyman Industrial Electrician experience does not count toward the experience requirement for the Texas Master Electrician credential.

The Texas Master Electrician license and the Texas Electrical Contractor license are not the same credential. The Master Electrician license applies to the individual. The Electrical Contractor license applies to the contracting business.

A Texas electrical contracting business must have a licensed Master Electrician serving as its Master Electrician of Record. The Master Electrician may also own the electrical contracting business.

The business side of electrical contracting can involve more than obtaining the individual Master Electrician credential. A contractor must also establish and maintain the business structure and satisfy the applicable requirements connected with operating as a licensed electrical contracting business.

Test Information and Study Materials

Services and Separately Derived Systems: Services are heavily represented on both exam portions. Review service conductors, equipment, disconnects, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, transformers, and service calculations.

Feeders: Study feeder conductor sizing, load calculations, grounded conductors, overcurrent protection, equipment grounding conductors, and applicable demand factors.

Branch Circuits: Review conductor sizing, continuous loads, circuit ratings, equipment supply, grounding, overcurrent protection, and branch-circuit calculations.

Grounding and Bonding: Study Article 250 thoroughly. Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, and separately derived systems.

Electrical Wiring Methods: Review raceways, cable systems, conductors, boxes, enclosures, fittings, physical protection, wet locations, and Chapter 3 requirements.

Electrical Equipment: Study panelboards, switchboards, disconnects, receptacles, appliances, HVAC equipment, equipment ratings, and working-clearance requirements.

Motors and Generators: Motors represent an important part of the calculation examination. Practice motor conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, and multi-motor feeder calculations.

Renewable Energy Technologies: Review photovoltaic systems and electric vehicle charging equipment, including conductor, disconnect, equipment, grounding, and overcurrent-protection requirements.

Service and Feeder Calculations: Practice applying demand factors and calculating general lighting, appliance, motor, heating, cooling, and other electrical loads.

Code Navigation: Use the highlighted and tabbed NEC throughout preparation. Practice identifying the governing subject before searching so that the correct article or table can be found efficiently.

Timed Practice: Practice the NEC Knowledge Portion and Calculations Portion independently. The two sections have different time limits and require different pacing strategies.

Long-Term Study: With 1 year of course access, candidates can begin with overall NEC familiarity, progress into detailed subject review and calculations, and use later study sessions for timed practice and reinforcement.

Business Preparation: Candidates planning to operate an electrical contracting business should also understand the distinction between their individual Master Electrician credential and the separate business requirements associated with an Electrical Contractor license.
