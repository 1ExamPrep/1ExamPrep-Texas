### Open Book Test

The Texas Environmental Air Class B Contractor examination is an **open book test**.

Candidates may bring approved references identified for the examination. Open-book testing makes advance organization important because candidates must locate information efficiently while working within the testing time limit.

Approved reference books may generally be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes made before the examination. Permanent index tabs may be used when they meet testing-center requirements. Temporary tabs, removable notes, and loose additional papers are not permitted.

### Licensing Steps

1. **Meet the age requirement.** Applicants must be at least 18 years old.

5. **Submit the contractor application to TDLR.** TDLR reviews applicant eligibility before authorizing examination registration.
6. **Receive examination eligibility.** Approved applicants receive instructions for scheduling the required examination through PSI.
7. **Prepare with the current approved references.** Study Texas law, contractor rules, HVAC codes, technical references, and design manuals applicable to Environmental Air Class B.
8. **Take the Environmental Air Class B examination.** Complete the scored and non-scored examination portions within the allotted testing time.

10. **Complete remaining licensing requirements.** Follow TDLR procedures, including applicable insurance and licensing documentation requirements, before the contractor license is issued.

### State Requirements

A Texas **Class B Environmental Air Conditioning** license authorizes contracting work involving environmental air-conditioning systems within the Class B capacity limits.

The Class B license permits work on individual cooling systems with a capacity of **25 tons or less** and individual heating systems with an output of **1.5 million Btu per hour or less**.

Environmental Air Conditioning refers to treating air for human comfort by controlling temperature, humidity, cleanliness, ventilation, and circulation. This includes common residential and qualifying commercial heating and air-conditioning applications within the Class B capacity limits.

A building or complex may contain multiple HVAC units whose combined capacity exceeds the Class B limits, provided each complete individual unit remains within the applicable Class B limitation.

Texas requires applicants to satisfy practical-experience requirements before qualifying for a contractor license. The standard pathway requires at least **48 months of practical experience during the preceding 72 months** under qualifying supervision.

The contractor examination is only one part of the licensing process. Candidates must also complete the applicable TDLR application, experience documentation, insurance, and licensing requirements.
