### Open Book Test

The Texas Maintenance Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The approved technical reference is the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

Soft-bound editions of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the exam-room reference.

The NEC may contain highlighting, underlining, and permitted notes prepared before the examination. Permanent NEC index tabs that comply with the applicable testing rules may also be used.

Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination session. Loose inserted papers and prohibited temporary materials are not permitted.

An open-book format should not be treated as an invitation to search every question from scratch. Strong candidates usually benefit from knowing the likely article or chapter before opening the code. Online preparation can help reinforce this skill by combining question review with repeated reference navigation.

### State Requirements

A Texas Maintenance Electrician is licensed to perform **electrical maintenance work under the general supervision of a Master Electrician**.

Applicants must document at least **8,000 hours of on-the-job training** under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Maintenance Electrician.

The qualifying experience must be properly documented as part of the licensing application. TDLR reviews the submitted information before granting examination eligibility.

The Maintenance Electrician credential is different from the Texas Journeyman Electrician license. It is specifically directed toward electrical maintenance work and carries a more limited scope than the general Journeyman Electrician credential.

Maintenance electricians may encounter existing systems involving panels, conductors, motors, controls, lighting, HVAC equipment, branch circuits, grounding systems, raceways, and other installed electrical equipment. Exam preparation should therefore focus on understanding how NEC requirements apply to inspection, maintenance, replacement, and repair situations.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to annual renewal requirements. Continuing education requirements also apply to the credential.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans:** Review Article 100 terminology, electrical theory, basic calculations, and the interpretation of electrical plans. Candidates should understand voltage, current, resistance, power, and common electrical relationships.

**Electrical Services:** Study service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, equipment ratings, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, and related Article 230 requirements.

**Separately Derived Systems:** Review transformers, system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and related Article 250 provisions.

**Electrical Feeders:** Study feeder conductor sizing, feeder load requirements, grounded conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, and bonding.

**Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors:** With 10 scored items, this is one of the largest test categories. Review conductor ampacity, circuit ratings, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, grounding conductors, and load calculations.

**GFCI and AFCI Protection:** Review the locations and equipment for which ground-fault and arc-fault protection is required. These requirements can appear in branch-circuit and equipment-related questions.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 is important across multiple examination categories. Study grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, feeder grounding, and separately derived systems.

**Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials:** Nine scored items cover Chapter 3 subjects. Review raceways, cables, boxes, enclosures, conductors, fittings, support requirements, physical protection, wet locations, and underground installations.

**Raceways:** Study permitted uses, support and securing requirements, conductor fill, bends, fittings, environmental limitations, and protection from physical damage.

**Boxes and Enclosures:** Review box fill, junction boxes, pull boxes, covers, accessibility, enclosure ratings, and proper use of cabinets and panels.

**Conductors:** Study ampacity, insulation ratings, temperature limitations, correction factors, adjustment factors, terminal ratings, and conductor identification.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** This category also contains 10 scored items. Review receptacles, switches, luminaires, appliances, HVAC equipment, working clearances, equipment ratings, and general installation requirements.

**Air-Conditioning and Refrigeration Equipment:** Study Article 440 requirements involving conductors, disconnects, branch-circuit protection, equipment ratings, and related installation requirements.

**Appliances:** Review Article 422, including branch-circuit requirements, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, flexible cords, and permanently connected appliances.

**Working Space:** Article 110 working-space and access rules are important in maintenance situations. Review required clearances, dedicated equipment space, guarding, access, and safe equipment installation.

**Motors and Generators:** Four scored items address motors and generators. Review motor conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, and generator requirements.

**Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means:** Review contactors, switches, controllers, disconnect ratings, location, accessibility, grouping, and safe equipment isolation.

**Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions:** Eight scored items cover specialized installations. Candidates should review special NEC chapters that may apply to maintenance work in commercial, industrial, and other environments.

**Existing Electrical Systems:** Maintenance electricians frequently work on systems that have already been installed and placed into service. Preparation should include understanding how NEC requirements affect replacement equipment, modifications, service work, and maintenance activities.

**Code Navigation:** Use the 2026 NEC throughout your online preparation. Practice identifying the subject of a question first, then locating the correct article, section, table, or exception.

**Timed Practice:** Candidates receive 180 minutes for 60 scored items, which provides an average of approximately three minutes per scored question. Practice under similar pacing so difficult code lookups do not consume excessive time.

**Study by Exam Weight:** Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors and Electrical Equipment and Devices are the two largest categories. Wiring Methods and Materials, Services, and Special Occupancies also represent major portions of the examination and deserve regular review.
