### Open Book Test

The Texas Maintenance Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The current approved examination reference is the **National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own acceptable NEC book to the examination center.

TDLR permits a soft-bound edition of the NEC for the examination. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound NEC copies are not permitted in the testing room. Candidates should prepare the correct edition before test day and become familiar with the physical organization of the book.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to know electrical theory and code structure. With limited testing time, candidates benefit from recognizing the subject of a question before searching the NEC. Knowing whether the question involves branch circuits, services, motors, wiring methods, equipment, grounding, or special conditions can direct the candidate to the correct article much faster.

Practice navigating frequently used NEC sections, tables, definitions, and cross-references. Candidates should also become comfortable identifying exceptions and informational connections that may change how a general rule applies to a specific installation.

### Licensing Steps

1. **Gain qualifying electrical experience.** Texas requires Maintenance Electrician applicants to document substantial on-the-job electrical training.
2. **Work under qualified supervision.** The required experience must be completed under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Maintenance Electrician.
3. **Complete 8,000 hours of on-the-job training.** Applicants must document the full qualifying experience requirement before applying for the Maintenance Electrician license.
4. **Obtain experience verification.** Each supervising Master Electrician or Maintenance Electrician must verify the qualifying work using the applicable TDLR experience-verification documentation.
5. **Submit the Maintenance Electrician application.** The completed application and required documentation are submitted to TDLR.
6. **Receive examination authorization.** After the application and experience are reviewed and approved, TDLR notifies the applicant that they are eligible to test.
7. **Schedule the examination with PSI.** Approved candidates use the examination instructions provided for the Texas electrician program.
8. **Prepare using the 2026 NEC.** Study the official content categories and practice locating the requirements relevant to maintenance electrical work.
9. **Take the Texas Maintenance Electrician examination.** Complete the scored and non-scored portions within the allotted testing time.
10. **Earn the required passing score.** A score of 70% or higher is required.
11. **Complete licensing requirements.** After satisfying the examination and other applicable TDLR requirements, the Maintenance Electrician license can be issued.

### State Requirements

Texas licenses Maintenance Electricians to perform **electrical maintenance work under the general supervision of a Master Electrician**. The credential is different from a Journeyman Electrician or Master Electrician license and is intended specifically for electrical maintenance work within the scope authorized by Texas law.

Applicants must document at least **8,000 hours of on-the-job training** under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Maintenance Electrician.

Experience must be verified by each qualifying supervisor. TDLR may contact individuals or businesses involved in the verification process to confirm that reported experience satisfies the licensing requirement.

After an applicant submits the Maintenance Electrician application and TDLR verifies the required qualifications, the applicant becomes eligible to take the state licensing examination.

Texas electrician licenses are issued for one year and are renewed annually. License holders must continue to comply with the licensing and continuing-education requirements that apply to their credential.

Texas adopted the **2026 National Electrical Code** as the state electrical code effective September 1, 2026. The 2026 NEC is also the current reference used for Texas electrician examinations.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans:** Review NEC definitions and foundational electrical theory. Understand voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, and terminology used throughout the code. Basic plan interpretation can also support questions involving equipment and wiring systems.

**Services and Service Equipment:** Study service conductors, service equipment, service disconnecting means, ratings, grounding, bonding, and the relationship between service equipment and downstream distribution.

**Separately Derived Systems:** Review transformers and other separately derived systems, including grounding, bonding, system bonding jumpers, and grounding electrode conductor requirements.

**Feeders:** Study feeder conductor requirements, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and applicable load calculations.

**Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors:** This is one of the largest examination categories. Review branch-circuit ratings, conductor sizing, required circuits, receptacles, lighting outlets, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, and applicable calculation requirements.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 is essential to electrical maintenance work. Review equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode systems, bonding jumpers, effective ground-fault current paths, service bonding, and grounding for separately derived systems.

**Wiring Methods and Materials:** Review Chapter 3 wiring methods, cable types, raceways, conductor installation, support, protection from physical damage, boxes, conduit bodies, underground installations, and requirements for wet or damp locations.

**Boxes and Raceway Fill:** Practice box-fill and raceway-fill concepts. Understand conductor volume allowances, grounding conductor allowances, conduit fill, and the use of applicable NEC tables.

**Conductors:** Review conductor ampacity, insulation types, temperature ratings, correction factors, adjustment factors, terminal temperature limitations, and conductor identification.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** This category accounts for 10 scored items. Review switches, receptacles, luminaires, panelboards, transformers, appliances, heating equipment, air-conditioning equipment, and other commonly maintained electrical equipment.

**Motors and Generators:** Study motor conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, motor controllers, disconnecting means, motor full-load-current tables, and generator requirements.

**Controls and Disconnecting Means:** Review control devices, motor controllers, disconnect locations, accessibility, grouping requirements, ratings, and safe isolation of equipment for maintenance.

**Special Occupancies:** Become familiar with NEC requirements that apply to specialized locations such as hazardous locations and other occupancies with additional electrical rules.

**Special Equipment:** Review equipment-specific NEC requirements when applicable, including signs, cranes, elevators, electric vehicle equipment, generators, and other specialized systems.

**Special Conditions:** Study emergency systems, standby systems, interconnected power sources, and other Chapter 7 conditions that can affect electrical maintenance work.

**Code Navigation:** Practice locating requirements by article and topic rather than depending entirely on the index. Efficient use of Article 100 definitions, chapter organization, tables, and article cross-references can save valuable examination time.

**Timed Practice:** The scored portion allows 180 minutes for 60 items, followed by additional time for non-scored items. Practice maintaining a steady pace and avoid spending too much time on one difficult code lookup.

**Maintenance-Oriented Review:** Focus on understanding existing electrical installations as well as new-code requirements. Maintenance electricians may need to recognize unsafe conditions, evaluate equipment, identify correct replacement components, and understand how conductors, protection, controls, and disconnects interact within operating systems.
