### Open Book Test

The Texas Maintenance Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The technical reference for the current examination is the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition**. Candidates should become comfortable using the code efficiently before test day.

Acceptable soft-bound NEC editions may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound versions are not permitted in the testing room. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before testing, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination session.

### State Requirements

A Texas Maintenance Electrician performs electrical maintenance work under the **general supervision of a Master Electrician**.

Applicants for the Maintenance Electrician license must document at least **8,000 hours of on-the-job training** under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Maintenance Electrician.

Qualifying experience must be documented as part of the state application process. TDLR reviews the application and supporting experience documentation before the applicant is approved to take the examination.

The Maintenance Electrician license has a more limited scope than the Texas Journeyman Electrician credential. Its purpose is focused on electrical maintenance work rather than the broader electrical work authorized by the Journeyman Electrician license.

Maintenance work can involve existing electrical systems such as panels, feeders, branch circuits, lighting, motors, appliances, HVAC equipment, controls, disconnecting means, grounding systems, and installed wiring. Candidates should therefore be prepared to apply NEC requirements to practical maintenance situations.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to annual renewal and applicable continuing education requirements.

The **2026 National Electrical Code** is the current technical reference for the Texas Maintenance Electrician examination.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans:** Review Article 100 definitions, basic electrical theory, voltage, current, resistance, power, plan interpretation, and common electrical calculations.

**Electrical Services:** Study service conductors, service equipment, service disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, equipment ratings, grounding, bonding, and related service requirements.

**Separately Derived Systems:** Review transformers, system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and related Article 250 requirements.

**Electrical Feeders:** Study feeder conductor sizing, feeder loads, overcurrent protection, grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, grounding, and bonding.

**Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors:** This is one of the largest examination categories. Review conductor ampacity, branch-circuit ratings, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, conductor sizing, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, and applicable calculations.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 supports many areas of the exam. Study grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, services, feeders, and separately derived systems.

**Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials:** Nine scored items are assigned to this area. Review raceways, cable systems, conductors, boxes, fittings, enclosures, securing and supporting, physical protection, and environmental requirements.

**Conductor Ampacity:** Practice using applicable NEC ampacity tables and applying terminal temperature limitations, conductor insulation ratings, correction factors, and adjustment factors.

**Boxes and Enclosures:** Review box fill, conductor volume allowances, covers, junction boxes, cabinets, enclosures, and accessibility requirements.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** This category contains 10 scored items. Review receptacles, switches, appliances, luminaires, HVAC equipment, working clearances, equipment ratings, grounding, and disconnecting means.

**Appliances:** Review Article 422 requirements involving branch circuits, grounding, flexible cords, overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, and permanently connected appliances.

**Air-Conditioning and Refrigeration Equipment:** Study Article 440 conductor requirements, disconnects, equipment ratings, branch circuits, and overcurrent protection relevant to maintenance work.

**Motors and Generators:** Review motor conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, and applicable generator requirements.

**Electrical Control Devices:** Study controllers, contactors, switches, relays, disconnecting means, equipment ratings, accessibility, and general control-device requirements.

**Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions:** Eight scored items are assigned to specialized installations. Review the NEC chapters that address locations, equipment, and conditions requiring rules beyond general wiring provisions.

**Existing Electrical Systems:** Maintenance electricians regularly work with equipment already in service. Study how NEC requirements apply to repairs, replacements, equipment changes, branch circuits, conductors, grounding, controls, and disconnecting means.

**Code Navigation:** Use the highlighted and tabbed NEC during every phase of preparation. Practice identifying the topic of a question first, then moving directly to the likely article, section, table, or cross-reference.

**Cross-Reference Practice:** Many questions require understanding how several NEC requirements work together. Practice moving between equipment-specific articles and general provisions involving wiring methods, grounding, conductor sizing, and overcurrent protection.

**Timed Practice:** Candidates receive 180 minutes for 60 scored items. Practice maintaining a steady pace of approximately three minutes per scored question while allowing additional time for calculations and detailed code searches.

**Study by Exam Weight:** Give particular attention to Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors, Electrical Equipment and Devices, Wiring Methods and Materials, Services, and Special Occupancies. These categories make up a substantial percentage of the examination.

**Long-Term Preparation:** With **1 year of course access**, candidates can divide study into stages. Early preparation can focus on general NEC organization, followed by detailed subject review, practice questions, code-navigation drills, timed review, and final reinforcement of weaker areas.

## How does the Maintenance Electrician exam work?

The Maintenance Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has 60 scored items in 180 minutes and costs $64.
- The TDLR application fee is $20.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
