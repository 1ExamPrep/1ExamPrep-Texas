### Open Book Test

The Texas Maintenance Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The current approved examination reference is the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

Any soft-bound edition of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound NEC copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes prepared before the examination session. Permanent index tabs manufactured or provided by the NEC publisher are permitted under the testing rules.

Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or otherwise mark the NEC during the examination. Homemade, DIY, or non-NEC tabs are not permitted. Additional paper that is loose, taped, adhered, stapled, or otherwise added to the book is also prohibited.

### Licensing Steps

1. **Gain qualifying electrical maintenance experience.** Texas requires substantial supervised on-the-job electrical training before an applicant can qualify for the Maintenance Electrician license.
2. **Work under qualified supervision.** Qualifying experience must be completed under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Maintenance Electrician.
3. **Complete 8,000 hours of on-the-job training.** The full experience requirement must be documented before applying for the license.
4. **Obtain experience verification.** Qualifying supervisors must verify the electrical work used to satisfy the experience requirement.
5. **Submit the Maintenance Electrician application.** The completed application and required documentation are submitted to TDLR.
6. **Receive examination authorization.** After the application and experience have been reviewed and verified, the applicant is approved to test.
7. **Schedule the examination through PSI.** Approved applicants follow the PSI scheduling process for Texas electrician examinations.
8. **Prepare with the 2026 NEC.** Review the current content outline and practice locating the provisions most relevant to maintenance electrical work.
9. **Take the Texas Maintenance Electrician examination.** Complete the scored and non-scored items within the allotted time.
10. **Earn the required passing score.** A score of 70% or better is required.
11. **Complete the remaining licensing process.** After satisfying the examination and other TDLR requirements, the Maintenance Electrician license can be issued.

### State Requirements

Texas Maintenance Electricians are licensed to perform **electrical maintenance work while working under the general supervision of a Master Electrician**.

Applicants must document at least **8,000 hours of on-the-job training** under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Maintenance Electrician.

The experience requirement reflects the practical nature of maintenance electrical work. Candidates are expected to have substantial exposure to existing electrical systems, conductors, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, equipment, motors, controls, grounding and bonding, and other electrical installations encountered during maintenance and repair.

After TDLR receives and verifies the completed application, the applicant receives authorization to take the licensing examination through PSI.

The Maintenance Electrician license is different from a Texas Journeyman Electrician license. The Maintenance Electrician credential is specifically directed toward electrical maintenance work and does not create the same general scope associated with the Journeyman Electrician credential.

Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually. License holders must satisfy the renewal requirements that apply to their credential.

Texas adopted the **2026 National Electrical Code** effective September 1, 2026. The current Texas electrician examinations are referenced to the 2026 NEC.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans:** Review Article 100 definitions, electrical theory, basic calculations, and plan-reading concepts. Candidates should understand voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, and common electrical terminology.

**Electrical Services and Service Equipment:** Eight scored items address services, service equipment, and separately derived systems. Review load calculations, fault current, service equipment, grounding and bonding, and transformer-related requirements.

**Separately Derived Systems:** Study transformers, grounding electrode conductors, system bonding jumpers, equipment grounding conductors, and related Article 250 provisions.

**Electrical Feeders:** Review feeder load calculations, grounded conductors, grounding and bonding, and feeder overcurrent protection. Understand how feeder requirements interact with downstream equipment and branch circuits.

**Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors:** This is one of the largest exam categories. Review GFCI and AFCI protection, branch-circuit calculations, branch-circuit overcurrent protection, conductor sizing, and grounding and bonding conductors.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 is important across several examination categories. Study grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, services, feeders, and separately derived systems.

**Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials:** Nine scored items cover raceways and cable trays, boxes and enclosures, cabinets and panels, cables and cords, and underground wiring. Candidates should become comfortable navigating Chapter 3 and related tables.

**Raceways:** Review raceway installation, support, conductor fill, bends, wet locations, underground installations, and physical protection. Maintenance work frequently requires evaluating or modifying existing raceway systems.

**Boxes and Enclosures:** Study box fill, enclosures, junction boxes, pull boxes, accessibility, covers, and equipment protection. Existing electrical systems often require maintenance electricians to identify whether boxes and enclosures remain code compliant.

**Conductors:** Review ampacity, insulation types, temperature limitations, adjustment factors, correction factors, terminal ratings, and conductor identification.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Ten scored items address receptacles and switches, luminaires, air-conditioning and refrigeration equipment, appliances, and workspace clearance. This is another major study category.

**Air-Conditioning, Refrigeration, and Heating Equipment:** Review disconnecting means, branch circuits, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, equipment ratings, and the NEC provisions applicable to HVAC equipment.

**Appliances:** Study fixed and cord-connected appliances, branch-circuit requirements, overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, and equipment grounding.

**Workspace Clearance:** Review Article 110 requirements involving working space, access, dedicated equipment space, equipment condition, and safe maintenance access.

**Motors and Generators:** Four scored items address motors and generators. Review motor conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, and generator requirements.

**Control Devices and Disconnecting Means:** Three scored items cover electrical controls and disconnects. Review ratings, accessibility, location, grouping, lockability, and safe isolation of equipment.

**Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions:** Eight scored items address specialized installations. Review the NEC chapters covering special occupancies, special equipment, and special operating conditions relevant to maintenance work.

**Existing Electrical Systems:** Maintenance electricians frequently work on equipment that has been in service for years. Preparation should include understanding how present NEC requirements interact with repair, replacement, and maintenance decisions.

**Highlighted and Tabbed Navigation Practice:** Use the prepared NEC during timed practice sessions. Identify the subject first, move to the likely article or table, confirm the requirement, and return to the question efficiently.

**Time Management:** Candidates receive 180 minutes for 60 scored items. Practice maintaining a steady pace of approximately three minutes per scored question while reserving extra time for calculations and complex code searches.
