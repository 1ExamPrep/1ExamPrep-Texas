Exam Details

The Texas Maintenance Electrician examination is administered through PSI after the applicant has been approved to test by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: Texas Maintenance Electrician
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 60
  • Time for Scored Items: 180 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

The current examination content outline distributes the 60 scored items across the following subject areas:

  • Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans: 5 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 8 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 3 items
  • Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: 10 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 9 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 10 items
  • Motors and Generators: 4 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 3 items
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: 8 items

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors and Electrical Equipment and Devices are the two largest individual categories with 10 scored items each. Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials contributes nine items, while Services and Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions each account for eight.

This distribution makes broad preparation important. Candidates should focus heavily on branch circuits, electrical equipment, wiring methods, and services while still covering feeders, motors, controls, calculations, theory, plans, and special installations.

Unless an examination question specifically states otherwise, conductors are considered copper.

Open Book Test

The Texas Maintenance Electrician examination is an open book test.

The approved technical reference is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing an acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

Soft-bound editions of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before testing, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination session.

The included rental NEC is highlighted and tabbed to support efficient preparation. Candidates should use the codebook throughout the 6 months of course access so frequently tested articles, tables, and code relationships become familiar before exam day.

An open-book examination still rewards familiarity. Candidates who know the general location of a requirement can spend less time searching and more time interpreting the question and applying the code correctly.

Licensing Steps

  1. Gain qualifying electrical maintenance experience. Texas requires supervised on-the-job training before an applicant may qualify for the Maintenance Electrician license.
  2. Work under qualified supervision. Qualifying experience must be completed under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Maintenance Electrician.
  3. Complete 8,000 hours of on-the-job training. The required experience must be documented before applying for the credential.
  4. Obtain experience verification. Qualifying supervisors must verify the electrical experience submitted with the application.
  5. Submit the Maintenance Electrician application. The completed application and supporting documentation are submitted to TDLR.
  6. Receive examination authorization. After TDLR reviews and approves the application, the applicant becomes eligible to test.
  7. Schedule the examination through PSI. Approved candidates follow the PSI scheduling process.
  8. Prepare with the 2026 NEC. Review services, branch circuits, feeders, wiring methods, electrical equipment, motors, controls, and special installations.
  9. Use the highlighted and tabbed rental NEC. Practice locating important code provisions repeatedly during the course-access period.
  10. Complete structured online preparation. Use the included 6 months of course access for organized exam review and practice-oriented study.
  11. Take the Texas Maintenance Electrician examination. Complete the scored and non-scored questions within the allotted testing time.
  12. Earn the required passing score. A score of at least 70% is required.
  13. Complete the remaining licensing process. Satisfy all applicable TDLR requirements for issuance of the Maintenance Electrician license.

State Requirements

A Texas Maintenance Electrician is licensed to perform electrical maintenance work under the general supervision of a Master Electrician.

Applicants must document at least 8,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Maintenance Electrician.

The experience must be properly documented as part of the licensing application. TDLR reviews the submitted information before the applicant is approved to take the state examination.

The Maintenance Electrician credential has a more limited scope than the Texas Journeyman Electrician license. It is specifically directed toward maintenance electrical work rather than the broader scope associated with the Journeyman Electrician credential.

Maintenance electrical work may involve existing panels, feeders, branch circuits, equipment, motors, controls, wiring systems, disconnects, lighting, appliances, HVAC equipment, grounding systems, and other installed electrical systems. Candidates should therefore be prepared to apply the NEC to a variety of maintenance and service conditions.

Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and are subject to annual renewal requirements. Continuing education requirements also apply to the credential.

The 2026 National Electrical Code became the Texas electrical code effective September 1, 2026, and the current Texas Maintenance Electrician examination references this edition.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans: Review Article 100 definitions, basic electrical theory, plan interpretation, voltage, current, resistance, power, and common electrical calculations.

Electrical Services: Study service conductors, service equipment, service disconnects, ratings, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, and related Article 230 requirements.

Separately Derived Systems: Review transformers, system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and applicable Article 250 requirements.

Electrical Feeders: Study feeder conductor sizing, loads, grounded conductors, overcurrent protection, equipment grounding conductors, and feeder grounding and bonding.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: With 10 scored items, this is one of the largest examination categories. Review conductor ampacity, branch-circuit ratings, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, GFCI and AFCI requirements, and conductor sizing.

Grounding and Bonding: Article 250 is important across multiple examination categories. Study grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, services, feeders, and separately derived systems.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials: Nine scored items cover Chapter 3 subjects. Review raceways, cables, boxes, enclosures, conductors, fittings, support requirements, underground installations, wet locations, and physical protection.

Raceways: Study permitted uses, securing and supporting, conductor fill, bends, environmental limitations, and protection from physical damage.

Boxes and Enclosures: Review box fill, pull boxes, junction boxes, accessibility, covers, enclosures, cabinets, and panel-related requirements.

Conductors: Review ampacity, insulation types, temperature ratings, correction factors, adjustment factors, terminal limitations, and conductor identification.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Ten scored items make this another major category. Study receptacles, switches, luminaires, appliances, HVAC equipment, working space, equipment ratings, and general installation requirements.

Air-Conditioning and Refrigeration Equipment: Review Article 440 conductor, disconnect, branch-circuit, and overcurrent-protection requirements relevant to maintenance work.

Appliances: Review Article 422, including branch circuits, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, flexible cords, grounding, and permanently connected equipment.

Working Space and Equipment Access: Article 110 contains important requirements involving clearances, guarding, access, dedicated equipment space, and safe electrical equipment installation.

Motors and Generators: Four scored items cover motors and generators. Review motor conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, and generator requirements.

Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: Study contactors, switches, controllers, disconnect ratings, accessibility, location, grouping, and safe equipment isolation.

Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: Eight scored items cover specialized electrical installations. Candidates should review the NEC chapters involving special locations, equipment, and operating conditions that may appear in maintenance work.

Existing Electrical Systems: Maintenance electricians frequently work on equipment already in service. Preparation should include understanding how NEC requirements affect repairs, replacements, equipment changes, and maintenance activity.

Code Navigation: Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC throughout the course. Practice identifying the subject first, moving to the relevant article or table, confirming the requirement, and returning to the question efficiently.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 180 minutes for 60 scored items. Practice maintaining an average pace of approximately three minutes per scored question while leaving additional time for calculations and difficult code searches.

Study by Exam Weight: Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors and Electrical Equipment and Devices are the largest categories. Wiring Methods and Materials, Services, and Special Occupancies also represent significant portions of the examination.
