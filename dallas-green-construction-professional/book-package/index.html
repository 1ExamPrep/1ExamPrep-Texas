Test Information and Study Materials

The best way to use this exam book package is to study the 2021 International Green Construction Code by topic and chapter. Students should first become familiar with the organization of the code. Review the table of contents, definitions, administrative provisions, technical sections, referenced standards, and appendices. A candidate who knows where topics are located will be better prepared for open book testing.

Scope and administration should be part of the study plan. Candidates should understand how the IgCC applies to commercial buildings, how jurisdictional requirements may affect compliance, and how project documentation supports enforcement. Green construction compliance often depends on clear documentation, plan review, inspection records, commissioning reports, and verification of selected compliance paths.

Site sustainability is another major area. Students should study provisions related to site development, natural resource protection, heat island reduction, stormwater management, transportation-related features, landscape considerations, and site performance. These topics connect the building project to the surrounding environment and can affect both plan review and inspection.

Water conservation should receive careful attention. Candidates should review indoor water-use reduction, plumbing fixture efficiency, appliance and equipment water use, outdoor water-use reduction, irrigation controls, alternative water sources, and water measurement. Commercial green construction often requires clear understanding of how potable water demand is reduced and documented.

Energy efficiency is a central part of green construction. Students should study building envelope performance, mechanical systems, service water heating, lighting, controls, energy metering, renewable energy readiness, and coordination with energy-related standards. Energy questions may require candidates to understand how the building envelope, HVAC systems, electrical systems, and controls work together to improve performance.

Indoor environmental quality is also important. Candidates should review ventilation, filtration, pollutant control, moisture management, daylighting, views, acoustics, thermal comfort, and material emissions. These provisions are connected to occupant health, comfort, and productivity, making them an important part of commercial green building review and inspection.

Materials and resources should be studied as a separate topic. Students should review construction waste management, recycling, material selection, environmental product declarations, life-cycle considerations, regional materials, recycled content, and documentation requirements. These topics often involve compliance records, product data, and project-specific documentation.

Commissioning and building operation concepts should also be reviewed. Green construction does not end when a building is completed. Candidates should understand the role of commissioning, system verification, owner documentation, operation and maintenance information, and performance-related records. These topics help ensure that green construction measures are installed, tested, and documented properly.

Timed reference practice is one of the most important study habits for the ICC GCP exam. Students should practice reading a question, identifying the subject, choosing the correct chapter, locating the section, and applying the requirement. This helps build speed and confidence with the 2021 International Green Construction Code.

How does the Dallas Commercial Green Construction Professional exam work?

The Dallas Commercial Green Construction Professional exam is set by a Texas city or by TDLR rather than by a statewide contractor board. The city or agency approves you, then the exam is scheduled with ICC or PSI.

  • Texas does not license general contractors at the state level; the Texas Residential Construction Commission was abolished in 2009.
  • Cities set their own registration rules, and some, such as San Antonio, require an ICC exam for certain trades.
  • San Antonio sign operators need a passing ICC exam, a $45,000 bond and $1,000,000 in general liability insurance.
  • Texas code enforcement officers register with TDLR after the TEEX course and a closed-book PSI exam that costs $50.
