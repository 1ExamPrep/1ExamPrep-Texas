How does the Dallas Commercial Green Construction Professional exam work?

The Dallas Commercial Green Construction Professional exam is set by a Texas city or by TDLR rather than by a statewide contractor board. The city or agency approves you, then the exam is scheduled with ICC or PSI.

  • Texas does not license general contractors at the state level; the Texas Residential Construction Commission was abolished in 2009.
  • Cities set their own registration rules, and some, such as San Antonio, require an ICC exam for certain trades.
  • San Antonio sign operators need a passing ICC exam, a $45,000 bond and $1,000,000 in general liability insurance.
  • Texas code enforcement officers register with TDLR after the TEEX course and a closed-book PSI exam that costs $50.

Is the Dallas Commercial Green Construction Professional exam open book?

It depends on the exam. The San Antonio ICC sign exams are open book, while the Texas Code Enforcement Officer exam is closed book. The bulletin for your exam has the rule.

Who issues the Dallas Commercial Green Construction Professional license or certification in Texas?

It comes from the city that requires it, or TDLR for code enforcement officers. Buying a package here does not register you or make you eligible; the board approves you first, and the exam vendor schedules the test.

Does Texas have a state general contractor license?

No. Texas has no statewide general contractor license, and the residential construction commission that registered builders was abolished in 2009. Cities such as San Antonio and Dallas set their own registration rules.

What does a San Antonio sign operator need besides the exam?

A $45,000 bond, a yearly city fee of $100 for on-premises signs or $375 for billboards, $500,000 in workers' compensation and employer's liability and $1,000,000 in general liability insurance.
