### State Requirements

Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor licensing is administered by TDLR under **Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1302** and **Texas Administrative Code Chapter 75**.

A **Class A** license allows the license holder to work on systems of any size within the authorized endorsement. Unlike Class B, Class A does not impose the Class B cooling-capacity and heating-output limitations.

The **Commercial Refrigeration** endorsement covers the use of mechanical or absorption equipment to control temperature or humidity to satisfy the intended use of a specific space. Commercial refrigeration and qualifying process cooling and heating work include equipment such as coolers, freezers, ice machines, and equipment used for applicable temperature and humidity control.

The standard experience pathway requires at least **48 months of practical air-conditioning and refrigeration experience during the preceding 72 months** under the supervision of a licensed Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor.

An applicant who has held a certified technician designation for the preceding 12 months may qualify through a pathway requiring at least **36 months of practical experience during the preceding 48 months**.

Texas also recognizes certain exceptions and alternative experience qualifications for eligible applicants with relevant education, engineering backgrounds, military training, or qualifying industrial experience.

After passing the examination, a Class A contractor must maintain the commercial general liability insurance required by TDLR while the license is active. The current minimum Class A coverage is $300,000 per occurrence for property damage and bodily injury, $600,000 aggregate for property damage and bodily injury, and $300,000 aggregate for products and completed operations.

**Equipment Requirements:** Approximately 12 questions address equipment requirements. Study installation, access, clearances, equipment ratings, machinery-room considerations, code requirements, and proper application of refrigeration equipment.

**Piping:** Approximately 11 questions cover piping. Review refrigerant piping, suction lines, liquid lines, discharge lines, oil return, fittings, piping support, insulation, installation practices, and system configuration.

**Service:** Approximately 11 questions cover service and troubleshooting. Candidates should understand common refrigeration-system faults, electrical diagnosis, refrigerant charging, evacuation, recovery, leak detection, compressor problems, and system performance.

**Basic Refrigeration Components:** Approximately 10 questions address compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, receivers, accumulators, filter-driers, sight glasses, and other refrigeration components.

**Refrigeration Principles:** Approximately 10 questions test fundamental concepts such as pressure-temperature relationships, sensible and latent heat, heat transfer, saturation, superheat, subcooling, compression, condensation, expansion, and evaporation.

**Safety:** Approximately seven questions focus on safe work practices. Review refrigerant handling, electrical safety, pressure hazards, personal protective equipment, workplace procedures, and applicable safety requirements.

**Air Distribution:** Approximately six questions address air-distribution principles. The included Ductulator can support study of airflow, velocity, friction rate, and duct sizing.

**Testing:** Approximately six questions involve testing. Review pressure and temperature measurements, superheat, subcooling, electrical measurements, refrigerant system testing, leak testing, and system performance evaluation.

**Combustion Air:** Approximately three questions involve combustion-air concepts. Review applicable mechanical and fuel-gas code requirements when fuel-fired equipment is part of a system.

**Commercial Refrigeration Applications:** Study walk-in coolers, freezers, reach-in equipment, ice machines, refrigerated spaces, condensing units, evaporators, compressors, and commercial system controls.

**Defrost Systems:** Review off-cycle, electric, and other applicable defrost methods, including controls, timing, termination, and system behavior during and after defrost.

**Refrigerant Charging:** Understand charging methods, refrigerant state, system operating conditions, superheat and subcooling, and how charge affects equipment performance.
