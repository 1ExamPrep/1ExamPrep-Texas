Reference Books

  • TMS 402/602-16 Building Code Requirements and Specification for Masonry Structures
    The primary masonry code and specification reference for structural masonry construction, including design requirements, construction specifications, quality assurance, materials, reinforcement, grout, mortar, tolerances, and inspection-related provisions.
  • Inspectors Handbook for Reinforced Grout Brick Masonry, 15th Edition
    A field-focused masonry inspection reference used to study reinforced grouted brick masonry construction, inspection practices, reinforcement placement, grout placement, workmanship, tolerances, and common field conditions.
  • ASTM A951/A951M-26
    A standard specification related to steel wire for masonry joint reinforcement and related masonry reinforcement applications.
  • ASTM C90-24a
    A standard specification for loadbearing concrete masonry units, including requirements for concrete masonry unit properties and acceptance.
  • ASTM C140/C140M-26
    A standard test method for sampling and testing concrete masonry units and related units, including procedures used to evaluate masonry unit properties.
  • ASTM C216-26
    A standard specification for facing brick made from clay or shale, including requirements related to grade, type, appearance, durability, and performance.
  • ASTM C270-25b
    A standard specification for mortar for unit masonry, including mortar types, proportion specifications, property specifications, and related requirements.
  • ASTM C476-23
    A standard specification for grout for masonry, including requirements for grout materials, proportions, consistency, and compressive strength.
  • ASTM C1019-26
    A standard test method for sampling and testing grout for masonry, including procedures for preparing, curing, and testing grout specimens.
  • ASTM A615/A615M-26
    A standard specification for deformed and plain carbon-steel bars for concrete reinforcement, used in reinforced masonry applications where reinforcing bars are required.
  • ASTM C1314-24
    A standard test method for constructing and testing masonry prisms used to determine masonry compressive strength.

Test Information and Study Materials

The best way to use this exam book package is to study by topic and reference type. Start with TMS 402/602-16 because it provides the main framework for structural masonry requirements. Students should understand the difference between code provisions and specification provisions, and they should know where to find requirements for materials, inspection, construction, tolerances, reinforcement, grout, mortar, and quality assurance.

Quality assurance should be a major study area. Candidates should review inspection levels, required verification, submittals, field inspection duties, compliance with approved documents, and construction observations. Structural masonry inspection is not only about knowing the material; it is about knowing what must be verified during construction.

Reinforcement placement is another important topic. Students should study reinforcing bar requirements, placement tolerances, lap splices, minimum cover, bar supports, clearances, embedded items, and how reinforcement interacts with grout placement. ASTM A615/A615M-26 and ASTM A951/A951M-26 support study of reinforcement-related material requirements.

Grout and mortar should receive careful attention. ASTM C270-25b covers mortar for unit masonry, while ASTM C476-23 covers grout for masonry. ASTM C1019-26 covers sampling and testing grout. Candidates should understand the difference between mortar and grout, how each material is used, how samples are handled, and why testing procedures matter for acceptance.

Masonry units are another major study category. ASTM C90-24a supports study of loadbearing concrete masonry units. ASTM C140/C140M-26 supports sampling and testing of concrete masonry units and related units. ASTM C216-26 supports study of facing brick requirements. Candidates should understand which standard applies to which unit type and how material acceptance connects to inspection.

Masonry prism testing should also be studied. ASTM C1314-24 covers construction and testing of masonry prisms used to determine compressive strength. Candidates should review prism construction, curing, testing, compressive strength determination, and how prism testing relates to masonry performance and acceptance.

The Inspectors Handbook for Reinforced Grout Brick Masonry, 15th Edition should be used to support field understanding. Candidates should study inspection practices, grout placement conditions, reinforced brick masonry details, cleanouts, cells, consolidation, reinforcement alignment, and common construction issues. This handbook helps connect code requirements to what inspectors see in the field.

Timed study is important because this is an open book exam. Students should practice choosing the correct reference quickly. For example, a question about grout testing should lead to ASTM C1019. A question about grout material should lead to ASTM C476. A question about mortar should lead to ASTM C270. A question about concrete masonry units should lead to ASTM C90 or ASTM C140. A question about masonry prisms should lead to ASTM C1314. Building that reference-selection skill is one of the most important parts of preparation.

How does the San Antonio Structural Masonry (84CY) exam work?

The San Antonio Structural Masonry (84CY) exam is set by a Texas city or by TDLR rather than by a statewide contractor board. The city or agency approves you, then the exam is scheduled with ICC or PSI.

  • Texas does not license general contractors at the state level; the Texas Residential Construction Commission was abolished in 2009.
  • Cities set their own registration rules, and some, such as San Antonio, require an ICC exam for certain trades.
  • San Antonio sign operators need a passing ICC exam, a $45,000 bond and $1,000,000 in general liability insurance.
  • Texas code enforcement officers register with TDLR after the TEEX course and a closed-book PSI exam that costs $50.
