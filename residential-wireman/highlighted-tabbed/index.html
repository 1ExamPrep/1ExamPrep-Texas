Exam Details

The Texas Residential Wireman Electrician examination is administered through PSI after the applicant has been approved to test by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: Texas Residential Wireman Electrician
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 80
  • Time for Scored Items: 240 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%

Open Book Test

The Texas Residential Wireman Electrician examination is an open book test.

The current examination reference is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

TDLR permits soft-bound editions of the NEC for electrician examinations. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room.

A highlighted and tabbed NEC can be especially useful during preparation because residential examination questions may require movement between several different articles. A single dwelling installation question could involve a branch-circuit requirement, conductor ampacity, grounding, overcurrent protection, equipment installation, and a special residential provision.

Candidates should use the prepared NEC during timed study sessions and practice moving directly to frequently used residential sections. Developing familiarity with the organization of the book can reduce unnecessary searching and leave more time for calculations and code application.

Licensing Steps

  1. Meet an approved qualification pathway. Texas allows Residential Wireman applicants to qualify through an approved career and technology education program or qualifying supervised electrical experience.
  2. Complete qualifying on-the-job training when using the experience pathway. Applicants must document at least 4,000 hours of electrical training.
  3. Work under qualified supervision. The experience must be completed under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Residential Wireman.
  4. Obtain experience verification. Each qualifying supervisor must document the electrical experience used toward the application.
  5. Submit the Residential Wireman application. The completed application and applicable supporting documentation are submitted to TDLR.
  6. Receive examination authorization. TDLR reviews the application and notifies eligible candidates that they are approved to test.
  7. Schedule the examination through PSI. Approved candidates follow the PSI scheduling process for Texas electrician examinations.
  8. Prepare using the 2026 NEC. Review residential services, branch circuits, feeders, grounding, wiring methods, equipment, calculations, and special installations.
  9. Take the Texas Residential Wireman examination. Complete the scored and non-scored items within the allotted testing time.
  10. Earn the required passing score. Candidates must achieve at least 70%.
  11. Complete the remaining licensing process. Satisfy all applicable TDLR requirements for issuance of the Residential Wireman license.

State Requirements

A Texas Residential Wireman is licensed to perform electrical installations in single-family and multifamily dwellings not exceeding four stories.

Applicants may qualify by providing proof of completion of an approved career and technology education program or by documenting at least 4,000 hours of on-the-job electrical training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Residential Wireman.

Applicants using the experience pathway must obtain verification from each Master Electrician or Residential Wireman who supervised the qualifying work. TDLR may contact the individuals involved to verify submitted experience.

Completion of an approved career and technology education program does not eliminate the examination requirement. Candidates using that pathway must still take and pass the Texas Residential Wireman examination.

The Residential Wireman credential has a more limited scope than the general Texas Journeyman Electrician license. The license is focused specifically on electrical installations in qualifying single-family and multifamily dwellings.

Residential Wireman licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually.

Texas adopted the 2026 National Electrical Code effective September 1, 2026. The updated Texas electrician examinations were re-referenced to the 2026 NEC for the current testing cycle.

Test Information and Study Materials

Electrical Services: Services are the largest examination category. Review Article 230, service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, service ratings, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, and residential service arrangements.

Dwelling Load Calculations: Practice Article 220 calculations for general lighting, small-appliance circuits, laundry circuits, ranges, dryers, water heaters, appliances, heating, cooling, and other dwelling loads.

Separately Derived Systems: Review transformers, grounding electrode conductors, system bonding jumpers, and related Article 250 provisions.

Feeders: Study Article 215, feeder conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, neutral requirements, equipment grounding conductors, and residential feeder calculations.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: Fourteen scored items are assigned to this category. Study required residential circuits, conductor sizing, continuous loads, circuit ratings, overcurrent protection, receptacles, and lighting outlets.

GFCI Protection: Review residential GFCI requirements for kitchens, bathrooms, garages, outdoor locations, laundry areas, basements, sinks, and other covered locations.

AFCI Protection: Study dwelling-unit AFCI requirements and understand which branch circuits and areas require arc-fault protection under the current NEC.

Grounding and Bonding: Review Article 250, grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, and fault-current paths.

Wiring Methods and Materials: Ten scored items cover wiring methods. Study nonmetallic-sheathed cable, raceways, boxes, enclosures, conductor support, underground wiring, wet locations, and protection from physical damage.

Boxes and Enclosures: Practice box-fill calculations, conductor volume allowances, grounding-conductor allowances, junction boxes, outlet boxes, and proper enclosure installation.

Conductor Ampacity: Review Article 310 tables, conductor insulation, temperature ratings, correction factors, adjustment factors, and terminal limitations.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Twelve scored items cover receptacles, switches, luminaires, appliances, HVAC equipment, and required working clearances. This is another major examination category.

Residential Appliances: Review branch-circuit requirements and disconnecting means for ranges, ovens, dryers, dishwashers, disposals, water heaters, and other permanently connected appliances.

Heating and Air-Conditioning Equipment: Study Articles 424 and 440 along with related branch-circuit, conductor, overcurrent-protection, and disconnect requirements.

Motors and Generators: Review basic motor conductor, overload, overcurrent-protection, control, and disconnect requirements. Residential standby generators and related equipment may also require familiarity with several NEC articles.

Swimming Pools and Similar Installations: Study grounding, bonding, GFCI protection, receptacle locations, pool equipment, pumps, luminaires, and applicable Article 680 requirements.

Renewable Energy Technologies: The current examination outline includes photovoltaic systems and electric vehicle charging equipment. Review the NEC provisions that apply to residential solar and EV installations.

Highlighted and Tabbed NEC Navigation: Practice moving quickly among major residential topics. A prepared NEC is most useful when the candidate combines the tabs with repeated study and knowledge of the overall code structure.

Time Management: The examination allows 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a consistent pace while leaving additional time for calculations or questions requiring several NEC references.
