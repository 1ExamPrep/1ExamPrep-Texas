### Open Book Test

The Texas Residential Wireman Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The approved technical reference is the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition**. Candidates are responsible for bringing an acceptable copy of the NEC to the examination site.

Soft-bound editions of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the exam-room reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before the examination, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new markings, notes, highlighting, or indexing during the testing session.

Open-book testing still requires familiarity with the code. Candidates who know approximately where residential service, branch-circuit, grounding, receptacle, appliance, wiring-method, and special-installation requirements are located can spend more time interpreting and answering questions.

Online exam prep can reinforce this skill by combining subject review with regular NEC navigation instead of relying only on memorization.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Electrical Services:** Services are the largest examination category. Review Article 230, service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, service ratings, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, and residential service arrangements.

**Dwelling Load Calculations:** Practice Article 220 calculations involving general lighting loads, small-appliance circuits, laundry circuits, appliances, ranges, dryers, heating, cooling, and other dwelling loads.

**Separately Derived Systems:** Review transformers, system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and related Article 250 requirements.

**Feeders:** Study Article 215, feeder conductor sizing, neutral requirements, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, and residential feeder load calculations.

**Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors:** With 14 scored items, this is one of the most important categories. Review required dwelling branch circuits, conductor ampacity, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, circuit ratings, receptacles, lighting outlets, and appliance circuits.

**GFCI Protection:** Review residential GFCI requirements for kitchens, bathrooms, garages, outdoor areas, laundry spaces, basements, sinks, and other covered locations.

**AFCI Protection:** Study the dwelling-unit branch circuits and locations that require arc-fault circuit-interrupter protection under the current NEC.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 appears across multiple residential topics. Review grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, and fault-current paths.

**Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials:** Ten scored items cover Chapter 3 wiring methods. Review nonmetallic-sheathed cable, raceways, boxes, enclosures, underground wiring, conductor support, physical protection, and wet-location requirements.

**Nonmetallic-Sheathed Cable:** Residential wiring frequently uses Type NM cable. Study permitted uses, securing and supporting, protection from physical damage, boring and notching considerations, and installation requirements.

**Boxes and Enclosures:** Practice box-fill calculations and review junction boxes, outlet boxes, covers, accessibility, conductor volume allowances, grounding-conductor allowances, and enclosure requirements.

**Conductor Ampacity:** Review Article 310 tables, conductor insulation types, temperature ratings, adjustment factors, correction factors, and terminal limitations.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Twelve scored items cover receptacles, switches, luminaires, appliances, HVAC equipment, and required working clearances.

**Receptacles:** Study required receptacle locations, spacing, grounding, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, tamper-resistant requirements, and residential circuit relationships.

**Appliances:** Review Article 422 and the branch-circuit, disconnect, overcurrent-protection, flexible-cord, and grounding requirements that apply to household appliances.

**Heating and Air-Conditioning Equipment:** Review Articles 424 and 440 along with related branch-circuit, conductor, disconnect, and overcurrent-protection requirements.

**Motors and Generators:** Review basic motor conductors, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, and applicable residential generator provisions.

**Control Devices and Disconnecting Means:** Study switches, controllers, equipment disconnects, accessibility, location, ratings, and safe isolation of electrical equipment.

**Swimming Pools and Similar Installations:** Review Article 680 requirements for grounding, bonding, GFCI protection, receptacle locations, pool equipment, pumps, luminaires, spas, and related installations.

**Renewable Energy Technologies:** The examination includes renewable-energy subjects. Review photovoltaic systems and electric vehicle charging equipment that may appear in residential installations.

**Electric Vehicle Charging Equipment:** Become familiar with branch circuits, load calculations, equipment ratings, overcurrent protection, and other NEC provisions that apply to EV charging installations.

**Photovoltaic Systems:** Review the basic NEC structure for photovoltaic systems, including conductors, disconnects, overcurrent protection, grounding, and equipment installation.

**Code Navigation:** Use the 2026 NEC throughout online exam preparation. Practice identifying the subject of the question first and then moving directly to the most likely NEC article or table.

**Timed Practice:** Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a steady pace while leaving additional time for residential load calculations and questions requiring several code references.

**Study by Exam Weight:** Prioritize Services, Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors, Electrical Equipment and Devices, and Wiring Methods and Materials while still reviewing every category on the examination outline.
