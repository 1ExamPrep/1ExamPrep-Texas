### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans:** Review Article 100 definitions, basic electrical theory, voltage, current, resistance, power, common electrical calculations, and residential plan interpretation. Understanding terminology is important because exam questions often depend on applying a specific NEC definition correctly.

**Electrical Services:** Services are the largest examination category. Review service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, service ratings, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, and dwelling service calculations.

**Service Calculations:** Practice calculating dwelling loads using the applicable NEC rules for general lighting, small-appliance circuits, laundry circuits, ranges, dryers, heating, air conditioning, motors, and other loads.

**Separately Derived Systems:** Review transformers and other qualifying systems, including system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, and equipment grounding requirements.

**Electrical Feeders:** Study feeder conductor sizing, grounded conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, feeder loads, and dwelling-unit feeder requirements.

**Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors:** With 14 scored items, branch circuits are a major study area. Review conductor ampacity, continuous loads, circuit ratings, required dwelling circuits, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, and overcurrent protection.

**Required Residential Circuits:** Study kitchen small-appliance circuits, laundry circuits, bathroom circuits, garage receptacles, outdoor outlets, HVAC equipment, appliances, and other residential branch-circuit requirements.

**GFCI Protection:** Review dwelling locations requiring ground-fault circuit-interrupter protection, including bathrooms, kitchens, garages, outdoors, basements, laundry areas, sinks, and other applicable locations.

**AFCI Protection:** Study arc-fault circuit-interrupter requirements for dwelling-unit branch circuits and the locations and conditions addressed by the NEC.

**Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials:** Ten scored items cover wiring methods, raceways, cables, conductors, boxes, fittings, enclosures, physical protection, securing and supporting, and environmental requirements.

**Nonmetallic-Sheathed Cable:** Residential installations commonly use Type NM cable. Review permitted uses, securing and supporting requirements, protection from physical damage, installation through framing members, and conductor requirements.

**Boxes and Enclosures:** Practice box-fill calculations and review conductor allowances, equipment grounding conductors, cable clamps, device allowances, accessibility, and enclosure requirements.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Twelve scored items cover electrical equipment. Review panelboards, receptacles, switches, appliances, luminaires, HVAC equipment, disconnects, equipment ratings, and working clearances.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 applies throughout residential electrical work. Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, and feeder grounding requirements.

**Motors and Generators:** Review conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, and basic generator provisions.

**Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means:** Study switches, controllers, equipment disconnects, accessibility, location, ratings, and proper equipment isolation.

**Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions:** Review the NEC rules that apply to specialized installations beyond ordinary residential branch circuits and services.

**Renewable Energy Technologies:** Study applicable photovoltaic and related renewable-energy requirements included within the examination outline, including conductors, disconnects, equipment, grounding, and overcurrent protection.

**Code Navigation:** Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC throughout preparation. Practice identifying the main subject first and then moving directly to the likely article, table, or cross-reference.

**Cross-Reference Practice:** Residential questions can involve several NEC provisions. A service, appliance, or branch-circuit question may also involve grounding, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, or disconnect requirements.

**Timed Practice:** Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a consistent pace while reserving additional time for calculations and detailed code searches.

## How does the Residential Wireman exam work?

The Residential Wireman exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has 80 scored items in 240 minutes and costs $64.
- The TDLR application fee is $20.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
- Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
