Exam Details

The Texas Residential Wireman examination is administered through PSI after an applicant receives authorization to test from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: Texas Residential Wireman
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 80
  • Time for Scored Items: 240 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

The 80 scored examination items are distributed across the following content areas:

  • Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans: 8 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 16 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 7 items
  • Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: 14 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 10 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 12 items
  • Motors and Generators: 2 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 2 items
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: 7 items
  • Renewable Energy Technologies: 2 items

Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems is the largest category with 16 scored items. Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors follows with 14 items, while Electrical Equipment and Devices contains 12 items.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials contributes 10 scored items. These categories together represent a substantial portion of the examination, making services, branch circuits, equipment, conductors, and wiring methods important study priorities.

With 240 minutes for 80 scored items, candidates have an average of approximately three minutes per scored question. Developing efficient NEC navigation can help preserve additional time for calculations and questions involving multiple code provisions.

Open Book Test

The Texas Residential Wireman examination is an open book test.

The technical reference used for the current examination is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates should become comfortable navigating the NEC before testing so they can locate applicable requirements efficiently.

Acceptable soft-bound NEC editions may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before testing, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination session.

The rental NEC included with The 1 Package is highlighted and tabbed for organized preparation and reference navigation. Candidates can use the same prepared codebook throughout the 1 year of course access to reinforce familiarity with major residential electrical provisions.

An open-book examination still requires strong preparation. Candidates should be able to recognize whether a question involves a service, feeder, branch circuit, wiring method, dwelling load calculation, equipment requirement, grounding provision, motor, special installation, or renewable energy system before searching the code.

State Requirements

The Texas Residential Wireman license is an individual electrician credential designed for residential electrical work within its defined scope. A Residential Wireman may perform qualifying electrical installations in single-family and multifamily dwellings not exceeding four stories.

Under the standard experience pathway, applicants must document at least 4,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job electrical training. Texas also provides an approved career and technical education pathway that may apply to qualifying candidates.

The Residential Wireman license is more limited in scope than the Texas Journeyman Electrician license. Candidates should understand that earning the Residential Wireman credential does not automatically provide authority to perform every type of commercial, industrial, or unrestricted electrical installation.

The examination reflects the residential focus of the credential through its strong emphasis on services, branch circuits, wiring methods, equipment, conductors, and other subjects commonly encountered in dwelling electrical systems.

The Residential Wireman license is an individual credential. Operating an electrical contracting business involves separate contractor licensing and compliance considerations beyond holding the individual Residential Wireman license.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans: Review Article 100 definitions, basic electrical theory, voltage, current, resistance, power, common electrical calculations, and residential plan interpretation. Understanding terminology is important because exam questions often depend on applying a specific NEC definition correctly.

Electrical Services: Services are the largest examination category. Review service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, service ratings, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, and dwelling service calculations.

Service Calculations: Practice calculating dwelling loads using the applicable NEC rules for general lighting, small-appliance circuits, laundry circuits, ranges, dryers, heating, air conditioning, motors, and other loads.

Separately Derived Systems: Review transformers and other qualifying systems, including system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, and equipment grounding requirements.

Electrical Feeders: Study feeder conductor sizing, grounded conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, feeder loads, and dwelling-unit feeder requirements.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: With 14 scored items, branch circuits are a major study area. Review conductor ampacity, continuous loads, circuit ratings, required dwelling circuits, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, and overcurrent protection.

Required Residential Circuits: Study kitchen small-appliance circuits, laundry circuits, bathroom circuits, garage receptacles, outdoor outlets, HVAC equipment, appliances, and other residential branch-circuit requirements.

GFCI Protection: Review dwelling locations requiring ground-fault circuit-interrupter protection, including bathrooms, kitchens, garages, outdoors, basements, laundry areas, sinks, and other applicable locations.

AFCI Protection: Study arc-fault circuit-interrupter requirements for dwelling-unit branch circuits and the locations and conditions addressed by the NEC.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials: Ten scored items cover wiring methods, raceways, cables, conductors, boxes, fittings, enclosures, physical protection, securing and supporting, and environmental requirements.

Nonmetallic-Sheathed Cable: Residential installations commonly use Type NM cable. Review permitted uses, securing and supporting requirements, protection from physical damage, installation through framing members, and conductor requirements.

Boxes and Enclosures: Practice box-fill calculations and review conductor allowances, equipment grounding conductors, cable clamps, device allowances, accessibility, and enclosure requirements.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Twelve scored items cover electrical equipment. Review panelboards, receptacles, switches, appliances, luminaires, HVAC equipment, disconnects, equipment ratings, and working clearances.

Grounding and Bonding: Article 250 applies throughout residential electrical work. Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, and feeder grounding requirements.

Motors and Generators: Review conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, and basic generator provisions.

Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: Study switches, controllers, equipment disconnects, accessibility, location, ratings, and proper equipment isolation.

Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: Review the NEC rules that apply to specialized installations beyond ordinary residential branch circuits and services.

Renewable Energy Technologies: Study applicable photovoltaic and related renewable-energy requirements included within the examination outline, including conductors, disconnects, equipment, grounding, and overcurrent protection.

Code Navigation: Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC throughout preparation. Practice identifying the main subject first and then moving directly to the likely article, table, or cross-reference.

Cross-Reference Practice: Residential questions can involve several NEC provisions. A service, appliance, or branch-circuit question may also involve grounding, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, or disconnect requirements.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a consistent pace while reserving additional time for calculations and detailed code searches.

Study by Exam Weight: Give strong attention to Services, Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors, Electrical Equipment and Devices, and Wiring Methods and Materials. Together, these categories make up a significant portion of the examination.

Business Formation: The 1 Package includes support for forming an LLC or Corporation so customers can establish a formal business entity for their contracting plans.

EIN Filing with the IRS: The package includes assistance obtaining an Employer Identification Number. An EIN can be used to open business bank accounts, manage taxes properly, hire employees, and operate the contracting business professionally.

Contractor Compliance Guidance: The 1 Package includes guidance to help customers understand compliance considerations connected with operating an electrical contracting business in Texas.

Long-Term Preparation: With 1 year of course access, candidates can divide their preparation into manageable phases. Begin with NEC organization and residential fundamentals, move into calculations and detailed subject review, and use later sessions for timed practice and reinforcement of weaker topics.
