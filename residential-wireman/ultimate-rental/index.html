Exam Details

The Texas Residential Wireman Electrician examination is administered through PSI after the applicant receives authorization to test from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: Texas Residential Wireman Electrician
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 80
  • Time for Scored Items: 240 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

The 80 scored examination items are distributed across the following content areas:

  • Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans: 8 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 16 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 7 items
  • Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: 14 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 10 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 12 items
  • Motors and Generators: 2 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 2 items
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: 7 items
  • Renewable Energy Technologies: 2 items

Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems is the largest category with 16 scored items. Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors follows with 14 items, while Electrical Equipment and Devices accounts for 12 items.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials contains another 10 items. Together, these major categories represent a substantial part of the examination and deserve regular attention throughout the study period.

With 240 minutes for 80 scored questions, candidates should practice maintaining a consistent pace while allowing additional time for residential calculations and code questions that require multiple references.

Open Book Test

The Texas Residential Wireman Electrician examination is an open book test.

The technical reference for the current examination is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates should become familiar with the code before examination day so they can locate residential electrical requirements efficiently.

Acceptable soft-bound NEC editions may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the testing room. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before testing, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination session.

The included rental NEC is highlighted and tabbed for organized study and code-navigation practice. The 1 year of course access gives candidates time to repeatedly work with the same codebook until major residential articles and tables become familiar.

Open-book testing still requires preparation. Candidates should be able to recognize whether a question involves a service, feeder, branch circuit, conductor, grounding requirement, wiring method, appliance, receptacle, pool, photovoltaic system, or electric vehicle charging installation before beginning the code search.

State Requirements

A Texas Residential Wireman is licensed to perform electrical installations in single-family and multifamily dwellings not exceeding four stories.

Applicants following the standard experience pathway must document at least 4,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Residential Wireman.

Texas also recognizes approved career and technology education pathways that can provide an alternative qualification route. Candidates using an education pathway must meet the requirements applicable to that program.

Qualifying experience must be properly documented as part of the state application process. The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation reviews the application before granting examination eligibility.

The Residential Wireman credential has a narrower scope than the Texas Journeyman Electrician license. Its scope is specifically focused on qualifying residential electrical installations.

Residential electrical work can involve services, panels, feeders, branch circuits, receptacles, lighting outlets, grounding and bonding, appliances, HVAC equipment, pools, spas, photovoltaic systems, electric vehicle charging equipment, and other electrical systems installed in qualifying dwellings.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to annual renewal and applicable continuing education requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

Electrical Services: Services are the largest examination category. Review service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, service ratings, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and residential service arrangements.

Residential Load Calculations: Practice dwelling calculations involving general lighting loads, small-appliance circuits, laundry circuits, household appliances, ranges, dryers, heating, cooling, and other residential loads.

Separately Derived Systems: Review transformers, grounding electrode conductors, system bonding jumpers, equipment grounding conductors, and related grounding and bonding requirements.

Electrical Feeders: Study feeder conductors, residential feeder calculations, grounded conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and feeder supply arrangements.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: This category contains 14 scored items. Review branch-circuit conductor sizing, circuit ratings, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, required residential circuits, receptacle circuits, appliance circuits, and lighting circuits.

GFCI Protection: Review GFCI requirements for bathrooms, garages, outdoor areas, kitchens, laundry areas, basements, sinks, pools, and other covered residential locations.

AFCI Protection: Study the dwelling-unit branch circuits and locations requiring arc-fault circuit-interrupter protection under the current NEC.

Grounding and Bonding: Article 250 is an important study area. Review grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, and effective ground-fault current paths.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials: Ten scored items cover Chapter 3 topics. Review cables, raceways, conductors, boxes, enclosures, securing and supporting, underground wiring, wet locations, and protection from physical damage.

Nonmetallic-Sheathed Cable: Residential installations commonly use Type NM cable. Study permitted uses, support requirements, physical protection, installation through framing members, and conductor requirements.

Boxes and Enclosures: Practice box-fill calculations and review conductor volume allowances, equipment grounding conductor allowances, covers, accessibility, junction boxes, and enclosure requirements.

Conductor Ampacity: Practice using NEC ampacity tables while applying temperature limitations, insulation ratings, correction factors, adjustment factors, and terminal requirements.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Twelve scored items cover receptacles, switches, luminaires, appliances, HVAC equipment, panel-related requirements, working clearances, and equipment installation.

Receptacle Requirements: Study residential receptacle spacing, required locations, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, grounding, and dwelling-unit installation rules.

Appliances: Review Article 422 requirements involving appliance circuits, disconnecting means, grounding, flexible cords, overcurrent protection, and permanently connected equipment.

Heating and Air-Conditioning Equipment: Review Articles 424 and 440 along with conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, branch-circuit, and disconnecting requirements.

Motors and Generators: Review basic motor conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit protection, controllers, disconnecting means, and residential generator requirements.

Pools and Similar Installations: Study Article 680 requirements for swimming pools, spas, grounding, bonding, receptacles, pumps, underwater luminaires, GFCI protection, and related equipment.

Renewable Energy Technologies: Review photovoltaic systems and electric vehicle charging equipment that may be installed in residential occupancies.

Photovoltaic Systems: Study conductors, equipment, overcurrent protection, disconnects, grounding, and the basic NEC structure governing residential solar installations.

Electric Vehicle Charging Equipment: Review equipment ratings, branch circuits, load calculations, overcurrent protection, and other NEC provisions applicable to EV charging installations.

Code Navigation: Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC during every stage of preparation. Practice recognizing the topic first, moving to the likely article or table, and confirming the requirement efficiently.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a consistent pace while allowing more time for residential calculations and questions that require several NEC references.

Study by Exam Weight: Give strong attention to Services, Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors, Electrical Equipment and Devices, and Wiring Methods and Materials. Together, these categories make up a substantial percentage of the examination.

Long-Term Preparation: With 1 year of course access, candidates can divide preparation into stages. Begin with basic NEC organization and residential fundamentals, move into detailed subject review and calculations, and use later study sessions for timed practice and reinforcement of weaker areas.
