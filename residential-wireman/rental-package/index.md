### Open Book Test

The Texas Residential Wireman Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The approved technical reference is the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition**. Candidates are responsible for bringing an acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

Soft-bound editions of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the examination-room reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before the examination, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination session.

The rental NEC included with this package is highlighted and tabbed to support preparation before test day. Candidates should use the book throughout the **6 months of course access** so commonly tested residential code subjects become familiar.

Open-book testing does not remove the need for code knowledge. A candidate who already knows whether a question belongs in the service, feeder, branch-circuit, grounding, appliance, wiring-method, or special-installation area can often find the applicable rule more efficiently.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Electrical Services:** Services are the largest examination category. Review Article 230, service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, service ratings, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and residential service arrangements.

**Dwelling Load Calculations:** Practice Article 220 calculations involving general lighting, small-appliance circuits, laundry circuits, ranges, dryers, appliances, heating, cooling, and other dwelling loads.

**Separately Derived Systems:** Review transformers, system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and applicable Article 250 requirements.

**Electrical Feeders:** Study feeder conductor sizing, load calculations, neutral requirements, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and residential feeder arrangements.

**Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors:** Fourteen scored items are assigned to this category. Study conductor ampacity, continuous loads, circuit ratings, overcurrent protection, required residential circuits, receptacles, lighting outlets, and appliance circuits.

**GFCI Protection:** Review GFCI requirements for bathrooms, garages, outdoor locations, kitchens, laundry areas, basements, sinks, and other covered residential locations.

**AFCI Protection:** Study the dwelling-unit branch circuits and locations requiring arc-fault circuit-interrupter protection under the current NEC.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 is important across multiple residential topics. Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, and effective fault-current paths.

**Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials:** Ten scored items cover Chapter 3 subjects. Review nonmetallic-sheathed cable, raceways, conductors, boxes, enclosures, underground wiring, support requirements, and protection from physical damage.

**Nonmetallic-Sheathed Cable:** Residential wiring frequently uses Type NM cable. Study permitted uses, securing and supporting, protection, installation through framing members, and related requirements.

**Boxes and Enclosures:** Practice box-fill calculations and review outlet boxes, junction boxes, conductor volume allowances, grounding-conductor allowances, covers, and accessibility.

**Conductor Ampacity:** Review Article 310 tables, insulation ratings, terminal temperature limitations, correction factors, adjustment factors, and conductor sizing.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Twelve scored items cover receptacles, switches, luminaires, appliances, HVAC equipment, working clearances, and related installation requirements.

**Receptacles:** Review residential receptacle spacing, required locations, grounding, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, and other dwelling-unit requirements.

**Appliances:** Study Article 422 requirements for household appliances, including branch circuits, overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, flexible cords, grounding, and permanently connected appliances.

**Heating and Air-Conditioning Equipment:** Review Articles 424 and 440 along with conductor, branch-circuit, disconnect, and overcurrent-protection requirements.

**Motors and Generators:** Review basic motor conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit protection, controllers, disconnecting means, and residential generator requirements.

**Swimming Pools and Similar Installations:** Study Article 680 requirements for grounding, bonding, GFCI protection, receptacle locations, pumps, pool equipment, luminaires, spas, and related installations.

**Renewable Energy Technologies:** Review photovoltaic systems and electric vehicle charging equipment that may be installed in residential occupancies.

**Electric Vehicle Charging Equipment:** Study branch circuits, equipment ratings, load calculations, overcurrent protection, and other NEC provisions that apply to EV charging installations.

**Photovoltaic Systems:** Review conductor requirements, disconnects, overcurrent protection, grounding, equipment, and the basic NEC structure governing residential photovoltaic installations.

**Highlighted and Tabbed Navigation:** Use the prepared rental NEC during timed practice. Learn to identify the subject first, move directly to the likely article or table, confirm the requirement, and return to the question efficiently.

**Timed Practice:** Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a consistent pace while leaving extra time for dwelling calculations and questions requiring multiple NEC references.

## How does the Residential Wireman exam work?

The Residential Wireman exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has 80 scored items in 240 minutes and costs $64.
- The TDLR application fee is $20.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
