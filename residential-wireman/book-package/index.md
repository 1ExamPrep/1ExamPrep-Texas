### Open Book Test

The Texas Residential Wireman examination is an **open book test**.

The current examination reference is the **National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own compliant NEC book to the examination site.

Any soft-bound edition of the 2026 NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted. The NEC Handbook is not allowed as the examination reference.

The NEC book may be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes prepared before the examination. Permanent index tabs manufactured or provided by the NEC book publisher are permitted. Homemade, do-it-yourself, or non-NEC tabs are not allowed.

Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or otherwise mark the NEC during the examination session. Additional paper, whether loose, taped, adhered, or stapled into the book, is also prohibited.

Open-book preparation should include repeated practice locating key residential provisions. Candidates should become familiar with Articles 100, 110, 210, 215, 220, 225, 230, 240, 250, 300, 310, 314, 334, 406, 410, 422, 424, 430, 440, 445, 625, 680, 690, and other sections commonly applied to residential electrical installations.

### Licensing Steps

1. **Complete an approved qualification pathway.** Texas Residential Wireman applicants may qualify through an approved career and technology education program or through qualifying supervised on-the-job electrical training.
2. **Complete the required experience when using the work-experience pathway.** Applicants must document 4,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training.
3. **Work under qualified supervision.** The 4,000 hours must be completed under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Residential Wireman.
4. **Obtain experience verification.** Each supervising Master Electrician or Residential Wireman must verify the applicant's qualifying experience using the required documentation.
5. **Submit the Residential Wireman license application.** The application and supporting documentation are submitted to TDLR.
6. **Receive examination approval.** After TDLR reviews and verifies the application, the candidate receives authorization to take the licensing examination.
7. **Schedule the examination through PSI.** Eligible applicants follow the PSI scheduling process for the Texas Residential Wireman examination.
8. **Prepare with the 2026 NEC.** Study the code sections, calculations, and content categories included in the examination outline.
9. **Take the Texas Residential Wireman examination.** Complete the scored and non-scored portions within the allotted testing time.
10. **Earn the required passing score.** A score of 70% or higher is required.
11. **Complete the remaining licensing requirements.** Once the experience, application, and examination requirements have been satisfied, TDLR can complete the license process.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans:** Review Article 100 definitions, basic electrical theory, residential plan reading, voltage, current, resistance, power, and common calculation methods. Understanding terminology can help candidates interpret both the question and the relevant NEC rule.

**Electrical Services:** Services are the largest examination category. Study Article 230, service conductors, service equipment, service disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, clearances, ratings, and residential service configurations.

**Dwelling Load Calculations:** Become comfortable using Article 220 for residential load calculations. Review general lighting loads, small-appliance circuits, laundry circuits, appliances, dryers, cooking equipment, heating, cooling, motors, and applicable demand factors.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Review Article 250 carefully. Residential candidates should understand grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, and grounding for separately derived systems.

**Feeders:** Study Article 215 and related residential feeder requirements. Review feeder load calculations, grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, overcurrent protection, and conductor sizing.

**Branch Circuits:** Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors account for 14 scored items. Review required dwelling-unit circuits, circuit ratings, conductor sizes, continuous loads, receptacle outlets, lighting outlets, and overcurrent protection.

**GFCI Protection:** Understand where ground-fault circuit-interrupter protection is required in dwelling units and special residential locations. Pay attention to kitchens, bathrooms, garages, outdoors, laundry areas, basements, sinks, and other NEC-covered locations.

**AFCI Protection:** Review dwelling-unit arc-fault circuit-interrupter requirements, including the circuits and areas where AFCI protection applies.

**Receptacles and Switches:** Study residential receptacle spacing, required outlets, countertop and work-surface requirements, outdoor outlets, bathroom receptacles, garage receptacles, switch locations, and device ratings.

**Wiring Methods:** Review raceways, boxes, cabinets, panels, cables, cords, and underground wiring. Residential installations commonly require familiarity with nonmetallic-sheathed cable and other wiring methods permitted by the NEC.

**Boxes and Enclosures:** Practice box-fill calculations, conductor volume allowances, equipment grounding conductor allowances, and installation requirements for outlet, device, pull, and junction boxes.

**Conductor Ampacity:** Study Article 310 tables, conductor temperature ratings, terminal limitations, ampacity adjustment, correction factors, and proper conductor sizing.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Twelve exam items cover receptacles, switches, luminaires, heating and cooling equipment, appliances, and workspace clearances. This is one of the largest study areas.

**Appliances:** Review ranges, ovens, dryers, water heaters, dishwashers, disposals, and other residential equipment. Understand branch-circuit sizing, disconnect requirements, and overcurrent protection.

**Air Conditioning, Refrigeration, and Heating Equipment:** Study Articles 424 and 440 along with related branch-circuit and disconnect requirements. Residential electricians frequently encounter heat pumps, air-conditioning condensers, electric furnaces, and fixed electric heating equipment.

**Motors and Generators:** Review basic motor conductor, protection, control, and disconnect requirements. Generator topics may include residential standby systems and equipment connections.

**Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions:** Review swimming pools, fountains, RVs, mobile homes, emergency systems, and other special installations included in the examination outline.

**Swimming Pools and Similar Installations:** Pay particular attention to grounding, bonding, GFCI protection, equipment location, receptacles, luminaires, pumps, and wiring methods around pools and fountains.

**Renewable Energy Technologies:** The current content outline includes photovoltaic systems and electric vehicle chargers. Review Articles 625 and 690 and the residential requirements that interact with these systems.

**NEC Navigation:** Practice locating rules by article rather than searching the index for every question. Efficient navigation is especially useful for services, branch circuits, dwelling calculations, grounding, wiring methods, appliances, pools, photovoltaic systems, and EV charging.

**Timed Practice:** Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Work on maintaining a consistent pace while leaving enough time for calculations and more involved code searches.
