### Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The technical reference for the current examination is the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition**. Candidates should become familiar with the structure of the NEC before examination day so they can locate applicable requirements efficiently under timed conditions.

Acceptable soft-bound NEC editions may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as the testing reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before testing, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination session.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Electric Signs and Outline Lighting:** This is the largest exam category with 31 scored items. Give substantial attention to the NEC provisions governing signs, outline lighting systems, associated wiring, markings, disconnects, grounding, and equipment.

**Article 600:** Study Article 600 carefully. Candidates should understand the rules covering electric signs, outline lighting, field-installed and manufactured systems, disconnecting means, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, and sign-related electrical equipment.

**Branch Circuits:** Review sign branch-circuit requirements together with general branch-circuit rules. Study conductor sizing, circuit ratings, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, grounding, and equipment supply requirements.

**Definitions, Calculations, and Theory:** Ten scored items cover electrical terminology and fundamentals. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, electrical circuits, conductor concepts, loads, and basic calculations.

**Electrical Services:** Eight scored items cover service conductors, service equipment, disconnects, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, and separately derived systems.

**Electrical Feeders:** Study feeder conductors, feeder loads, overcurrent protection, equipment grounding conductors, and applicable grounding and bonding requirements.

**Wiring Methods and Materials:** Review raceways, cables, conductors, boxes, fittings, enclosures, support requirements, outdoor locations, wet locations, and physical protection.

**Outdoor Sign Installations:** Sign electricians often work with electrical equipment exposed to weather. Study wet-location requirements, enclosure suitability, conductor types, raceways, fittings, and corrosion considerations.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Eight scored items cover equipment requirements. Review transformers, power supplies, switches, disconnects, overcurrent devices, control equipment, and equipment installation rules.

**Transformers and Power Supplies:** Review grounding, overcurrent protection, conductor requirements, disconnecting means, ratings, and installation provisions associated with equipment that supplies electric signs and outline lighting.

**Electrical Control Devices:** Study contactors, relays, timers, photocells, switches, and other control devices that may operate sign and lighting systems.

**Disconnecting Means:** Review accessibility, location, ratings, identification, grouping, and sign-specific disconnect provisions. Understanding disconnect requirements is particularly important for Article 600 installations.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Study Article 250 and its relationship to sign installations. Review equipment grounding conductors, bonding of metal components, effective ground-fault current paths, and grounding requirements for associated equipment.

**Conductor Ampacity:** Practice using NEC ampacity tables and applying conductor insulation ratings, terminal temperature limitations, correction factors, and adjustment factors.

**Overcurrent Protection:** Review how conductors, branch circuits, equipment, transformers, and power supplies relate to overcurrent protective devices.

**Outline Lighting:** Study the specialized requirements that apply to outline-lighting systems, including wiring, power supplies, grounding, bonding, branch circuits, and disconnects.

**Code Cross-References:** Sign questions may require using both Article 600 and general NEC articles. Practice following cross-references so related requirements can be located efficiently.

**Code Navigation:** Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC during regular study. Practice identifying the subject first, moving directly to the appropriate article or table, and confirming the requirement before answering.

**Timed Practice:** Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a steady pace while allowing additional time for questions requiring multiple references or calculations.

**Study by Exam Weight:** Give the greatest attention to Electric Signs and Outline Lighting, while maintaining strong coverage of Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors, Definitions and Theory, Services, Electrical Equipment, and Wiring Methods.

**Long-Term Preparation:** With **1 year of course access**, candidates can organize preparation into phases. Begin with general NEC familiarity, move into sign-focused Article 600 study, then add timed practice, cross-reference work, and final reinforcement of weaker subjects.

The Journeyman Sign Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has 80 scored items in 240 minutes and costs $64.

- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
