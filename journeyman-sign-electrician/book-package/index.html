About this Journeyman Sign Electrician package

Prepare for the Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician examination with the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition, the current electrical code reference used for Texas electrician examinations. Texas adopted the 2026 National Electrical Code as the state electrical code effective September 1, 2026, making this edition the primary technical reference for candidates pursuing the Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician license through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

The Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician license is designed for electricians who perform electrical sign work while working under the general supervision of a licensed Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician. Electrical sign work includes specialized electrical installations associated with signs, outline lighting, electrical sign equipment, controls, branch circuits, disconnecting means, wiring methods, and related electrical systems.

The licensing examination is administered through PSI for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). The current examination contains 80 scored items and places significant emphasis on electric signs and outline lighting. Candidates are also tested on electrical services, branch circuits, feeders, wiring methods, conductors, electrical equipment, calculations, theory, motors, controls, and disconnecting means.

The 2026 NEC is the foundation of this book package. Candidates should become comfortable navigating the code instead of attempting to memorize every requirement. The examination is open book, but the testing time is limited. Successful preparation therefore involves learning both the electrical concepts and the location of the NEC rules used to solve sign-electrician questions.

Article 600 is especially important for Journeyman Sign Electrician candidates because it covers electric signs and outline lighting. However, the examination reaches well beyond Article 600. Candidates should also understand services, branch circuits, conductor sizing, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, disconnects, transformers, equipment, and other NEC requirements that interact with sign installations.

Studying from the 2026 edition also helps candidates work with the same code cycle currently used for Texas electrician examinations. This reduces confusion caused by changes in article language, tables, exceptions, definitions, and installation requirements between NEC editions.

Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician examination is administered by PSI after TDLR has reviewed and approved an applicant's licensing application and qualifying experience.

  • Exam: Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 80
  • Time for Scored Items: 240 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

The current examination blueprint distributes the 80 scored items across the following subject areas:

  • Definitions, Calculations, and Theory: 10 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 8 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 3 items
  • Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: 10 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 6 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 8 items
  • Motors: 1 item
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 3 items
  • Electric Signs and Outline Lighting: 31 items

Electric Signs and Outline Lighting is the largest examination category, accounting for 31 of the 80 scored items. This makes sign-specific NEC knowledge the central focus of preparation, but the remaining categories still represent more than half of the examination and should not be overlooked.

Unless a question specifically states otherwise, conductors are considered copper for examination purposes.

Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician examination is an open book test.

The approved technical reference is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own acceptable NEC book to the testing center.

Any acceptable soft-bound edition of the 2026 NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound NEC copies are not permitted in the examination room. Candidates should make sure the physical copy they plan to bring complies with the examination rules before test day.

Because the examination is open book, efficient NEC navigation should be part of regular preparation. Candidates should practice locating sign requirements, definitions, branch-circuit rules, service provisions, conductor tables, grounding and bonding requirements, and wiring methods without spending excessive time searching.

Article 600 should become especially familiar. Candidates should know how sign requirements interact with other NEC chapters rather than treating Article 600 as an isolated section. A sign question may require information from Article 600 together with requirements for conductors, grounding, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, disconnects, or transformers.

The NEC can help confirm a code rule, but it does not replace electrical understanding. Candidates still need to recognize which requirement applies and how to use the information found in the code to answer the question.

Licensing Steps

  1. Gain qualifying electrical sign experience. Texas requires substantial supervised on-the-job training before a person can qualify for the Journeyman Sign Electrician license.
  2. Work under qualified supervision. Qualifying experience must be obtained under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician.
  3. Complete 8,000 hours of on-the-job training. Applicants must document the required experience before applying for the Journeyman Sign Electrician license.
  4. Obtain experience verification. Each Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician who supervised qualifying work must document the applicant's experience using the required TDLR verification process.
  5. Submit the Journeyman Sign Electrician application. The completed application and supporting documentation are submitted to TDLR for review.
  6. Receive examination approval. After TDLR verifies the application and experience, the applicant receives authorization to take the licensing examination.
  7. Schedule the examination through PSI. Approved applicants follow the PSI scheduling instructions provided for Texas electrician examinations.
  8. Prepare using the 2026 NEC. Review both sign-specific requirements and the general electrical subjects identified in the examination content outline.
  9. Take the Journeyman Sign Electrician examination. Complete the scored and non-scored items within the allotted testing period.
  10. Earn the required passing score. A score of 70% or higher is required to pass.
  11. Complete the licensing process. Satisfy any remaining TDLR requirements before the license is issued.

State Requirements

A Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician is licensed to perform electrical sign work while working under the general supervision of a Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician.

Applicants must complete at least 8,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician licensed in Texas.

The qualifying experience must be documented and verified by the individuals who supervised the applicant. Applicants who worked under more than one qualifying supervisor must obtain the required verification for the experience claimed under each supervisor.

Electrical sign work is a specialized area of the Texas electrical licensing program. Work can involve manufacturing, installing, maintaining, extending, connecting, or reconnecting electrical wiring systems and associated equipment used with signs and outline lighting within the scope authorized by Texas law.

The sign-electrician licensing structure distinguishes Journeyman Sign Electricians from general Journeyman Electricians. The Journeyman Sign Electrician credential is focused on electrical sign work rather than the full scope of general electrical work authorized by a Journeyman Electrician license.

Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year and must be renewed annually. License holders must comply with applicable renewal and continuing-education requirements.

Texas adopted the 2026 National Electrical Code effective September 1, 2026. The updated electrician examinations were also re-referenced to the 2026 NEC for the current testing cycle.

Test Information and Study Materials

Electric Signs and Outline Lighting: This is the most important individual category on the examination, accounting for 31 scored items. Study Article 600 thoroughly, including general requirements for electric signs and outline lighting, installation, listing and marking, branch circuits, disconnects, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, and equipment.

Sign Disconnecting Means: Review the location, accessibility, identification, and operating requirements that apply to disconnecting means for signs and outline-lighting systems. Understand when disconnects are required and how they relate to sign equipment.

Branch Circuits for Signs: Study branch-circuit sizing, conductor requirements, overcurrent protection, and the NEC provisions specifically affecting signs. Candidates should understand both Article 600 rules and the general branch-circuit requirements in Article 210.

Definitions, Calculations, and Theory: Ten scored items cover basic electrical theory, calculations, terminology, and NEC definitions. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, load calculations, conductor relationships, and definitions that influence sign installations.

Services and Service Equipment: Eight questions involve services, service equipment, and separately derived systems. Study service conductors, service ratings, disconnecting means, service equipment, grounding, bonding, and installation requirements.

Feeders: Review feeder conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding, neutral requirements, and how feeders serve sign circuits and related electrical equipment.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: Ten scored items cover branch circuits and conductors. Study conductor ampacity, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, required circuits, temperature limitations, and conductor sizing.

Grounding and Bonding: Sign installations require careful attention to grounding and bonding. Review Article 250 requirements, equipment grounding conductors, bonding of metal parts, grounding paths, and the relationship between sign equipment and the electrical system supplying it.

Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: Six scored items address wiring methods. Review Chapter 3 raceways, cables, conductor installation, boxes, enclosures, support, physical protection, wet locations, and outdoor installations.

Outdoor and Wet Locations: Electrical signs are frequently installed outdoors. Review weatherproof and weather-resistant equipment requirements, wet-location wiring methods, raceway provisions, enclosures, fittings, conductor insulation, and protection from environmental exposure.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Eight questions cover electrical equipment and devices. Study transformers, switches, luminaires, overcurrent devices, power supplies, controllers, and other equipment associated with electrical sign installations.

Motors: The examination includes a smaller motor category, but candidates should still know basic Article 430 requirements relating to motor conductors, protection, controls, and disconnecting means when motor-operated equipment is involved.

Control Devices: Review switches, relays, contactors, time controls, photocells, and other control equipment that may be used to operate signs or outline lighting.

NEC Article 600 Navigation: Practice finding Article 600 requirements quickly. Pay attention to cross-references that direct the user to other NEC articles because many exam questions require applying a general code requirement together with a sign-specific provision.

Transformers and Power Supplies: Signs and lighting systems may use transformers or power supplies. Review installation, overcurrent protection, grounding, conductor requirements, and applicable equipment provisions.

LED Sign Systems: Modern signs commonly use light-emitting-diode technologies. Candidates should understand how the NEC treats sign equipment, power supplies, wiring, disconnects, grounding, and installation requirements regardless of the specific lighting technology used.

Outline Lighting: Study the NEC requirements that apply specifically to outline-lighting systems, including wiring, equipment, disconnecting means, and installation.

Raceways and Boxes: Review raceway fill, box sizing, fittings, conductor protection, supports, and outdoor installation considerations. Sign work frequently involves transitions between building wiring and sign equipment.

Conductor Ampacity: Practice using the Article 310 ampacity tables and applying temperature limitations, adjustment factors, correction factors, and termination requirements.

Overcurrent Protection: Understand how branch-circuit and equipment overcurrent protection applies to sign loads. Review Article 240 and the related requirements referenced by Article 600.

Timed Reference Practice: Candidates have 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice solving both direct code questions and calculation questions under time limits so NEC searches do not consume too much of the testing period.
