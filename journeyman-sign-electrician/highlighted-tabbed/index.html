Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician examination is administered by PSI after an applicant has been approved to test by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 80
  • Time for Scored Items: 240 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

The examination content is distributed across the following major subject areas:

  • Definitions, Calculations, and Theory: 10 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 8 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 3 items
  • Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: 10 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 6 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 8 items
  • Motors: 1 item
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 3 items
  • Electric Signs and Outline Lighting: 31 items

Electric Signs and Outline Lighting is the largest examination category, accounting for 31 scored items. This makes Article 600 and related sign-installation requirements a major focus of preparation.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors and Definitions, Calculations, and Theory each account for 10 scored items. Services and Electrical Equipment and Devices each contribute eight items. Candidates should therefore prepare broadly rather than studying Article 600 alone.

Unless an examination question specifically states otherwise, conductors are treated as copper.

Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician examination is an open book test.

The current approved examination reference is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own compliant NEC book to the examination site.

A soft-bound edition of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound NEC copies are not permitted in the examination room.

The NEC may be highlighted, underlined, and contain permitted notes prepared before the examination session. Permanent NEC index tabs manufactured or provided for use with the NEC may also be used under the applicable testing rules.

Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination. Homemade or temporary tabs and additional loose paper are not permitted.

The highlighted and tabbed NEC included with this package is intended to support organized preparation. Candidates should still practice using the book repeatedly so they can move quickly between Article 600 and the general NEC provisions that often apply to sign installations.

Licensing Steps

  1. Gain qualifying electrical sign experience. Texas requires substantial supervised on-the-job training before an applicant may qualify for the Journeyman Sign Electrician license.
  2. Work under qualified supervision. Qualifying experience must be completed under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician.
  3. Complete 8,000 hours of on-the-job training. Applicants must document the required electrical sign experience before applying for the license.
  4. Obtain experience verification. Each Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician who supervised qualifying work must verify the applicable experience.
  5. Submit the Journeyman Sign Electrician application. The application and experience documentation are submitted to TDLR.
  6. Receive examination approval. After the application and experience are reviewed and verified, the applicant receives authorization to test.
  7. Schedule the examination through PSI. Approved candidates follow the PSI scheduling process for Texas electrician examinations.
  8. Prepare using the 2026 NEC. Study Article 600 and the broader electrical subjects identified in the examination content outline.
  9. Take the Journeyman Sign Electrician examination. Complete the scored and non-scored items within the allotted testing time.
  10. Earn the required passing score. Candidates must achieve a score of at least 70%.
  11. Complete the remaining licensing requirements. Finish any remaining TDLR requirements before the license is issued.

State Requirements

A Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician is licensed to perform electrical sign work while working under the general supervision of a Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician.

Applicants must complete at least 8,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician.

The qualifying experience must be properly documented by each supervisor whose hours are included in the application. TDLR reviews the submitted information before authorizing the applicant to take the licensing examination.

The Journeyman Sign Electrician license is a specialized credential. It is focused on electrical sign work rather than the full scope of general electrical work associated with a Texas Journeyman Electrician license.

Electrical sign work involves electrical wiring systems, apparatus, equipment, and components associated with signs and outline lighting within the scope established by Texas law and the NEC.

The license is valid for one year and must be renewed annually. License holders must also satisfy the continuing education requirements that apply to Texas electrician credentials.

Test Information and Study Materials

Electric Signs and Outline Lighting: With 31 scored items, this is the largest examination category. Article 600 should receive substantial attention during preparation. Study sign construction and installation requirements, branch circuits, disconnecting means, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, equipment, markings, and outline-lighting provisions.

Article 600 Navigation: Practice finding sign requirements quickly. Article 600 frequently directs candidates to general NEC requirements found elsewhere in the code, so learning the cross-references is important.

Sign Branch Circuits: Review branch-circuit requirements that apply specifically to signs and outline lighting as well as the general rules of Article 210. Study conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, continuous loads, and circuit requirements.

Disconnecting Means: Understand disconnect location, accessibility, identification, ratings, and other requirements affecting electrical signs and related equipment.

Grounding and Bonding: Review Article 250 along with sign-specific grounding and bonding provisions. Candidates should understand equipment grounding conductors, bonding of metal parts, effective ground-fault current paths, and grounding requirements for associated equipment.

Definitions, Calculations, and Theory: Ten scored items address electrical theory and calculations. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, conductor relationships, loads, NEC definitions, and basic calculation procedures.

Services: Eight scored items involve services, service equipment, and separately derived systems. Review service conductors, service disconnects, equipment ratings, grounding, bonding, transformers, and related requirements.

Feeders: Review feeder conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and load considerations.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: Ten scored items cover branch circuits and conductors. Study conductor ampacity, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, temperature limitations, adjustment factors, and correction factors.

Wiring Methods and Materials: Review Chapter 3 raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, supports, conductor protection, wet locations, outdoor wiring, and appropriate methods for sign installations.

Outdoor and Wet Locations: Many signs are exposed to weather. Review wet-location conductors, enclosures, fittings, raceways, equipment ratings, corrosion protection, and weather-related installation requirements.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Eight scored items address equipment and devices. Study transformers, power supplies, switches, overcurrent devices, luminaires, electrical equipment, and applicable installation requirements.

Transformers and Power Supplies: Electrical signs may use transformers and power supplies. Review conductor requirements, protection, grounding, disconnects, equipment ratings, and installation methods.

Motors: Although motors represent a smaller portion of the examination, candidates should understand basic motor conductor, protection, controller, and disconnect requirements.

Controls: Review contactors, relays, timers, photocells, switches, and other devices used to control sign and lighting systems.

Outline Lighting: Study equipment, wiring methods, branch circuits, disconnects, grounding, and other requirements specifically associated with outline-lighting installations.

Conductor Ampacity: Practice using Article 310 tables and applying temperature ratings, terminal limitations, correction factors, and adjustment factors.

Overcurrent Protection: Review Article 240 and related provisions governing conductor and equipment protection. Understand how general overcurrent requirements interact with Article 600.

Highlighted and Tabbed NEC Navigation: Use the prepared codebook during timed study sessions. Practice identifying the subject, moving to the correct tabbed section, confirming the applicable requirement, and returning to the question efficiently.

Time Management: Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a steady pace while leaving enough time for calculations and questions that require more than one NEC reference.
