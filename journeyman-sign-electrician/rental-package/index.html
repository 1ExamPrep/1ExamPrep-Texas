Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician examination is administered through PSI after the applicant has received approval to test from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 80
  • Time for Scored Items: 240 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book

The current examination content outline distributes the 80 scored items across the following subject areas:

  • Definitions, Calculations, and Theory: 10 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 8 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 3 items
  • Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: 10 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 6 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 8 items
  • Motors: 1 item
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 3 items
  • Electric Signs and Outline Lighting: 31 items

Electric Signs and Outline Lighting is the largest category with 31 scored items. This makes sign-specific NEC preparation a major study priority. Candidates should still prepare broadly because nearly two-thirds of the exam also covers general electrical knowledge that supports sign installations.

Definitions, Calculations, and Theory and Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors each contain 10 scored items. Services and Electrical Equipment and Devices contain eight items each, while Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials accounts for six.

This content distribution rewards a balanced preparation plan. Article 600-related subjects deserve substantial attention, but general electrical fundamentals should remain part of daily study.

Licensing Steps

  1. Gain qualifying electrical sign experience. Texas requires substantial supervised on-the-job training before an applicant may qualify for the Journeyman Sign Electrician license.
  2. Work under qualified supervision. Qualifying experience must be completed under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician.
  3. Complete 8,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training. The required experience must be documented as part of the licensing process.
  4. Obtain experience verification. Each qualifying Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician who supervised the applicant must verify the applicable experience.
  5. Submit the Journeyman Sign Electrician application. The application and required experience documentation are submitted to TDLR.
  6. Receive examination authorization. After the application and experience are reviewed, eligible applicants are approved to test.
  7. Schedule the examination through PSI. Approved candidates follow the PSI scheduling process.
  8. Prepare for the examination. Give substantial attention to Electric Signs and Outline Lighting while also reviewing the general electrical subjects included on the exam outline.
  9. Use the highlighted and tabbed rental book. Practice code navigation regularly throughout the study period.
  10. Use the full course-access period effectively. The package includes 6 months of course access for structured preparation and review.
  11. Take the Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician examination. Complete the scored and non-scored questions within the allotted testing time.
  12. Earn the required passing score. Candidates must achieve at least 70%.
  13. Complete all remaining licensing requirements. Follow the applicable TDLR process for issuance of the license.

State Requirements

A Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician is licensed to perform electrical sign work under the general supervision of a Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician.

Applicants must document at least 8,000 hours of on-the-job training under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician or Master Sign Electrician.

The experience submitted with the application must be properly verified. TDLR reviews the documentation before authorizing eligible applicants to take the examination.

The Journeyman Sign Electrician credential is a specialized electrical license. It focuses on electrical sign work rather than the broader electrical scope associated with the Texas Journeyman Electrician license.

Electrical sign work can involve the installation, alteration, servicing, and maintenance of electrical signs and outline lighting systems within the scope established for the credential. The work may include branch circuits, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, transformers, power supplies, controls, disconnects, and related electrical equipment.

Understanding this specialized scope can help candidates connect their practical experience to the subjects appearing on the examination. Article 600 is central to sign work, but many sign installations also require applying general NEC provisions from other chapters.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to annual renewal requirements and the continuing education requirements that apply to the credential.

Test Information and Study Materials

Electric Signs and Outline Lighting: This is the largest exam category with 31 scored items. Candidates should devote significant study time to electrical sign and outline-lighting requirements.

Article 600: Study sign construction and installation requirements, branch circuits, markings, disconnecting means, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, electrical equipment, and other rules applicable to electric signs and outline lighting.

Sign Branch Circuits: Review sign-specific branch-circuit provisions along with general branch-circuit rules. Understand conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, circuit loading, and equipment supply requirements.

Disconnecting Means: Study disconnect location, accessibility, ratings, identification, grouping, and the requirements associated with sign equipment.

Grounding and Bonding: Review equipment grounding conductors, bonding of metal components, effective ground-fault current paths, and the grounding requirements that apply to sign installations.

Definitions, Calculations, and Theory: Ten scored items address electrical terminology and fundamentals. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, electrical circuits, and common calculations.

Electrical Services: Eight scored items involve services, service equipment, and separately derived systems. Study service conductors, disconnects, equipment ratings, grounding, bonding, and transformer-related requirements.

Electrical Feeders: Review feeder conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounded conductors, grounding and bonding, and feeder supply arrangements.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: Ten scored items cover conductor ampacity, branch-circuit loads, continuous loads, circuit ratings, overcurrent protection, and related calculations.

Wiring Methods and Materials: Six scored items address raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, supports, conductors, weather exposure, physical protection, and general Chapter 3 requirements.

Outdoor Sign Installations: Many electrical signs are exposed to weather. Study wet-location equipment, weather-resistant enclosures, appropriate raceways, conductor suitability, corrosion protection, and outdoor wiring requirements.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Eight scored items cover equipment requirements. Review transformers, power supplies, luminaires, switches, overcurrent devices, and related electrical equipment.

Transformers and Power Supplies: Sign installations may rely on transformers and power supplies. Review conductor protection, equipment ratings, disconnecting means, grounding, bonding, and installation requirements.

Motors: Although motors represent a smaller portion of the exam, candidates should still understand basic conductor, protection, control, and disconnect requirements for motor loads.

Electrical Control Devices: Review contactors, switches, relays, timers, photocells, and other control equipment that may operate sign and lighting systems.

Outline Lighting: Study wiring methods, branch circuits, grounding, bonding, disconnects, equipment, and the specialized code requirements associated with outline-lighting systems.

Conductor Ampacity: Practice using NEC ampacity tables and applying insulation ratings, terminal temperature requirements, correction factors, and adjustment factors.

Overcurrent Protection: Review the relationship between conductor ampacity, protective devices, equipment requirements, and sign branch circuits.

Code Cross-References: Sign questions often require movement between sign-specific rules and general NEC provisions. Practice following references between Article 600 and the general requirements for conductors, grounding, branch circuits, wiring methods, and equipment.

Highlighted and Tabbed Navigation: Use the rental book during timed practice. Identify the main subject first, move to the relevant tab, locate the applicable rule, and confirm the answer efficiently.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a steady pace while leaving additional time for calculations and questions requiring several code references.

Study by Exam Weight: Give the greatest attention to Electric Signs and Outline Lighting while maintaining strong coverage of Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors, Definitions and Theory, Services, Electrical Equipment, and Wiring Methods.
