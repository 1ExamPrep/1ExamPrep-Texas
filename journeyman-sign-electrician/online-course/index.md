### Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Sign Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The current approved technical reference is the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

Soft-bound editions of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs prepared before the examination. Candidates may not add new markings, notes, or indexing during the testing session.

Open-book testing still requires efficient preparation. Candidates who know approximately where sign, grounding, branch-circuit, wiring-method, service, and disconnect requirements are located can spend more time interpreting questions and less time searching through the codebook.

Article 600 often works together with other NEC chapters. Candidates should practice following cross-references rather than treating the sign article as an isolated section of the code.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Electric Signs and Outline Lighting:** With 31 scored items, this is the most important individual examination category. Article 600 should receive substantial attention throughout the study process.

**Article 600:** Review the general requirements for electric signs and outline lighting, including installation, branch circuits, markings, disconnecting means, grounding, bonding, wiring methods, equipment, and related requirements.

**Sign Branch Circuits:** Study the branch-circuit provisions that apply specifically to signs along with the general rules of Article 210. Candidates should be comfortable with conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, continuous loads, and circuit requirements.

**Disconnecting Means:** Understand requirements for disconnect location, accessibility, ratings, grouping, identification, and control of sign equipment.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Review Article 250 and sign-specific grounding and bonding requirements. Study equipment grounding conductors, bonding of metal components, effective ground-fault current paths, and equipment grounding connections.

**Definitions, Calculations, and Theory:** Ten scored items cover electrical theory and terminology. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, electrical calculations, and important NEC definitions.

**Electrical Services:** Eight scored items cover services, service equipment, and separately derived systems. Study service conductors, service disconnects, grounding, bonding, equipment ratings, and transformers.

**Feeders:** Review feeder conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and feeder load requirements.

**Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors:** Ten scored items cover branch circuits and conductor requirements. Study conductor ampacity, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, temperature limitations, correction factors, and adjustment factors.

**Wiring Methods and Materials:** Review raceways, boxes, enclosures, cables, conductors, fittings, support requirements, wet locations, outdoor installations, and physical protection.

**Outdoor Sign Installations:** Many sign systems are exposed to weather. Study wet-location requirements, weather-resistant equipment, suitable enclosures, corrosion protection, outdoor raceways, and conductor suitability.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Eight scored items cover electrical equipment and devices. Review transformers, power supplies, switches, overcurrent devices, luminaires, and equipment installation requirements.

**Transformers and Power Supplies:** Sign systems may use transformers and power supplies. Review conductor protection, disconnecting means, equipment ratings, grounding, bonding, and installation requirements.

**Motors:** Although motors account for only one scored item, candidates should still understand basic motor conductor, protection, controller, and disconnect requirements.

**Control Devices:** Study contactors, relays, timers, photocells, switches, and other control devices used in sign and outline-lighting systems.

**Outline Lighting:** Review wiring methods, branch circuits, disconnects, grounding, bonding, equipment, and the specific NEC requirements that apply to outline-lighting systems.

**Conductor Ampacity:** Become comfortable using Article 310 tables and applying conductor insulation ratings, temperature limitations, adjustment factors, correction factors, and terminal limitations.

**Overcurrent Protection:** Review Article 240 and related requirements. Understand how overcurrent-protection rules apply to sign branch circuits, conductors, equipment, and associated electrical systems.

**Code Cross-References:** Article 600 frequently depends on requirements found elsewhere in the NEC. Practice following cross-references to Articles 110, 210, 240, 250, 300, 310, and other applicable sections.

**Code Navigation Practice:** Use the 2026 NEC during online preparation. Practice recognizing the type of question first, then locating the applicable article, section, table, or exception.

**Timed Practice:** Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a steady pace while leaving enough time for calculations and questions that require multiple code references.

**Study by Exam Weight:** Give Article 600 substantial attention because Electric Signs and Outline Lighting accounts for 31 scored items. At the same time, continue reviewing theory, branch circuits, services, equipment, and wiring methods so the remainder of the examination is covered effectively.
