### Exam Details

The Texas Plumbing Inspector examination is a state licensing examination administered through Pearson VUE for the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners.

- **Exam:** Texas Plumbing Inspector
- **Licensing Agency:** Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners
- **Testing Provider:** Pearson VUE
- **Exam Format:** Computer based
- **Passing Score:** 70%
- **Exam Type:** Closed book

The TSBPE Plumbing Inspector Examination Review Course identifies the examination content as including **248 multiple-choice questions** plus four pipe-sizing exercises. The multiple-choice portion includes:

- **Plumbing Codes in General:** 193 questions
- **Water Heater Installation:** 30 questions
- **Plumbing License Law and Board Rules:** 25 questions

The examination review material also identifies four major sizing exercises:

- Fuel gas pipe sizing
- Water pipe sizing
- Gas-fired appliance vent sizing
- Drainage system pipe sizing

Many plumbing-code questions involve evaluating full-size, reduced-scale, or illustrated plumbing installations. Candidates should therefore prepare not only to recall code requirements but also to recognize violations in practical system layouts.

Water-heater inspection is a substantial exam topic. Candidates should expect to evaluate installations for issues involving relief valves, discharge piping, combustion air, venting, location, shutoff valves, drain pans, gas connections, and other requirements.

### Closed Book Test

The Texas Plumbing Inspector examination is a **closed book test**. Personal plumbing codes, notes, study guides, papers, and other unauthorized aids are not permitted in the examination room.

The books in this package are intended for study before exam day. Candidates should use them to understand plumbing-code requirements well enough to identify correct and incorrect installations without relying on a personal reference during the test.

A useful preparation method is to study one topic at a time and then practice recalling the rules without the book open. For example, after reviewing water-heater requirements, candidates can work through installation scenarios and identify possible violations from memory before checking the code.

The same approach can be used for drainage, venting, water supply, fuel gas, traps, fixtures, and general installation requirements. Closed-book recall becomes more effective when candidates understand why a rule exists and how it affects an actual plumbing system.

Pipe-sizing exercises should also receive dedicated practice. Candidates should learn the process for evaluating fixture loads, developed lengths, system demand, and the sizing information provided for each type of piping system.

### Licensing Steps

1. **Meet the basic applicant requirements.** Applicants must satisfy the eligibility requirements established by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners.
2. **Meet an approved qualification pathway.** Applicants may qualify through an eligible professional license or through the required combination of plumbing-related training and experience.
3. **Complete 500 hours of training and experience when using that pathway.** Applicants who do not already hold one of the recognized qualifying licenses may qualify through a combined total of 500 hours of approved plumbing-industry training and experience.
4. **Document qualifying experience.** Applicable on-the-job plumbing experience or training in plumbing-code enforcement must be documented through the required Plumbing Inspector employment certification process.
5. **Submit fingerprints.** Texas requires fingerprint data for plumbing examination applicants who have not previously completed the requirement with TSBPE.
6. **Submit the Plumbing Inspector examination application.** Provide the required application and supporting documentation to the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners.
7. **Receive Authorization to Test.** After TSBPE approves the application, Pearson VUE provides examination authorization and scheduling instructions.
8. **Schedule the examination through Pearson VUE.** Select an available computer-based testing appointment.
9. **Prepare for code, inspection, law, and sizing topics.** Study plumbing systems, water heaters, Texas law and rules, and the four principal sizing applications.
10. **Take and pass the Plumbing Inspector examination.** Candidates must achieve the required passing score.
11. **Complete the licensing and affiliation requirements.** After passing, complete the remaining TSBPE licensing process and provide documentation of affiliation with a political subdivision when applicable.

### State Requirements

Texas provides several ways to qualify for the Plumbing Inspector examination. An applicant may qualify by holding a current Texas **Master Plumber** license, Texas **Journeyman Plumber** license, Texas **Professional Engineer** license, Texas **Architect** registration, or a Plumbing Inspector license from another state with substantially equivalent requirements.

Applicants who do not hold one of those qualifying credentials may become eligible by completing a combined total of **500 hours of training and experience in the plumbing industry**.

Qualifying training may include approved plumbing-related certifications, training academies, endorsement programs, backflow-prevention training, continuing professional education, plumbing-trade work experience, and supervised plumbing-code enforcement training.

On-the-job work experience in the plumbing trade or an approved similar trade may contribute toward the 500-hour requirement. Documented on-the-job training in plumbing-code enforcement under a licensed Plumbing Inspector may also contribute toward eligibility.

A licensed Texas Plumbing Inspector may inspect plumbing work within the jurisdiction of a political subdivision with which the inspector is affiliated. Affiliation is established through employment by or a contract with the political subdivision to perform plumbing inspections.

A Plumbing Inspector may also perform customer service inspection certification for a public drinking-water system under applicable Texas requirements.

Texas Plumbing Inspectors must avoid financial or advisory interests in plumbing companies when performing inspection duties within their affiliated jurisdiction. The inspector's responsibility is to apply adopted plumbing requirements and Texas plumbing law consistently.

Texas plumbing licenses renew annually. Licensees must satisfy applicable continuing professional education requirements for renewal. Plumbing Inspector licenses renew on the state's inspector renewal schedule rather than automatically sharing the expiration date of a Journeyman or Master Plumber license held by the same individual.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions:** Begin by learning proper plumbing terminology. Correct understanding of terms such as building drain, building sewer, fixture drain, trap arm, vent stack, stack vent, water service, developed length, and drainage fixture unit makes other code provisions easier to interpret.

**General Regulations:** Review permitted and prohibited materials, installation methods, pipe protection, support, bedding, corrosion protection, structural penetrations, testing, and inspection requirements.

**Plumbing Fixtures:** Study approved fixtures, fixture fittings, installation clearances, water closets, lavatories, showers, bathtubs, sinks, automatic clothes washers, dishwashers, and other plumbing fixtures.

**Water Heaters:** This is a major examination subject. Review approved locations, supply valves, temperature and pressure relief valves, discharge piping, drain pans, combustion air, venting, fuel-gas connections, and common installation violations.

**Water Supply and Distribution:** Review piping materials, installation methods, cross-connection control, vacuum breakers, air gaps, pressure regulation, backflow prevention, valves, fixture-unit demand, and water-pipe sizing.

**Sanitary Drainage:** Study approved materials, slope, cleanouts, changes in direction, drainage fittings, building drains, building sewers, testing, fixture-unit loads, and drainage-pipe sizing.

**Indirect and Special Waste:** Review air gaps, indirect connections, food-handling applications, health-care and laboratory waste, corrosive waste, and other special drainage arrangements.

**Venting:** Understand individual vents, common vents, wet venting, stack vents, vent stacks, grading, termination, vent sizing, and connection requirements.

**Traps:** Review fixture traps, trap seals, prohibited traps, protection against siphonage and backpressure, and trap-arm requirements.

**Fuel Gas:** Study approved piping materials, gas-pipe installation, testing, appliance connections, shutoff valves, regulators, combustion air, and gas-fired appliance venting.

**Fuel Gas Pipe Sizing:** Practice evaluating a piping diagram serving multiple appliances and determining minimum pipe sizes from the meter through each branch.

**Water Pipe Sizing:** Practice evaluating fixture loads and selecting minimum water-service and distribution sizes based on the information provided.

**Gas-Fired Appliance Vent Sizing:** Review individual and combined vent systems and practice selecting appropriate vent sizes for gas-fired appliances.

**Drainage Pipe Sizing:** Practice calculating drainage fixture units and selecting minimum pipe sizes for multi-story drainage systems.

**Inspection Recognition:** Candidates should practice identifying violations from photographs, diagrams, scaled installations, and written scenarios. Inspector-level preparation requires recognizing what is wrong with an installation as well as knowing the correct requirement.

**Texas Plumbing License Law and Board Rules:** Review inspector responsibilities, license requirements, supervision, enforcement, prohibited conduct, and the standards that apply to plumbing professionals and inspectors.

**Closed-Book Practice:** After learning a code topic, answer questions without consulting the reference. This helps build the recall needed for a closed-book computer-based examination.
