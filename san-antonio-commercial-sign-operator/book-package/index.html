Open Book Test

The ICC 974 Texas (San Antonio) Commercial Sign Operator exam is an open book test. Open book is an advantage only if you can navigate the reference quickly. With 70 questions in 3 hours, you do not have unlimited time to search page-by-page. The goal is to build a repeatable routine that helps you locate the right section fast and confirm the best answer without overthinking.

Open-book success usually comes down to four habits:

  • Know where definitions live: Many questions start with terminology, classifications, or rule triggers that depend on the ordinance wording.
  • Use keywords from the question: Pull key terms from the question and use them to locate the relevant ordinance section.
  • Read above and below the line you found: Exceptions, limitations, and conditions are often located nearby.
  • Practice pacing: Timed practice helps you avoid spending too long on any single question.

Many candidates improve dramatically simply by practicing “find and confirm” drills: set a timer, choose a topic from the outline, and practice locating the governing ordinance language quickly and consistently.

Test Information and Study Materials

To get the most out of your reference, it helps to study in a way that matches how the exam is built. The exam outline is organized by broad competency areas, and your study should mirror those buckets. Instead of reading straight through the ordinance, build a cycle of learning, practice, and navigation drills that targets each category.

1) Build a strong foundation in terms and definitions (15%)

Definitions are not just vocabulary—they’re the foundation for how the ordinance is applied. A single definition can change the outcome of a permit-related question or a compliance scenario. A strong method is to keep a personal glossary of the terms you see repeatedly while studying, then practice applying those definitions in “either/or” scenarios.

  • Study tip: When you learn a definition, write one short example of how it would affect a decision on a job.
  • Navigation tip: Practice finding definition sections quickly. This helps you avoid losing time when the exam asks about classification language.

2) Keep plan reading simple and steady (5%)

Plan Reading is a smaller portion of the outline, but it can still be a difference-maker because the questions tend to reward careful attention. Your goal here is steady familiarity: be comfortable interpreting basic plan-related information and understanding how it connects to a compliance decision.

  • Study tip: Don’t overbuild this section. Short, repeated practice is usually more effective than one big cram session.
  • Exam pacing tip: Because plan reading is a smaller weight, avoid letting these questions consume too much time.

3) Treat licensing and permit regulations as a “process” category (20%)

Licensing and permit regulation questions often test whether you understand how regulated sign work is approached and documented within the ordinance framework. The best way to study this section is to think in steps: what triggers oversight, what expectations apply, and what the ordinance requires in order for work to move forward properly.

  • Study tip: Create a short checklist of the most common permit-related concepts you encounter in the ordinance, then practice locating the exact section that supports each concept.
  • Accuracy tip: When answer choices are similar, the difference is usually a condition, exception, or a specific piece of ordinance wording.

4) Make general requirements and safety a weekly priority (30%)

This is one of the highest-weight categories on the 974Y outline. You want this material to feel familiar. While every ordinance is written differently, this category commonly involves the “baseline rules” that govern how signs are regulated to support safe installation and ongoing compliance expectations.

A good approach is to study this material in layers:

  • Layer 1: Learn the headings and how the ordinance organizes safety-driven requirements.
  • Layer 2: Practice finding the rule quickly from a keyword in a question.
  • Layer 3: Practice reading for conditions, exceptions, and details that change the final answer.

Because this category is heavily weighted, your goal is speed with confidence. You should be able to locate key sections quickly and verify the correct answer without second-guessing your navigation.

5) Train for erection and maintenance with scenario-style studying (30%)

Erection and Maintenance is also a major portion of the exam. Many candidates benefit most when they study this category through realistic scenarios rather than “page reading.” The exam is multiple-choice, so scenario thinking helps you identify what the question is truly asking and what part of the ordinance would control the decision.

  • Study tip: Turn headings into practice prompts. For each major topic you study, ask: “If I saw this on a job, what would the ordinance require me to do?”
  • Navigation tip: Build a mental map of where erection-focused material and maintenance-focused material tend to appear, so you’re not searching blindly.

A practical open-book routine for exam day

Open book works best when you use a consistent sequence. Here’s a simple routine many candidates follow during practice so it feels natural on the exam:

  1. Read the question twice. The first read is for the general topic; the second read is for keywords.
  2. Identify 2–3 keywords. Pick words that would likely appear in headings or ordinance language.
  3. Locate the controlling section. Find the rule, then read the surrounding text for conditions or exceptions.
  4. Eliminate wrong answers. Use the ordinance language to rule out close distractors.
  5. Commit and move on. Pacing matters. Don’t let one question steal time from five easier ones.

When you practice this routine before test day, you build speed and reduce anxiety. You’ll also learn how the exam likes to phrase questions, which makes it easier to recognize what you need to look up.

What exam is this book package for?

Is the ICC 974Y exam open book or closed book?

The ICC 974 Texas (San Antonio) Commercial Sign Operator exam is an open book test.

How many questions are on the ICC 974Y exam, and how long is the test?

The exam has 70 multiple-choice questions with a 3-hour time limit.

What reference does the exam use?

The exam reference listed for the 974Y outline is 2017 Chapter 28 Sign Code and Billboard Ordinance (San Antonio Chapter 28 City Code, “Sign and Billboard Ordinance”).

What topics should I focus on most?

Study according to the outline weights. The largest portions are General Requirements and Safety (30%) and Erection and Maintenance (30%), followed by Licensing and Permit Regulations (20%), Terms and Definitions (15%), and Plan Reading (5%).

Do I need to memorize the ordinance if the exam is open book?

You don’t need to memorize every line, but you do need to become comfortable navigating the ordinance quickly. The best prep is learning where key topics are located and practicing fast “find and confirm” lookups under time pressure.

How should I study in the final week before the exam?

Shift your focus toward timed navigation drills and practice-style review. Spend steady time in the highest-weight outline areas and rehearse locating the governing ordinance language quickly so you can maintain pacing on exam day.

Does passing the exam automatically mean I’m licensed?

Passing the exam is typically part of a broader local process handled by the jurisdiction. Additional registration steps and administrative requirements may apply beyond the exam itself.

How does the San Antonio Commercial Sign Operator (974Y) exam work?

The San Antonio Commercial Sign Operator (974Y) exam is set by a Texas city or by TDLR rather than by a statewide contractor board. The city or agency approves you, then the exam is scheduled with ICC or PSI.

  • ICC exam 974 has 70 questions in 3 hours, is open book and costs $115.
  • It is written to the City of San Antonio sign code, Chapter 28.
  • Texas does not license general contractors at the state level; the Texas Residential Construction Commission was abolished in 2009.
  • Cities set their own registration rules, and some, such as San Antonio, require an ICC exam for certain trades.
  • San Antonio sign operators need a passing ICC exam, a $45,000 bond and $1,000,000 in general liability insurance.
  • Texas code enforcement officers register with TDLR after the TEEX course and a closed-book PSI exam that costs $50.
