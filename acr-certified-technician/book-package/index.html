About this Certified Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Technician package

Prepare for the Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Certified Technician examination with a comprehensive collection of Texas licensing references, current mechanical and fuel-gas codes, HVACR technical material, safety information, and air-distribution resources. This book package supports candidates preparing for the Texas ACR Certified Technician Examination administered for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

Texas distinguishes the Certified Technician designation from an Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor license. A Certified Technician works under the supervision of a licensed Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor and may assist with air-conditioning and refrigeration maintenance and repair work within that supervised employment structure. The Certified Technician designation is a voluntary qualification above the Registered Technician level and requires applicants to satisfy TDLR eligibility requirements and pass the Certified Technician examination.

This package brings together references covering Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration law and rules, occupational safety, mechanical systems, fuel-gas installations, refrigeration principles, HVACR equipment, troubleshooting, controls, service, and air distribution. Exam Details

Applicants seeking Texas Certified Technician status take the TX ACR Certified Technician Examination. The examination is administered through PSI for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: TX ACR Certified Technician Examination
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Credential: Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Certified Technician
  • Minimum Applicant Age: 18 years old

Candidates must first apply to TDLR and demonstrate that they meet the qualification requirements for Certified Technician status. After TDLR verifies eligibility, the applicant receives examination eligibility information and instructions for registering and scheduling the examination through PSI.

The Certified Technician exam is separate from the contractor endorsement examinations. Texas contractor applicants may take Commercial Refrigeration Class A, Environmental Air Class A, Commercial Refrigeration Class B, or Environmental Air Class B examinations depending on the license class and endorsement selected. Certified Technician applicants instead take the dedicated TX ACR Certified Technician Examination.

Because the technician exam is designed for individuals working directly with air-conditioning and refrigeration systems, candidates should prepare broadly across HVACR fundamentals, equipment operation, controls, refrigeration, mechanical-system requirements, gas-related concepts, safety, and Texas regulatory requirements.

Licensing Steps

The Texas Certified Technician designation is administered by TDLR. Applicants must meet an approved qualification pathway before becoming eligible to take the examination.

  1. Meet the minimum age requirement. Applicants must be at least 18 years old.
  2. Qualify through practical experience or approved training. Texas provides an experience pathway and an approved certification-training pathway.
  3. Use the practical-experience pathway when applicable. Applicants may qualify by documenting at least 24 months of air-conditioning and refrigeration-related work under the supervision of a licensed Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor.
  4. Use approved training when applicable. Applicants may also qualify by completing a department-approved certification program consisting of 2,000 hours of combined instruction and practical experience.
  5. Submit the Certified Technician application. Applicants must provide the required application and qualifying documentation to TDLR.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. TDLR reviews the application and verifies that the applicant satisfies the eligibility requirements.
  7. Register with PSI. After approval, candidates receive instructions for scheduling the TX ACR Certified Technician Examination.
  8. Prepare with the applicable reference materials. Review Texas law, mechanical codes, fuel-gas requirements, HVACR technical principles, service procedures, controls, and safety.
  9. Take and pass the Certified Technician examination. Complete the PSI examination within the application eligibility period.
  10. Maintain the Certified Technician designation. Certified Technicians must continue to work within the Texas supervision and credential requirements that apply to technicians.

State Requirements

Texas defines Certified Technician status as a technician qualification rather than a contractor license. A person holding the designation may call themselves a Certified Technician, but the credential does not independently authorize the technician to operate as an Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor.

Certified Technicians performing regulated air-conditioning and refrigeration work must work under the supervision of a licensed Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor. Technicians may not independently advertise air-conditioning and refrigeration contracting services under the technician credential.

An applicant can qualify through practical experience by documenting at least 24 months of air-conditioning and refrigeration-related work performed under the supervision of a licensed Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor.

Military HVACR experience may be combined with or used toward the practical-experience requirement when properly documented according to TDLR requirements.

Texas also permits qualification through a TDLR-approved Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Certification Training Program. The approved program must consist of 2,000 hours of combined instruction and practical experience. Applicants using this pathway must meet the applicable timing and documentation requirements associated with completion of the program.

Certified Technician status can also become part of a future contractor-license pathway. A person who has held the Certified Technician designation for the preceding 12 months and has accumulated at least 36 months of qualifying practical air-conditioning and refrigeration experience during the preceding 48 months may qualify to apply for a Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor license, subject to all applicable contractor requirements.

This makes the Certified Technician credential useful both as a professional technician designation and as a potential step toward contractor-level licensing for individuals who continue to gain qualifying experience.

Test Information and Study Materials

Refrigeration Principles: Review the basic refrigeration cycle and the relationship between compression, condensation, expansion, and evaporation. Understand sensible heat, latent heat, saturation, pressure-temperature relationships, superheat, and subcooling.

Compressors: Study common compressor types, operation, electrical requirements, lubrication, capacity, failure conditions, and troubleshooting. The compressor is a central component of both air-conditioning and refrigeration systems.

Evaporators and Condensers: Understand heat absorption at the evaporator and heat rejection at the condenser. Review airflow, temperature differences, refrigerant conditions, coil cleanliness, and common performance problems.

Metering Devices: Review thermostatic expansion valves, fixed metering devices, and other methods used to regulate refrigerant entering an evaporator. Understand how metering-device operation affects superheat and overall system performance.

Electrical Systems and Controls: HVACR technicians frequently work with thermostats, relays, contactors, capacitors, motors, transformers, pressure switches, control boards, and safety controls. Review electrical troubleshooting and common control-circuit operation.

Air Distribution: Study airflow, supply and return systems, duct sizing, static pressure, velocity, friction loss, registers, grilles, filters, and common airflow problems. The Ductulator can support practical study in this area.

Mechanical Codes: Use the 2024 IMC and UMC to review mechanical-equipment installation, access, clearances, ventilation, duct systems, exhaust, refrigeration systems, condensate disposal, combustion air, and equipment safety.

Fuel Gas: Use the 2024 IFGC to review gas piping, pressure testing, shutoff valves, regulators, appliance connections, combustion air, vent connectors, venting systems, and fuel-burning appliance requirements.

Service and Troubleshooting: Practice diagnosing symptoms such as inadequate cooling, high or low system pressures, improper superheat or subcooling, airflow restrictions, compressor problems, electrical faults, and control failures.

Refrigerant Handling: Review safe procedures involving system charging, recovery, evacuation, leak detection, cylinders, hoses, gauges, and pressure hazards. Technicians should understand how refrigerant conditions affect both system performance and servicing procedures.

Heating Equipment: Study gas-fired heating equipment, electric heating equipment, heat pumps, controls, combustion considerations, and common service issues encountered in environmental air systems.

Commercial Refrigeration: Review coolers, freezers, condensing units, evaporators, defrost systems, temperature controls, refrigeration piping, and other commercial applications that may be encountered by an HVACR technician.

Safety: Use OSHA material and technical references to strengthen awareness of workplace hazards, electrical safety, pressurized systems, hot surfaces, moving equipment, refrigerant handling, and proper work practices.

Texas Law and Rules: Review the difference between Registered Technician, Certified Technician, and Contractor credentials. Understand supervision requirements and the limitations placed on technicians working under a contractor.

Reference Navigation: Practice identifying the type of question before opening a reference. A gas-appliance question may require the IFGC, a mechanical installation question may require the IMC or UMC, a technician-rule question may require Chapter 75, and a refrigeration-theory question may lead to Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.

Frequently Asked Questions

What examination does a Texas Certified Technician applicant take?

Applicants approved for the Certified Technician designation take the TX ACR Certified Technician Examination.

Who administers the examination?

The examination is administered through PSI for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

Is a Certified Technician the same as a licensed Texas ACR Contractor?

No. Certified Technician is a technician designation. A Certified Technician must work under the supervision of a licensed Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor and does not independently hold contractor authority through the technician credential.

How old must I be to apply?

Applicants for Texas Certified Technician status must be at least 18 years old.

How much practical experience is required?

The practical-experience pathway requires at least 24 months of air-conditioning and refrigeration-related work under the supervision of a licensed Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor.

Is there a training-program pathway?

Yes. Applicants may qualify by completing a TDLR-approved certification training program consisting of 2,000 hours of combined instruction and practical experience.

Can military HVACR experience count?

Military air-conditioning and refrigeration experience may be used as part of the experience pathway when properly documented under TDLR requirements.

Can a Certified Technician advertise HVACR contracting services independently?

No. Texas technicians must work under the supervision of a licensed Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor and may not independently advertise air-conditioning and refrigeration contracting services under the technician designation.

Can Certified Technician status help me qualify for a contractor license later?

Yes. A person who has held Certified Technician status for the preceding 12 months and has at least 36 months of qualifying practical experience during the preceding 48 months may become eligible to apply for a contractor license, subject to the remaining TDLR requirements.

What code editions apply beginning September 1, 2026?

Texas updated its adopted codes to the 2024 International Mechanical Code, 2024 International Fuel Gas Code, and 2024 Uniform Mechanical Code.

Why is Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning included?

The book supports preparation for refrigeration principles, components, electrical systems, controls, heating and cooling equipment, service, troubleshooting, and commercial refrigeration concepts.

Why is a Ductulator included?

A Ductulator supports study of airflow, duct size, velocity, friction rate, and other air-distribution relationships encountered in HVAC systems.

What should I focus on during preparation?

Focus on refrigeration principles, HVACR components, controls, electrical systems, air distribution, fuel-gas requirements, mechanical codes, service, troubleshooting, safety, and Texas technician laws and rules.

How does the Certified Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Technician exam work?

The Certified Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Technician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. You apply to TDLR with proof of experience, then schedule the open-book exam with PSI.

  • The PSI exam has 60 scored items in 112 minutes and costs $60.
  • The TDLR application fee is $50 and the applicant must be at least 18.
  • TDLR licenses air conditioning and refrigeration contractors in two classes: Class B up to 25 tons cooling and 1.5 million BTU per hour heating, and Class A with no limit.

  • PSI gives the exams, and they are open book; since September 1, 2026 they reference the 2024 IMC, UMC and IFGC.
  • Contractors complete 8 hours of continuing education each year, including 1 hour of Texas law and rules.
