### Open Book Test

The Texas Residential Appliance Installer examination is an **open book test**.

The approved technical reference is the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition**. Candidates should be prepared to use the NEC efficiently during the examination rather than relying only on memorization.

Soft-bound NEC editions may be used, while loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound versions are not permitted in the testing room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the exam reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before testing, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination session.

### State Requirements

No prior electrical experience hours are required to qualify for the Residential Appliance Installer examination. ### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Calculations, and Theory:** Review common NEC definitions and basic electrical theory involving voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, conductors, loads, and electrical relationships. Candidates should understand the terminology used throughout the NEC.

**GFCI Protection:** GFCI requirements are central to residential appliance work. Review where GFCI protection is required and how those requirements apply to kitchens, bathrooms, garages, outdoor areas, laundry areas, basements, pools, spas, and other residential locations.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Study Article 250 carefully. Understand equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, grounding electrode systems, effective ground-fault current paths, and bonding of metal equipment.

**Appliance Installations:** Article 422 is particularly important. Review branch-circuit requirements, disconnecting means, flexible cords, overcurrent protection, grounding, appliance ratings, and permanently connected appliances.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Fifteen scored items come from this category. Study receptacles, switches, disconnects, appliances, HVAC equipment, overcurrent devices, and equipment installation requirements.

**Wiring Methods and Materials:** Ten scored items cover cables, raceways, conductors, boxes, fittings, enclosures, protection from physical damage, securing and supporting, and other Chapter 3 subjects.

**Nonmetallic-Sheathed Cable:** Review the permitted uses of Type NM cable in residential settings, support requirements, physical protection, and installation through framing members.

**Boxes and Enclosures:** Review box fill, conductor allowances, equipment grounding conductor allowances, box accessibility, covers, and enclosure requirements.

**Conductor Sizing:** Practice using NEC ampacity tables and applying terminal temperature limitations, conductor insulation ratings, adjustment factors, and correction factors.

**Motors:** Five scored items cover motor-related requirements. Review conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, and disconnecting means.

**Electrical Control Devices:** Five scored items address switches, controllers, relays, disconnecting means, and similar control equipment that may be associated with residential appliances and pool equipment.

**Pools, Spas, and Fountains:** This is the largest exam category with 18 scored items. Study Article 680 carefully, including swimming pools, spas, hot tubs, fountains, pumps, underwater luminaires, grounding, bonding, GFCI protection, and receptacle requirements.

**Equipotential Bonding:** Pool installations involve specialized bonding requirements. Understand bonding of conductive pool shells, perimeter surfaces, metal components, underwater lighting, electrical equipment, and other conductive parts where applicable.

**Pool Pump Motors:** Review branch-circuit requirements, GFCI protection, grounding, bonding, disconnecting means, and motor-related provisions for pool pump equipment.

**Spas and Hot Tubs:** Study GFCI protection, disconnects, grounding, bonding, receptacle locations, and other Article 680 rules that apply to spas and hot tubs.

**Fountains:** Review the requirements for fountains, pumps, underwater equipment, bonding, GFCI protection, wiring methods, and electrical equipment located near water.

**Working Space and Equipment Access:** Article 110 contains important requirements involving working clearances, accessibility, equipment installation, and guarding.

**Overcurrent Protection:** Review how conductor ampacity, equipment ratings, appliance requirements, motors, and branch circuits relate to overcurrent protective devices.

**Disconnecting Means:** Study disconnect requirements for appliances, motors, pool equipment, and other electrical equipment. Understand location, accessibility, ratings, and acceptable disconnecting methods.

**Code Navigation:** Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC during preparation. Practice identifying the subject first, moving to the correct article or table, and confirming the applicable requirement before choosing an answer.

**Timed Practice:** Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a consistent pace while allowing additional time for questions involving multiple code sections or calculations.

**Study by Exam Weight:** Give the greatest attention to Pools, Spas, and Fountains, GFCI and Grounding & Bonding, and Electrical Equipment and Devices. These categories make up a large portion of the scored examination.

## How does the Residential Appliance Installer exam work?

The Residential Appliance Installer exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has 80 scored items in 240 minutes and costs $64.
