Exam Details

The Texas Residential Appliance Installer examination is administered through PSI after the applicant receives authorization to test from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: Texas Residential Appliance Installer
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 80
  • Time for Scored Items: 240 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

The 80 scored examination items are distributed across the following content areas:

  • Definitions, Calculations, and Theory: 12 items
  • GFCI and Grounding & Bonding: 15 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 10 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 15 items
  • Motors: 5 items
  • Electrical Control Devices: 5 items
  • Pools, Spas, and Fountains: 18 items

Pools, Spas, and Fountains is the largest individual examination category with 18 scored items. GFCI and Grounding & Bonding and Electrical Equipment and Devices each contain 15 scored items, making these three areas especially important during preparation.

Definitions, Calculations, and Theory contributes 12 scored items, while Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials accounts for 10. Motors and Electrical Control Devices each contribute five items.

The exam structure means candidates should develop both broad NEC familiarity and strong knowledge of the specialized subjects that make up the largest parts of the Residential Appliance Installer examination.

Open Book Test

The Texas Residential Appliance Installer examination is an open book test.

The technical reference for the current examination is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates should become comfortable using the code before examination day so they can locate applicable requirements efficiently.

Acceptable soft-bound NEC editions may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound versions are not permitted in the testing room. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before testing, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination session.

The included rental NEC is highlighted and tabbed to support study and reference navigation. The 1 year of course access provides an extended period for candidates to practice locating appliance, grounding, GFCI, motor, wiring-method, and pool-related requirements.

Open-book testing still requires preparation. Candidates should know whether a question belongs in Article 250, Article 422, Article 430, Article 680, a Chapter 3 wiring-method article, or another part of the NEC before beginning the search.

State Requirements

The Texas Residential Appliance Installer license covers a specialized category of residential electrical work. It authorizes qualifying work connected with residential appliance installations within the scope established for the credential.

Unlike several other Texas electrician licenses, the Residential Appliance Installer credential does not require prior electrical experience hours before an applicant may qualify for the examination.

A licensed Residential Appliance Installer performs qualifying work on behalf of a licensed Residential Appliance Installation Contractor.

The credential also includes qualifying residential pool-related electrical work within its authorized scope. This connection is reflected in the examination blueprint, where Pools, Spas, and Fountains is the largest testing category.

The Residential Appliance Installer license should not be treated as equivalent to a Texas Journeyman Electrician, Residential Wireman, or Maintenance Electrician credential. It has its own defined scope and limitations.

Experience gained while working as a Residential Appliance Installer does not count toward the experience requirements for other Texas electrician licenses.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to applicable renewal and continuing education requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions, Calculations, and Theory: Review NEC definitions and basic electrical concepts involving voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, loads, conductors, and common calculations. Understanding terminology is important because many exam questions depend on recognizing how the NEC defines a device, location, system, or condition.

GFCI Protection: GFCI requirements are one of the central areas of this examination. Review residential locations where GFCI protection is required, including bathrooms, kitchens, garages, outdoor areas, laundry areas, basements, sinks, pool areas, spas, and other covered locations.

Grounding and Bonding: Study Article 250 carefully. Review equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, effective ground-fault current paths, grounding electrode systems, and bonding of conductive equipment.

Appliance Installations: Article 422 is a major study area. Review appliance branch circuits, disconnecting means, flexible cords, grounding, overcurrent protection, ratings, and permanently connected appliances.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: This category contains 15 scored items. Review receptacles, switches, disconnects, appliances, HVAC equipment, overcurrent devices, equipment ratings, and general installation requirements.

Wiring Methods and Materials: Ten scored items cover cables, raceways, conductors, boxes, fittings, enclosures, physical protection, securing and supporting, and other Chapter 3 subjects.

Nonmetallic-Sheathed Cable: Residential work commonly involves Type NM cable. Review permitted uses, securing and supporting, physical protection, installation through framing members, and related conductor requirements.

Boxes and Enclosures: Review box fill, conductor allowances, equipment grounding conductor allowances, covers, accessibility, junction boxes, and enclosure requirements.

Conductor Sizing: Practice using applicable NEC ampacity tables and applying terminal temperature limitations, conductor insulation ratings, adjustment factors, and correction factors.

Motors: Five scored items cover motor requirements. Review conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, and disconnecting means.

Electrical Control Devices: Five scored items cover switches, relays, contactors, controllers, disconnecting means, and similar equipment that may be associated with residential appliances and pool systems.

Pools, Spas, and Fountains: This is the largest examination category with 18 scored items. Candidates should spend substantial time studying Article 680 and its requirements for pools, spas, hot tubs, fountains, pumps, receptacles, lighting, grounding, bonding, and GFCI protection.

Equipotential Bonding: Pool installations involve specialized bonding rules. Study the bonding requirements for conductive pool shells, perimeter surfaces, metal components, underwater lighting systems, electrical equipment, and other conductive parts where applicable.

Pool Pump Motors: Review branch-circuit requirements, GFCI protection, grounding, bonding, disconnecting means, motor conductor sizing, and related pool-pump requirements.

Spas and Hot Tubs: Study GFCI protection, grounding, bonding, disconnects, receptacle locations, wiring methods, and other requirements that apply to spas and hot tubs.

Fountains: Review rules for pumps, underwater equipment, GFCI protection, bonding, grounding, wiring methods, and electrical equipment installed in or around fountain systems.

Working Space and Equipment Access: Review Article 110 requirements involving working clearances, accessibility, equipment installation, guarding, environmental suitability, and workmanship.

Overcurrent Protection: Study the relationship between conductor ampacity, equipment ratings, appliances, motors, branch circuits, and protective devices.

Disconnecting Means: Review disconnect requirements for appliances, motors, pool equipment, and other electrical equipment. Understand location, accessibility, rating, and accepted methods of disconnection.

Code Navigation: Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC throughout preparation. Practice identifying the main subject first, moving directly to the likely article or table, and confirming the applicable rule before answering.

Cross-Reference Practice: Some questions require applying more than one NEC article. An appliance or pool question may also require grounding, conductor, wiring-method, motor, or disconnect requirements. Practice moving between related sections efficiently.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Practice maintaining a consistent pace while allowing additional time for questions that involve multiple code sections or calculations.

Study by Exam Weight: Give the greatest attention to Pools, Spas, and Fountains, GFCI and Grounding & Bonding, and Electrical Equipment and Devices. Together, these areas make up a large share of the scored examination.

Long-Term Preparation: With 1 year of course access, candidates can divide study into manageable phases. Begin with general NEC organization and terminology, move into appliance and grounding topics, add detailed Article 680 review, and use later sessions for timed practice and reinforcement of weaker areas.
