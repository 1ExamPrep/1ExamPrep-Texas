### State Requirements

The Texas Residential Appliance Installer license is a specialized individual credential covering qualifying residential appliance installation work within the scope established for the license.

Unlike several other Texas electrician licenses, applicants are not required to document a specific number of prior electrical work hours before qualifying for the Residential Appliance Installer examination.

A licensed Residential Appliance Installer performs qualifying work on behalf of a licensed **Residential Appliance Installation Contractor**. The individual installer license and the contractor business credential serve different purposes within the Texas licensing structure.

The authorized scope can include qualifying residential appliance electrical work and certain residential pool-related electrical work. The examination reflects this specialization by assigning 18 scored questions to Pools, Spas, and Fountains, making that the largest individual subject category.

The Residential Appliance Installer credential is not equivalent to a Texas Journeyman Electrician, Residential Wireman, Maintenance Electrician, or Master Electrician license. Its authority is limited to the work included within the Residential Appliance Installer scope.

Experience gained while working as a Residential Appliance Installer does not count toward the experience requirements for other Texas electrician licenses.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Calculations, and Theory:** Twelve scored items address electrical terminology, calculations, and basic theory. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, conductor terminology, equipment definitions, and common electrical calculations.

**GFCI Protection:** GFCI requirements are an important part of the examination. Review dwelling locations where GFCI protection is required and pay particular attention to appliances, kitchens, bathrooms, garages, outdoor areas, laundry areas, sinks, pools, spas, and other covered locations.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Study Article 250 carefully. Review equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, effective ground-fault current paths, grounding electrode systems, and equipment bonding requirements.

**Appliance Installations:** Article 422 is an important study area. Review appliance branch circuits, disconnecting means, flexible cords, grounding, overcurrent protection, ratings, and permanently connected equipment.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** This category contains 15 scored items. Study receptacles, switches, disconnects, appliances, HVAC equipment, electrical panels, overcurrent devices, equipment ratings, and general installation requirements.

**Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials:** Ten scored items cover cables, raceways, conductors, boxes, fittings, enclosures, securing and supporting, physical protection, and other Chapter 3 subjects.

**Nonmetallic-Sheathed Cable:** Review permitted uses, securing and supporting, installation through framing members, protection from physical damage, conductor requirements, and other rules for Type NM cable.

**Boxes and Enclosures:** Practice box-fill calculations and review conductor allowances, equipment grounding conductor allowances, cable clamps, devices, covers, accessibility, and enclosure requirements.

**Motors:** Five scored items address motors. Review conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, and disconnecting means.

**Electrical Control Devices:** Five scored items cover switches, relays, controllers, contactors, and related control equipment. Candidates should understand ratings and basic installation requirements.

**Pools, Spas, and Fountains:** With 18 scored items, this is the largest examination category. Study Article 680 carefully, including requirements for pools, spas, hot tubs, fountains, pumps, receptacles, lighting, grounding, bonding, and GFCI protection.

**Equipotential Bonding:** Review bonding requirements for pool shells, perimeter surfaces, conductive components, underwater lighting systems, electrical equipment, and other applicable conductive parts.

**Pool Pump Motors:** Review branch-circuit requirements, GFCI protection, grounding, bonding, disconnecting means, conductor sizing, and motor requirements related to pool equipment.

**Spas and Hot Tubs:** Study GFCI protection, grounding, bonding, disconnects, receptacle locations, wiring methods, and other requirements applying to spas and hot tubs.

**Fountains:** Review requirements for pumps, underwater equipment, GFCI protection, bonding, grounding, wiring methods, and electrical equipment installed in or around fountains.

**Working Space and Equipment Access:** Review Article 110 requirements for working clearances, accessibility, equipment installation, guarding, environmental suitability, labeling, and workmanship.

**Overcurrent Protection:** Study the relationship between conductor ampacity, branch circuits, motors, appliances, equipment ratings, and overcurrent protective devices.

**Disconnecting Means:** Review disconnect requirements for appliances, motors, pool equipment, and other electrical equipment, including accessibility, location, ratings, and accepted methods of disconnection.

## How does the Residential Appliance Installer exam work?

The Residential Appliance Installer exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has 80 scored items in 240 minutes and costs $64.
- The license needs no continuing education, and the TDLR application fee is $30.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
- Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
