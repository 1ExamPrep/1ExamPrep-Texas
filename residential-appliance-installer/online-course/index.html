Exam Details

The Texas Residential Appliance Installer examination is administered through PSI after the applicant has completed the applicable TDLR application process and received examination eligibility.

  • Exam: Texas Residential Appliance Installer
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 80
  • Time for Scored Items: 240 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

The current examination outline distributes the 80 scored items across the following subject areas:

  • Definitions, Calculations, and Theory: 12 items
  • GFCI Protection and Equipment Grounding and Bonding: 15 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 10 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 15 items
  • Motors: 5 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 5 items
  • Swimming Pools, Spas, and Fountain Equipment: 18 items

Swimming Pools, Spas, and Fountain Equipment is the largest examination category with 18 scored items. GFCI Protection and Equipment Grounding and Bonding and Electrical Equipment and Devices each contain 15 scored items. Together, these areas represent a substantial portion of the examination.

Definitions, Calculations, and Theory contribute 12 scored items, while Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials account for another 10. Candidates should therefore prepare across the complete examination outline rather than concentrating exclusively on appliance questions.

Open Book Test

The Texas Residential Appliance Installer examination is an open book test.

The approved technical reference is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing an acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

Soft-bound editions of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as an examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before the examination, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new markings, notes, highlighting, or indexing during the examination session.

Open-book testing still rewards preparation. Candidates should become familiar with the NEC before test day so they can identify the likely article or section quickly. Repeated reference practice is especially useful for Article 422 appliance requirements and Article 680 pool, spa, and fountain requirements.

State Requirements

Texas does not require previous electrical experience hours to apply for the Residential Appliance Installer license. Applicants complete the applicable licensing process and must pass the required examination.

A Texas Residential Appliance Installer performs electrical work that is limited to residential appliance installation, including qualifying residential pool-related electrical installation and maintenance, on behalf of a licensed Residential Appliance Installation Contractor.

The individual Residential Appliance Installer license is separate from the Residential Appliance Installation Contractor business license. The contractor license applies to the business performing residential appliance installation work.

A Residential Appliance Installation Contractor must employ a licensed Residential Appliance Installer who serves as the installer of record. The licensed installer may also own the contracting business.

Texas treats this credential as a specialized electrical license. Experience performed solely as a Residential Appliance Installer does not count toward experience requirements for other Texas electrical licenses.

Residential Appliance Installer licenses are subject to annual renewal requirements and the continuing education requirements applicable to the credential.

The 2026 National Electrical Code became the Texas electrical code effective September 1, 2026, and current Texas Residential Appliance Installer examinations reference this edition.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions, Calculations, and Theory: Twelve scored items cover electrical terminology, theory, and calculations. Review Article 100 definitions along with voltage, current, resistance, power, loads, and basic circuit relationships.

GFCI Protection: GFCI requirements are a major exam subject. Study the locations and equipment requiring ground-fault circuit-interrupter protection, especially where appliances and pool-related equipment are installed.

Equipment Grounding and Bonding: Review equipment grounding conductors, bonding of metal equipment, grounding connections, fault-current paths, and the purpose of effective bonding.

Article 250: Grounding and bonding requirements appear across several examination categories. Candidates should become comfortable locating equipment grounding conductor, bonding, and grounding provisions efficiently.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials: Ten scored items cover raceways, boxes, enclosures, cables, conductors, flexible cords, fittings, and related wiring-method requirements.

Flexible Cords: Residential appliances frequently use cords and attachment plugs. Study the permitted uses, limitations, grounding requirements, protection from physical damage, and connection rules for flexible cords.

Boxes and Enclosures: Review accessibility, covers, weather exposure, box sizing, enclosures, fittings, and the requirements for equipment installed in damp or wet locations.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Fifteen scored items cover receptacles, switches, appliances, equipment, and related installation requirements. This is one of the largest examination categories.

Article 422 Appliances: Study branch circuits, overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, cord connections, permanently connected appliances, equipment ratings, and other requirements that apply to appliances.

Receptacles: Review receptacle ratings, grounding, GFCI protection, polarity, location, and installation requirements that can affect residential appliance connections.

Motors: Five scored items cover electric motors. Residential appliances, pool pumps, and related equipment may use motors, making Article 430 an important study area.

Motor Conductors and Protection: Review conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, motor controllers, and disconnecting means.

Electrical Control Devices: Study switches, relays, contactors, timers, controls, and other devices used to operate appliances and pool-related equipment.

Disconnecting Means: Review disconnect ratings, locations, accessibility, visibility, and equipment-specific requirements.

Swimming Pools, Spas, and Fountain Equipment: With 18 scored items, this is the largest examination category. Article 680 deserves substantial attention during preparation.

Pool Equipment: Review pumps, motors, receptacles, branch circuits, grounding, bonding, GFCI protection, disconnecting means, and wiring methods associated with swimming pool equipment.

Equipotential Bonding: Study the bonding requirements designed to reduce voltage gradients around swimming pools and similar installations. Understand which conductive components are included in the bonding system.

Pool Pumps: Pool pumps combine Article 680 requirements with motor provisions. Review GFCI protection, grounding, bonding, conductor requirements, controls, and disconnecting means.

Pool Luminaires: Study the requirements for underwater and pool-area lighting, including GFCI protection, bonding, junction equipment, clearances, and wiring methods.

Spas and Hot Tubs: Review GFCI protection, bonding, disconnecting means, receptacles, wiring methods, and electrical equipment requirements for spas and hot tubs.

Fountains: Study the electrical requirements for pumps, luminaires, wiring methods, GFCI protection, grounding, and bonding associated with fountains.

Clearances: Pool installations involve important horizontal and vertical clearance rules. Candidates should know how to locate requirements affecting conductors, equipment, receptacles, and electrical systems near water.

Code Navigation: Use the 2026 NEC throughout online preparation. Practice recognizing whether a question is primarily about an appliance, motor, pool installation, grounding requirement, wiring method, or control device before searching the code.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items, which provides an average of approximately three minutes per scored question. Practice maintaining a steady pace while reserving additional time for more complex code searches and calculations.

Study by Exam Weight: Give substantial attention to Swimming Pools, Spas, and Fountain Equipment; GFCI Protection and Equipment Grounding and Bonding; and Electrical Equipment and Devices. These are the three largest examination categories.
