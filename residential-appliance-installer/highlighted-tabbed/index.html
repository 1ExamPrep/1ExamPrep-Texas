Exam Details

The Texas Residential Appliance Installer examination is administered through PSI after the applicant has submitted a completed application and received approval to test from TDLR.

  • Exam: Texas Residential Appliance Installer
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 80
  • Time for Scored Items: 240 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book

The current examination content outline distributes the 80 scored items across the following subject areas:

Unless an examination question specifically states otherwise, conductors are considered copper.

Open Book Test

The Texas Residential Appliance Installer examination is an open book test.

The current approved examination reference is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

Any soft-bound edition of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies of the NEC are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes prepared before the examination session. Permanent index tabs manufactured or provided by the NEC publisher are permitted.

Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or otherwise mark the NEC during the examination session. Homemade, DIY, or non-NEC tabs are not permitted. Additional loose, taped, adhered, or stapled paper is also prohibited.

The highlighted and tabbed NEC in this package is designed to support preparation before test day. Candidates should still spend time learning the physical layout of the code and practicing how to move efficiently between the sections most relevant to residential appliance work.

State Requirements

Texas does not require previous experience hours to apply for a Residential Appliance Installer license. Applicants submit the individual license application and, once the application is received and verified, become eligible to take the licensing examination.

A Residential Appliance Installer is licensed to perform electrical work limited to residential appliance installation, including residential pool-related electrical installation and maintenance, on behalf of a licensed Residential Appliance Installation Contractor.

The individual Residential Appliance Installer license is separate from the Residential Appliance Installation Contractor business license. A contractor business must employ a licensed Residential Appliance Installer who serves as the installer of record. The installer may also own the contracting business.

A Residential Appliance Installer license may generally be assigned to only one Residential Appliance Installation Contractor unless the installer owns more than 50% of the residential appliance installation contracting business.

Texas also distinguishes Residential Appliance Installer experience from experience used to qualify for broader electrician credentials. Hours worked solely as a Residential Appliance Installer do not count as experience credit toward another Texas electrical license.

Residential Appliance Installer licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions, Calculations, and Theory: Twelve scored items cover NEC definitions and general electrical calculations. Review Article 100 terminology and foundational relationships involving voltage, current, resistance, power, conductors, and circuit loads.

GFCI Protection: GFCI requirements are a major part of the examination. Candidates should understand where GFCI protection is required for residential appliances, receptacles, outdoor locations, pool equipment, spas, fountains, and other installations covered by the NEC.

Equipment Grounding and Bonding: Study equipment grounding conductors, bonding of metallic equipment, fault-current paths, grounding terminals, and the relationship between grounding and bonding.

Equipotential Bonding: Pool-related electrical work makes equipotential bonding especially important. Review Article 680 requirements involving conductive parts around pools and the purpose of reducing voltage gradients in pool environments.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials: Ten scored items cover raceways, boxes, enclosures, cables, flexible cords, and conductors. Candidates should understand where different wiring methods are permitted and how they are protected and supported.

Flexible Cords: Residential appliances frequently use flexible cords and attachment plugs. Review the permitted uses, limitations, grounding requirements, protection from physical damage, and connection methods that apply.

Boxes and Enclosures: Study box sizing, accessibility, covers, weather exposure, wet-location requirements, and proper enclosure use for appliance and pool-related installations.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Fifteen scored items address receptacles, switches, appliances, workspace clearance, and utilization equipment. This is one of the largest categories on the examination.

Appliances: Review Article 422 and related requirements for appliance branch circuits, overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, flexible cords, permanently connected appliances, and equipment ratings.

Receptacles and Switches: Study receptacle ratings, grounding, GFCI protection, polarity, required locations, and switch requirements that may affect residential appliance installations.

Workspace Clearance: Review working-space and access requirements around applicable electrical equipment. Proper clearance is important during installation, maintenance, and servicing.

Motors: Five scored items address electric motors. Residential appliances and pool equipment may contain motors, making Article 430 an important study area. Review conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit protection, controllers, and disconnects.

Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: Five questions involve controllers, switches, and disconnects. Study disconnect location, ratings, accessibility, control methods, and equipment-specific rules.

Swimming Pools, Spas, and Fountain Equipment: This is the largest category with 18 scored items. Article 680 deserves substantial study time.

Pool Equipment: Review electrical requirements for pool pumps, equipment, motors, grounding, bonding, GFCI protection, disconnecting means, receptacles, and wiring methods.

Pool Heaters: Study branch-circuit requirements, disconnects, equipment grounding, bonding, GFCI protection where applicable, and proper electrical installation of pool heating equipment.

Pool Covers: Review electrically operated pool-cover equipment, including motors, controls, branch circuits, grounding, bonding, and disconnecting requirements.

Pool Luminaires: Study the special requirements for underwater and pool-area lighting, including GFCI protection, bonding, junction equipment, clearances, and wiring methods.

Pumps: Pool and spa pumps involve motor circuits, grounding, bonding, disconnects, and GFCI requirements. Candidates should understand how Article 680 interacts with Article 430 and other general NEC requirements.

Vertical and Horizontal Clearances: Review clearance rules affecting electrical conductors and equipment located around pools and similar installations.

Hot Tubs and Spas: Study bonding, GFCI protection, disconnecting means, receptacles, electrical equipment, and wiring methods applicable to hot tubs and spas.

Fountain Equipment: Review grounding, bonding, GFCI protection, wiring methods, pumps, luminaires, and electrical equipment associated with fountains.

Highlighted and Tabbed Navigation Practice: Use the prepared NEC during timed study sessions. Practice identifying the topic, locating the relevant article or table, confirming the rule, and returning to the question efficiently.

Time Management: Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Develop a steady pace while leaving additional time for calculations and questions that require multiple NEC references.
