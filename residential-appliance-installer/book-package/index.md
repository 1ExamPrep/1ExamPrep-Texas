### Open Book Test

The Texas Residential Appliance Installer examination is an **open book test**.

Candidates are permitted to reference the **National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition** during the examination. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own NEC book to the testing center.

Any acceptable soft-bound edition of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound NEC copies are not permitted in the examination room.

Preparing for an open-book test should include repeated code-navigation practice. Candidates should be able to move efficiently between electrical definitions, GFCI requirements, grounding and bonding rules, wiring methods, appliance provisions, motor requirements, disconnecting means, and swimming-pool equipment requirements.

Article 680 deserves particular attention because swimming pool, spa, and fountain equipment is the largest examination category. Candidates should also understand that pool-related questions frequently require the use of Article 680 together with general NEC requirements found elsewhere in the code.

Efficient preparation means learning both the rule and where to find it. A question about a pool pump motor may involve Article 680, Article 430, grounding and bonding provisions, GFCI requirements, and disconnecting means. Being able to recognize those relationships can save valuable examination time.

### State Requirements

Texas does **not require previous experience hours** to apply for a Residential Appliance Installer license. This distinguishes the credential from several other Texas electrical licenses that require thousands of hours of documented supervised electrical experience before examination eligibility.

A Residential Appliance Installer is licensed to perform electrical work limited to residential appliance installation, including qualifying residential pool-related electrical installation and maintenance work, on behalf of a licensed Residential Appliance Installation Contractor.

The individual installer credential and the contractor business license are separate. A Residential Appliance Installation Contractor must employ a licensed Residential Appliance Installer who serves as the installer of record. The installer may also own the contracting business.

A Residential Appliance Installer license generally may be assigned to only one Residential Appliance Installation Contractor unless the installer owns more than 50% of the contracting business under the applicable Texas requirements.

The Residential Appliance Installer license is valid for one year and must be renewed annually.

Experience accumulated solely while working as a Residential Appliance Installer does not count as qualifying experience toward another Texas electrician license.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Calculations, and Theory:** Twelve scored items address code definitions and general electrical calculations. Review Article 100 terminology along with basic voltage, current, resistance, power, conductor, and circuit relationships relevant to appliance installations.

**GFCI Protection:** GFCI requirements are a major examination topic. Candidates should understand where GFCI protection is required for residential appliance connections, receptacles, pool-related equipment, outdoor installations, and other locations addressed by the NEC.

**Equipment Grounding and Bonding:** Review equipment grounding conductors, bonding of metal equipment, fault-current paths, grounding terminals, bonding jumpers, and the distinction between grounding and bonding.

**Equipotential Bonding:** Pool-related electrical work makes equipotential bonding particularly important. Candidates should understand the purpose of bonding conductive parts around pools and how Article 680 addresses equipotential bonding requirements.

**Electrical Wiring Methods:** Ten scored items address wiring methods and electrical materials. Study raceways, cables, flexible cords, conductors, boxes, enclosures, fittings, supports, physical protection, and wiring suitable for wet or damp locations.

**Raceways:** Review commonly encountered raceway systems, installation requirements, fittings, conductor fill, support, protection, and location restrictions.

**Boxes and Enclosures:** Understand how boxes and enclosures protect electrical connections and equipment. Review box sizing, accessibility, covers, wet-location requirements, and appropriate installation practices.

**Cables, Flexible Cords, and Conductors:** Residential appliances are often connected through branch-circuit wiring, flexible cords, or equipment wiring. Review conductor types, ampacity, insulation, cord usage, strain relief, and protection against physical damage.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Fifteen scored items focus on receptacles, switches, appliances, workspace clearances, and utilization equipment. This is one of the largest exam categories and should receive substantial study time.

**Appliances:** Review Article 422 and related requirements for appliance branch circuits, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, flexible cords, permanently connected appliances, and equipment ratings.

**Receptacles, Switches, and Devices:** Understand device ratings, grounding, polarity, GFCI protection, location requirements, and proper use with residential appliance installations.

**Workspace Clearance:** Review required working spaces and access around applicable electrical equipment. Safe access is an important part of both installation and maintenance.

**Motors:** Five exam questions involve electric motors. Residential appliances and pool equipment may incorporate motors, making Article 430 knowledge important. Review motor conductors, overload protection, branch-circuit protection, controllers, and disconnects.

**Control Devices and Disconnecting Means:** Five scored items involve controllers, switches, and disconnects. Study disconnect locations, ratings, accessibility, control methods, and equipment-specific requirements.

**Swimming Pools, Spas, and Fountain Equipment:** With 18 scored items, this is the single largest exam category. Article 680 should be a major focus during preparation.

**Pool Equipment:** Review electrical requirements for pool pumps, motors, lighting equipment, receptacles, bonding, grounding, GFCI protection, disconnects, and wiring methods.

**Pool and Spa Heaters:** Understand the electrical requirements associated with heaters, including branch circuits, disconnects, grounding, bonding, GFCI protection where applicable, and equipment installation.

**Pool Covers:** Study electrically operated pool-cover equipment and the related branch-circuit, motor, control, grounding, bonding, and safety provisions.

**Spas and Hot Tubs:** Review GFCI protection, bonding, disconnects, receptacle locations, equipment wiring, and other Article 680 requirements that apply to spas and hot tubs.

**Fountains:** Understand the special electrical requirements for fountains, including GFCI protection, wiring methods, grounding, bonding, and equipment installed in or near water.

**Wet Locations:** Residential pool and appliance installations may involve wet or damp locations. Review conductor insulation, enclosures, fittings, receptacle covers, equipment ratings, and wiring methods suitable for the environment.

**NEC Navigation:** Practice locating Articles 100, 110, 250, 300, 310, 314, 400, 406, 422, 430, and 680 along with other related provisions. Recognizing the subject of a question before searching the code can improve speed.

**Timed Practice:** Candidates receive 240 minutes for 80 scored items. Build a steady test-taking pace while allowing extra time for calculations and questions requiring multiple code references.
