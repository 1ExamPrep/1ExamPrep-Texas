About this Journeyman Lineman package

Prepare for the Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician examination with the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition, the current electrical code reference used for Texas electrician examinations. Texas adopted the 2026 National Electrical Code effective September 1, 2026, and current Texas electrician testing permits candidates to use the 2026 NEC as the examination reference.

The Texas Journeyman Lineman license is a specialized electrical credential administered by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. It is designed for individuals whose electrical work involves equipment associated with the transmission and distribution of electricity from its original source to a substation for further distribution.

The Journeyman Lineman credential is a limited Texas electrical license and is not equivalent to a Journeyman Electrician license. The scope, experience requirements, and examination are specifically oriented toward lineman work and the electrical systems encountered in utility transmission and distribution environments.

The licensing examination is administered through PSI for TDLR. Candidates are tested on electrical definitions and theory, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods and materials, electrical equipment and devices, motors and generators, and electrical control devices and disconnecting means.

The 2026 National Electrical Code is the primary technical reference included in this package. Candidates should use the NEC not only to study individual code provisions but also to develop strong reference-navigation skills. Knowing where major topics are located can help candidates work efficiently during the open-book examination.

Lineman candidates should be comfortable with electrical fundamentals as well as practical code application. Questions may require knowledge of conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, service equipment, transformers, disconnecting means, electrical equipment, wiring methods, feeders, motors, generators, and related code provisions.

The Texas Journeyman Lineman exam contains fewer questions than several other Texas electrician exams, but the testing time still requires efficient decision-making. Candidates should be able to identify the subject of a question quickly, determine the appropriate NEC article or table, and apply the code requirement without spending excessive time searching.

Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician examination is administered by PSI after the applicant has been approved to test by TDLR.

  • Exam: Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 40
  • Time for Scored Items: 120 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 3
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 12 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

The current examination content outline distributes the 40 scored items across the following subject areas:

  • Definitions and Theory: 6 items
  • Electrical Services and Service Equipment: 8 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 4 items
  • Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: 2 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 2 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 10 items
  • Motors and Generators: 2 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 6 items

Electrical Equipment and Devices is the largest individual examination category with 10 scored items. Electrical Services and Service Equipment follows with eight items. Definitions and Theory and Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means each account for six items.

Unless an examination question specifically states otherwise, conductors are considered copper.

Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician examination is an open book test.

The current examination reference permitted by TDLR is the National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

A soft-bound edition of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound NEC copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes prepared before the examination session. Permanent index tabs manufactured or provided by the NEC book publisher are permitted.

Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or otherwise mark the NEC during the examination. Homemade, do-it-yourself, and non-NEC tabs are not allowed. Additional loose, taped, adhered, or stapled paper is also prohibited.

Open-book preparation should involve repeated practice with the physical NEC book that will be used on test day. Candidates should learn to find definitions, services, equipment, feeders, conductors, motors, generators, controllers, and disconnecting means quickly.

Because the examination allows 120 minutes for 40 scored items, candidates have an average of approximately three minutes per scored question. Efficient code navigation can make a significant difference when a question requires multiple code references or a calculation.

Licensing Steps

  1. Choose an approved experience pathway. Texas provides two primary qualification pathways for Journeyman Lineman applicants.
  2. Complete an approved apprenticeship pathway when applicable. One pathway requires 7,000 hours of training in an apprenticeship program approved by the United States Department of Labor.
  3. Use the experience pathway when applicable. Applicants may alternatively qualify with 3.5 years of experience as a Journeyman Lineman for an electric utility, electric cooperative, municipally owned utility, or electrical contractor in Texas.
  4. Document qualifying experience. Experience must be verified and signed by the appropriate supervising electrician.
  5. Submit the Journeyman Lineman license application. The application and required supporting documentation are submitted to TDLR for review.
  6. Receive examination authorization. After TDLR reviews and verifies the application, the applicant is notified of eligibility to take the licensing examination.
  7. Schedule the exam with PSI. Approved candidates follow the PSI scheduling instructions for the Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician examination.
  8. Prepare with the 2026 NEC. Study the official examination categories and practice efficient navigation of the current code.
  9. Take the licensing examination. Complete the scored and non-scored items within the allotted testing time.
  10. Achieve the required passing score. Candidates must score at least 70%.
  11. Complete the licensing process. After satisfying the application, experience, and examination requirements, complete any remaining TDLR requirements for license issuance.

State Requirements

A Texas Journeyman Lineman is licensed to engage in electrical work involving the maintenance and operation of equipment associated with the transmission and distribution of electricity from its original source to a substation for further distribution.

The Journeyman Lineman license is specifically identified by Texas as a limited license. It is not the equivalent of a Texas Journeyman Electrician license and does not provide the same general scope of electrical authority.

Applicants can qualify through a United States Department of Labor-approved apprenticeship program by completing at least 7,000 hours of training.

Texas also permits an alternative experience pathway. An applicant may qualify with 3.5 years of experience as a Journeyman Lineman while working for an electric utility, electric cooperative, municipally owned utility, or electrical contractor in Texas.

Qualifying experience must be documented and signed by the supervising electrician on the applicable experience-verification portion of the license application. TDLR may verify the reported work as part of its licensing review.

Journeyman Lineman licenses are valid for one year and must be renewed annually.

Licensed Journeyman Linemen must complete four hours of continuing education during each license cycle before renewal. The required continuing education must be completed through a TDLR-registered provider and address the applicable National Electrical Code, Texas electrician law and administrative rules, and electrical safety.

Texas adopted the 2026 National Electrical Code as the state electrical code effective September 1, 2026. Current Texas electrician examinations permit the 2026 NEC as the examination reference.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions and Theory: Six scored items address electrical definitions and theory. Review Article 100 terminology along with voltage, current, resistance, power, conductor properties, alternating current, direct current, electrical relationships, and foundational theory used in transmission and distribution work.

Electrical Services and Service Equipment: Eight questions focus on service installations and service equipment. Review service conductors, service equipment ratings, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, clearances, and service configurations.

Grounding and Bonding: Grounding and bonding requirements interact with many examination categories. Study Article 250, grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, grounded conductors, service bonding, and effective ground-fault current paths.

Electrical Feeders: Four scored items address feeders. Review feeder conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, neutral requirements, and feeder installation rules.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: Although this is a smaller exam category, candidates should still understand branch-circuit conductor sizing, load calculations, overcurrent protection, ampacity, voltage considerations, and conductor requirements.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials: Review raceways, conductors, cables, fittings, boxes, enclosures, supports, underground installations, wet-location requirements, and protection from physical damage.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: With 10 scored items, this is the largest examination category. Study electrical equipment ratings, utilization equipment, transformers, switches, overcurrent devices, equipment installation, protection, grounding, accessibility, and other device requirements.

Transformers: Linemen frequently work around systems involving transformers. Review transformer installation, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, and separately derived system concepts where applicable.

Overcurrent Protection: Study fuses, circuit breakers, conductor protection, equipment protection, interrupting ratings, and the relationship between overcurrent devices and conductor ampacity.

Motors and Generators: Two scored items cover motors and generators. Review conductor requirements, overcurrent protection, overload protection, disconnecting means, generators, and basic NEC provisions applying to these systems.

Electrical Control Devices: Controls are part of a six-item category combined with disconnecting means. Review switches, controllers, relays, control equipment, ratings, accessibility, and equipment operation.

Disconnecting Means: Study disconnect location, grouping, accessibility, ratings, identification, service disconnects, equipment disconnects, and safe isolation of electrical equipment.

Conductor Ampacity: Become comfortable with applicable ampacity tables, conductor temperature ratings, correction factors, adjustment factors, terminal limitations, and conductor identification.

Services and Distribution Equipment: Since lineman work is closely tied to electricity transmission and distribution, candidates should understand how NEC service requirements interact with conductors, equipment, disconnects, grounding, and customer-side electrical systems.

Electrical Safety Awareness: Although safety is not listed as a standalone content category in the current examination outline, safe electrical work is central to lineman operations. Code preparation should reinforce equipment isolation, grounding, clearances, overcurrent protection, proper conductor selection, and appropriate disconnecting methods.

Code Navigation: Practice identifying the subject category first and then going directly to the relevant NEC article or table. This is particularly valuable for services, equipment, conductors, grounding, overcurrent protection, motors, transformers, and disconnects.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 120 minutes for 40 scored items. Build a steady pace and practice moving past difficult questions when necessary so that sufficient time remains for the entire examination.

Calculation Practice: Review the electrical math required to work with current, voltage, power, conductor sizing, loads, and other basic NEC calculations. Even though the exam is not dominated by calculations, understanding the math supports several content categories.
