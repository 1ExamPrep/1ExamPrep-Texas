Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician examination is administered through PSI after an applicant receives authorization to test from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 40
  • Time for Scored Items: 120 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 3
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 12 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

The 40 scored examination items are distributed across the following content areas:

  • Definitions and Theory: 6 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 8 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 4 items
  • Branch Circuits: 2 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 2 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 10 items
  • Motors and Generators: 2 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 6 items

Electrical Equipment and Devices is the largest examination category with 10 scored items. Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems follows with eight items. Definitions and Theory and Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means each contribute six scored items.

With 120 minutes for 40 scored items, candidates have an average of approximately three minutes per scored question. Efficient code navigation can be especially important when questions require candidates to identify equipment rules, service requirements, grounding provisions, or disconnecting means.

Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician examination is an open book test.

The technical reference for the current examination is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates should become comfortable using the NEC efficiently before examination day.

Acceptable soft-bound NEC editions may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the testing room. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before testing, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination session.

The included rental NEC is highlighted and tabbed to support study and reference navigation. The 1 year of course access gives candidates an extended period to practice locating code requirements and become more familiar with the organization of the book.

Open-book testing still requires preparation. Candidates should be able to recognize whether a question relates to services, equipment, feeders, wiring methods, motors, generators, controls, grounding, or disconnecting means before beginning the code search.

State Requirements

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician credential is a specialized electrical license with a defined scope. It applies to qualifying electrical work associated with transmission and distribution systems from the original source of electricity to a substation for further distribution.

One qualifying pathway requires at least 7,000 hours of training in a Journeyman Lineman apprenticeship program approved by the United States Department of Labor.

Texas also recognizes an alternative pathway requiring at least 3.5 years of experience as a Journeyman Lineman for an electric utility, electric cooperative, municipally owned utility, or electrical contractor in Texas.

The Journeyman Lineman Electrician credential is not the same as the general Texas Journeyman Electrician license. Its work authority is limited to the transmission and distribution scope defined for the credential.

This distinction is important during both licensing and exam preparation. Candidates should study the general electrical code subjects on the examination while keeping their preparation connected to the systems and equipment associated with lineman work.

Experience gained under the Journeyman Lineman Electrician license does not count toward the experience requirement for the Texas Master Electrician credential.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to annual renewal requirements and applicable continuing education requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions and Theory: Six scored items cover electrical terminology and theory. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, electrical circuits, conductor terminology, equipment definitions, and the concepts needed to interpret NEC questions correctly.

Electrical Services: Eight scored items cover electrical services, service equipment, and separately derived systems. Review service conductors, service disconnects, equipment ratings, grounding, bonding, and overcurrent protection.

Separately Derived Systems: Review transformers, system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and the grounding and bonding rules associated with separately derived systems.

Electrical Feeders: Four scored items cover feeders. Study feeder conductors, overcurrent protection, equipment grounding conductors, grounding and bonding, and other feeder requirements.

Branch Circuits: Review branch-circuit conductors, circuit ratings, grounding, overcurrent protection, and equipment supply requirements.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials: Study raceways, cable systems, conductors, fittings, enclosures, boxes, physical protection, securing and supporting, and other Chapter 3 requirements.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: This is the largest examination category with 10 scored items. Give substantial attention to equipment installation, equipment ratings, disconnects, overcurrent protection, grounding, clearances, and general code requirements.

Equipment Ratings: Review voltage ratings, current ratings, interrupting ratings, equipment suitability, and the requirement to install electrical equipment according to applicable ratings and instructions.

Working Space and Equipment Access: Study Article 110 requirements involving working clearances, guarding, accessibility, dedicated spaces, environmental suitability, and equipment installation.

Grounding and Bonding: Article 250 supports many exam categories. Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, and separately derived systems.

Motors: Review motor conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, motor controllers, and disconnecting means.

Generators: Study generator conductors, disconnects, transfer equipment, grounding, bonding, and other NEC requirements that may apply to generator installations.

Electrical Control Devices: Review switches, controllers, relays, and related electrical control equipment. Understand equipment ratings and general installation requirements.

Disconnecting Means: Study disconnect location, accessibility, ratings, grouping, identification, and the relationship between disconnecting means and the electrical equipment they serve.

Overcurrent Protection: Review the relationship between conductor ampacity, equipment ratings, feeders, motors, services, and protective devices.

Code Navigation: Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC throughout preparation. Practice identifying the main subject of a question first, then moving directly to the likely article, section, or table.

Article 110: General Requirements for Electrical Installations is an important study area because it addresses equipment installation, working space, interrupting ratings, environmental suitability, guarding, labeling, and workmanship.

Article 230: Review service conductors, service equipment, service disconnects, overcurrent protection, and other service-related provisions because services represent a significant part of the examination.

Article 250: Grounding and bonding rules can appear across services, equipment, feeders, generators, and separately derived systems. Candidates should become comfortable navigating this article.

Article 430: Review motor conductors, overloads, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, and disconnecting means.

Cross-Reference Practice: Some exam questions require applying more than one NEC section. Practice moving between equipment-specific requirements and general provisions involving grounding, wiring methods, conductor sizing, or protection.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 120 minutes for 40 scored items. Practice maintaining an average pace of approximately three minutes per scored question while allowing extra time for more involved code searches.

Study by Exam Weight: Give the greatest attention to Electrical Equipment and Devices, Services and Separately Derived Systems, Definitions and Theory, and Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means. Together, these areas account for most of the scored examination.

Long-Term Preparation: With 1 year of course access, candidates can divide preparation into phases. Begin with NEC organization and electrical fundamentals, move into detailed subject review, and use later study sessions for timed practice and reinforcement of weaker areas.
