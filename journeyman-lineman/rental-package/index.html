Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician examination is administered through PSI after the applicant has received authorization to test from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 40
  • Time for Scored Items: 120 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 3
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 12 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

The examination content is distributed across the following scored subject areas:

  • Definitions and Theory: 6 items
  • Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems: 8 items
  • Electrical Feeders: 4 items
  • Branch Circuits: 2 items
  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 2 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 10 items
  • Motors and Generators: 2 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 6 items

Electrical Equipment and Devices is the largest individual category with 10 scored items. Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems follows with eight items. Definitions and Theory and Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means each contribute six scored items.

Although the examination contains fewer questions than several other Texas electrician exams, candidates should not treat it as a simple test. With 120 minutes for 40 scored items, candidates should be prepared to interpret technical questions efficiently and locate applicable NEC requirements without unnecessary searching.

Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician examination is an open book test.

The approved technical reference is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates should become comfortable navigating the code before examination day rather than depending on the book as a substitute for preparation.

Soft-bound NEC editions may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the testing room. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before the exam, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. New notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings may not be added during the testing session.

The NEC rental book included with this package is highlighted and tabbed to support study and reference navigation. Using the same prepared reference throughout the 6 months of course access can help candidates become more familiar with the organization of the code and the locations of commonly reviewed requirements.

Open-book testing still requires strong subject recognition. A candidate should be able to identify whether a question relates to services, equipment, feeders, wiring methods, motors, controls, disconnects, or theory before beginning the code search.

Licensing Steps

  1. Choose an applicable qualification pathway. Texas provides specific experience pathways for the Journeyman Lineman Electrician credential.
  2. Complete qualifying lineman training or experience. Applicants may qualify through at least 7,000 hours in an apprenticeship program approved by the United States Department of Labor or through the alternative qualifying work-experience pathway.
  3. Meet the alternative experience requirement when applicable. The alternative pathway requires at least 3.5 years of experience as a Journeyman Lineman for an electric utility, electric cooperative, municipally owned utility, or electrical contractor in Texas.
  4. Document qualifying experience. Applicants must provide the required information supporting their licensing pathway.
  5. Submit the Journeyman Lineman Electrician application. The application and required documentation are submitted to TDLR.
  6. Receive examination authorization. TDLR reviews the application before eligible applicants are approved to test.
  7. Schedule the examination through PSI. Approved candidates follow the PSI scheduling process.
  8. Study with the 2026 NEC. Review services, feeders, equipment, motors, generators, controls, disconnects, wiring methods, and applicable theory.
  9. Use the highlighted and tabbed rental NEC. Practice finding important articles and rules efficiently throughout preparation.
  10. Use the included online course. The package provides 6 months of course access for structured preparation and exam-focused review.
  11. Take the Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician examination. Complete the scored and non-scored questions within the allotted testing period.
  12. Earn the required passing score. A minimum score of 70% is required.
  13. Complete all remaining licensing requirements. Follow the applicable TDLR process for issuance of the Journeyman Lineman Electrician license.

State Requirements

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician credential is a specialized electrical license with a defined scope. It applies to electrical work associated with transmission and distribution systems from the original source of electricity to a substation for further distribution.

One qualification pathway requires at least 7,000 hours of training in a Journeyman Lineman apprenticeship program approved by the United States Department of Labor.

Texas also provides an alternative experience pathway. Applicants may qualify with at least 3.5 years of experience as a Journeyman Lineman for an electric utility, electric cooperative, municipally owned utility, or electrical contractor in Texas.

The Journeyman Lineman Electrician credential is not a general-purpose Journeyman Electrician license. Its authorized work is limited to the transmission and distribution scope established for the credential.

This distinction is important when preparing for the examination. Candidates should understand general electrical code concepts while keeping their study connected to the equipment and systems relevant to utility transmission and distribution work.

Experience gained under the Journeyman Lineman Electrician license does not qualify as experience toward the Texas Master Electrician licensing requirement.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to annual renewal requirements and applicable continuing education requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions and Theory: Six scored items cover electrical terminology and theory. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, grounding terminology, conductor terminology, equipment definitions, and other concepts needed to interpret NEC questions correctly.

Electrical Services: Eight scored items cover electrical services, service equipment, and separately derived systems. Review service conductors, equipment ratings, service disconnects, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and associated NEC provisions.

Separately Derived Systems: Review transformers, system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and the grounding and bonding requirements associated with separately derived systems.

Electrical Feeders: Four scored items cover feeders. Study feeder conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, equipment grounding conductors, and general feeder requirements.

Branch Circuits: Review branch-circuit conductors, overcurrent protection, equipment supply, grounding, and applicable general branch-circuit requirements.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials: Study conductors, raceways, cable systems, boxes, fittings, enclosures, physical protection, and the Chapter 3 requirements that may apply to electrical installations within the license scope.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: This is the largest examination category with 10 scored items. Candidates should spend substantial study time on equipment installation requirements, ratings, clearances, grounding, overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, and other rules associated with electrical devices and equipment.

Equipment Ratings: Understand voltage, current, interrupting ratings, equipment suitability, and the importance of installing electrical equipment within its listed and labeled limitations.

Working Space and Equipment Access: Review Article 110 requirements involving working clearances, guarding, accessibility, dedicated equipment space, installation workmanship, and equipment identification.

Grounding and Bonding: Article 250 supports many examination subjects. Review equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, grounding electrodes, service bonding, and separately derived systems.

Motors: Study motor conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, and disconnecting means.

Generators: Review generator installations, conductors, transfer equipment, grounding and bonding, disconnects, and other NEC requirements relevant to generator systems.

Electrical Controls: Six scored items are shared between control devices and disconnecting means. Review switches, controllers, relays, control equipment, ratings, locations, and applicable installation requirements.

Disconnecting Means: Study disconnect ratings, locations, accessibility, grouping requirements, identification, and the relationship between disconnects and the equipment they serve.

Overcurrent Protection: Review the relationship between conductor ampacity, equipment ratings, and protective devices. Understand basic overcurrent-protection principles and how they connect to services, feeders, motors, and equipment.

Code Navigation: Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC during regular study sessions. Practice recognizing the main subject of a question before opening the book, then move directly to the relevant article or table.

Cross-Reference Practice: NEC questions sometimes require information from more than one article. Practice following cross-references so that related requirements can be located without losing unnecessary time.

Article 110: General Requirements for Electrical Installations is an important study area because it contains rules affecting equipment installation, working space, interrupting ratings, labeling, environmental suitability, and workmanship.

Article 230: Review service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, and service installation requirements because services represent a significant portion of the examination.

Article 250: Grounding and bonding concepts can appear across services, equipment, feeders, generators, and separately derived systems. Candidates should be comfortable navigating this article.

Article 430: Review motor requirements, including conductors, overload protection, controllers, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, and disconnecting means.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 120 minutes for 40 scored items. This provides an average of approximately three minutes per scored question. Practice maintaining a steady pace while preserving extra time for questions requiring detailed code searches.

Study by Exam Weight: Give strong attention to Electrical Equipment and Devices, Services and Separately Derived Systems, Definitions and Theory, and Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means. Together, these subjects make up most of the scored examination.
