Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician examination is an open book test.

The approved technical reference is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing an acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

Soft-bound editions of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the exam-room reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before the examination, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new highlighting, notes, underlining, or indexing during the testing session.

Open-book testing rewards familiarity with the reference. Candidates who know where to locate service rules, feeder requirements, equipment provisions, generator articles, grounding requirements, and disconnecting means can use their time more effectively.

Online exam preparation should therefore include regular reference practice with the 2026 NEC instead of relying only on summaries or notes.

State Requirements

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician license is a specialized electrical credential for work on transmission and distribution systems from the original source of electricity to the substation.

Texas recognizes a qualifying pathway involving at least 7,000 hours of training in an apprenticeship program approved by the United States Department of Labor.

Texas also recognizes a pathway involving at least 3.5 years of experience as a Journeyman Lineman for an electric utility, electric cooperative, municipally owned utility, or electrical contractor in Texas.

Applicants must document the qualification pathway used and submit the required information to TDLR. The licensing agency reviews the application before granting examination eligibility.

The Journeyman Lineman Electrician credential has a limited scope and is different from the Texas Journeyman Electrician license. Candidates should understand the specific work authorized by the license before applying.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to annual renewal requirements. Continuing education requirements also apply to the credential.

The 2026 National Electrical Code became the Texas electrical code effective September 1, 2026, and current Journeyman Lineman Electrician examinations reference this edition.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions and Theory: Six scored items cover NEC terminology and basic electrical theory. Review Article 100 along with voltage, current, resistance, power, circuits, conductors, and common electrical relationships.

Electrical Services: Eight scored items involve services, service equipment, and separately derived systems. Review service conductors, service disconnecting means, service ratings, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, and related requirements.

Service Equipment: Study equipment ratings, interrupting ratings, disconnect requirements, grouping, accessibility, identification, and installation practices that apply to service equipment.

Separately Derived Systems: Review transformers, system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and the grounding and bonding requirements that apply to separately derived systems.

Electrical Feeders: Four scored items cover feeders. Study conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounded conductors, grounding and bonding, and feeder installation requirements.

Branch Circuits: Review branch-circuit conductors, overcurrent protection, circuit ratings, and the general NEC provisions that apply to equipment supplied by branch circuits.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials: Study the Chapter 3 requirements for raceways, conductors, cables, boxes, enclosures, fittings, supports, physical protection, and wiring methods.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: With 10 scored items, this is the largest exam category. Review equipment installation, equipment ratings, conductor terminations, grounding, accessibility, clearances, overcurrent protection, and general electrical equipment requirements.

Article 110: General Requirements for Electrical Installations are important across many categories. Review equipment listing and labeling, installation and use, conductor terminations, working space, guarding, and interrupting ratings.

Grounding and Bonding: Article 250 can affect services, feeders, equipment, and separately derived systems. Study equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, grounding electrodes, and fault-current paths.

Overcurrent Protection: Review Article 240 and the relationship between conductor ampacity, overcurrent devices, equipment protection, and circuit requirements.

Conductors: Study conductor ampacity, insulation ratings, temperature limitations, correction factors, adjustment factors, terminal temperature requirements, and conductor identification.

Motors and Generators: Two scored items cover motors and generators. Review conductor requirements, branch-circuit protection, overload protection, disconnects, grounding, and generator-related NEC provisions.

Generators: Become familiar with generator conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, disconnecting means, and other requirements that may apply to generator systems.

Electrical Control Devices: Six scored items are shared between controls and disconnecting means. Review switches, controllers, contactors, disconnect ratings, equipment control, and safe isolation.

Disconnecting Means: Study location, accessibility, ratings, grouping, visibility, lockability, and equipment-specific disconnect requirements.

Transformers: Review transformer conductor protection, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and separately derived system provisions where applicable.

Equipment Working Space: Review Article 110 working-space requirements, equipment access, clearances, and guarding. Safe access to electrical equipment is a recurring NEC principle.

Code Navigation: Use the 2026 NEC during online exam prep. Practice identifying the main subject of a question before opening the codebook, then move directly to the likely article, table, or section.

Cross-Reference Practice: Many questions may require more than one NEC article. A service question might require Article 230 and Article 250, while a generator question may connect generator rules with grounding, overcurrent protection, or disconnecting requirements.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 120 minutes for 40 scored items. Practice maintaining a pace of about three minutes per scored question while leaving room for more involved code lookups.

Study by Exam Weight: Give the greatest attention to Electrical Equipment and Devices, Services and Separately Derived Systems, Definitions and Theory, and Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means. These categories represent the largest portions of the examination.
