### Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

### State Requirements

The Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician license is a specialized credential covering electrical work on transmission and distribution systems from the original source of electricity to the substation.

Texas provides qualifying experience pathways for applicants seeking this license. One pathway is completion of at least **7,000 hours of training in an apprenticeship program approved by the United States Department of Labor**.

Another pathway is at least **3.5 years of experience as a Journeyman Lineman** for an electric utility, electric cooperative, municipally owned utility, or electrical contractor in Texas.

Applicants must provide documentation supporting the pathway used to qualify. TDLR reviews the information as part of the licensing process before examination authorization is granted.

The Journeyman Lineman Electrician license is limited in scope and is not the same as the Texas Journeyman Electrician license. Candidates should understand the specific work authorized by the credential before applying.

Texas Journeyman Lineman Electrician license holders are subject to the renewal requirements that apply to Texas electrician licenses. Continuing education requirements also apply to renewal.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions and Theory:** Six scored items cover NEC definitions and electrical theory. Review Article 100 terminology along with foundational electrical relationships involving voltage, current, resistance, power, conductors, and circuit behavior.

**Electrical Services:** Eight scored items involve services, service equipment, and separately derived systems. Study service conductors, service disconnecting means, service equipment ratings, grounding and bonding, and related requirements.

**Separately Derived Systems:** Review transformers, system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and the Article 250 requirements that apply to separately derived systems.

**Electrical Feeders:** Four scored items cover feeder requirements. Study conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and grounding and bonding.

**Branch Circuits:** Review branch-circuit conductor requirements, overcurrent protection, circuit ratings, and the general provisions that may apply to equipment supplied by branch circuits.

**Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials:** Study raceways, conductors, boxes, enclosures, supports, fittings, cable systems, and the Chapter 3 rules governing acceptable wiring methods.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** This is the largest examination category with 10 scored items. Candidates should study equipment installation, ratings, protection, identification, working clearances, and the requirements that govern electrical devices used within the scope of the license.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 is an important cross-cutting topic. Review effective ground-fault current paths, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, and grounding of services and separately derived systems.

**Service Equipment:** Practice locating NEC requirements for service disconnects, overcurrent protection, bonding, equipment ratings, grouping, accessibility, and related service installation issues.

**Transformers:** Review transformer installations, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and separately derived system requirements where applicable.

**Motors and Generators:** Two scored items address motors and generators. Review conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, overloads, disconnects, grounding, and the requirements applicable to generator systems.

**Generators:** Candidates should understand basic generator requirements, including disconnecting means, grounding, conductor protection, and interactions with other NEC provisions.

**Electrical Control Devices:** Six scored items are shared between controls and disconnecting means. Review switches, controllers, contactors, disconnects, ratings, locations, accessibility, and safe isolation of electrical equipment.

**Disconnecting Means:** Study requirements governing location, visibility, accessibility, ratings, grouping, and identification of disconnecting equipment.

**Conductors:** Review conductor ampacity, insulation ratings, temperature limitations, adjustment factors, correction factors, and terminal ratings. Understand how conductor rules apply across services, feeders, branch circuits, and equipment.

**Overcurrent Protection:** Review Article 240 and related rules governing conductor and equipment protection. Become comfortable locating fuse, circuit-breaker, and conductor protection provisions.

**Working Space and Equipment Access:** Article 110 contains important general requirements for electrical equipment. Review working clearances, dedicated equipment space, access, guarding, and equipment installation principles.

**Highlighted and Tabbed NEC Navigation:** Use the prepared book in timed practice sessions. Identify the topic, move to the likely NEC article, locate the correct section or table, confirm the requirement, and return to the question efficiently.

**Time Management:** Candidates receive 120 minutes for 40 scored items. That provides an average of approximately three minutes per scored item. Timed practice can help candidates balance direct code questions with questions requiring calculations or multiple code references.
