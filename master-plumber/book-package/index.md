### Closed Book Test

The Texas Master Plumber examination is a **closed book test**. Personal books, codes, study guides, notes, papers, scratch paper, and reference materials may not be brought into the examination room.

The testing center provides materials authorized by the examination sponsor when needed. Candidates should therefore prepare to answer plumbing-code and regulatory questions without referring to their personal IPC, IFGC, or UPC during the examination.

This testing format makes active recall especially important. Candidates should study a subject from the applicable plumbing code, close the book, and then answer questions or explain the requirements from memory. Returning to the book afterward helps identify mistakes and reinforce the correct rule.

The same approach can be used for sizing problems. Candidates should understand the process for determining pipe sizes rather than trying to memorize every sizing table. Texas Master Plumber review material explains that information needed for designated sizing exercises is provided during the examination, while candidates are expected to know how to use that information correctly.

Closed-book preparation should also include timed study sessions. With a large number of examination items and multiple subject areas, candidates need to maintain a steady pace while still devoting enough time to mathematics, diagrams, sizing, and system-design problems.

### State Requirements

Texas currently allows an applicant to qualify for the Master Plumber examination after holding a **Journeyman Plumber license for at least two years**. This requirement became effective September 1, 2025, reducing the previous standard waiting period.

An applicant may also qualify after holding a Journeyman Plumber license for at least **one year** when the applicant has successfully completed a training program approved by the United States Department of Labor Office of Apprenticeship.

Texas also provides eligibility pathways for individuals who currently hold a Master Plumber license in another state and previously held the applicable Journeyman Plumber credential for the required period.

A Texas Master Plumber may install, change, repair, service, or renovate plumbing while working under the supervision of a **Responsible Master Plumber**. A Master Plumber may directly supervise a Plumber's Apprentice on plumbing job sites and may supervise a Tradesman Plumber-Limited on job sites involving structures other than one- and two-family dwellings.

The Master Plumber license and the Responsible Master Plumber designation are related but distinct. The RMP designation carries additional responsibility for plumbing work offered and performed through a plumbing company and is the designation associated with obtaining permits and assuming responsibility for plumbing work performed under the applicable business structure.

Texas plumbing licenses renew annually. Master Plumbers must complete **six hours of approved continuing professional education** to remain eligible for renewal.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Plumbing Codes:** Review definitions, general regulations, plumbing fixtures, water heaters, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, indirect wastes, vents, traps, fuel-gas piping, materials, testing, and other code provisions that apply to commercial and residential plumbing systems.

**Definitions:** Strong code preparation starts with correct plumbing terminology. Review terms such as building drain, building sewer, branch, fixture drain, drainage fixture unit, trap arm, vent stack, stack vent, developed length, water service, indirect waste, and other definitions used throughout the codes.

**Water Heaters:** Water-heater installation is a major Master Plumber study subject. Review temperature and pressure relief valves, discharge piping, combustion air, appliance clearances, gas shutoff valves, venting, water connections, drain pans, locations, and common installation violations.

**Water Supply and Distribution:** Study water-service sizing, distribution piping, fixture-unit calculations, pressure requirements, valves, backflow prevention, cross-connection control, pipe materials, and system design.

**Sanitary Drainage:** Review drainage fixture units, pipe sizing, slope, changes in direction, building drains, stacks, horizontal branches, cleanouts, drainage fittings, testing, and proper system layout.

**Venting:** Understand vent sizing, fixture venting, wet venting, common vents, stack venting, vent stacks, developed length, vent terminals, and the relationship between drainage and vent systems.

**Traps:** Study trap seals, trap arms, prohibited traps, fixture connections, trap protection, and the effects of siphonage, backpressure, and other pressure changes within drainage systems.

**Fuel-Gas Pipe Sizing:** Practice interpreting piping diagrams and using demand, developed length, and sizing information to determine the minimum acceptable gas-pipe size supplying multiple appliances.

**Water-Pipe Sizing:** Review fixture-unit demand, developed length, pressure considerations, and sizing procedures used to determine water-service and distribution piping.

**Gas-Fired Appliance Vent Sizing:** Candidates should understand how to size individual and combined vent systems serving gas-fired appliances.

**Drainage Pipe Sizing:** Practice evaluating multi-story drainage systems, calculating drainage fixture-unit loads, and selecting minimum pipe sizes for stacks, branches, and building drainage piping.

**Three-Story Drainage and Vent Design:** The Master Plumber review material includes system-design preparation involving a three-story sanitary drainage and vent system serving multiple plumbing fixtures. Candidates should practice complete system layouts rather than studying individual fittings in isolation.

**Plumbing Mathematics:** Review practical mathematics used in plumbing design and installation. Candidates should be comfortable manipulating measurements, dimensions, areas, volumes, slopes, developed lengths, and other numerical relationships used in the trade.

**Texas Plumbing License Law and Board Rules:** Study the responsibilities and limitations associated with plumbing licenses, supervision, licensing categories, regulatory compliance, and the authority of the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners.

**Texas Accessibility Standards:** Master Plumbers should understand accessibility requirements affecting plumbing fixtures and facilities. Study clearances, fixture placement, accessible plumbing elements, and related dimensional requirements.

**OSHA Construction Safety:** Review workplace-safety subjects relevant to plumbing operations, including personal protective equipment, tools, welding and cutting, scaffolding, and excavations.

**Liquefied Petroleum Gas:** Master-level preparation includes LP-gas-related material. Review applicable gas-system concepts, piping, equipment installation, testing, and safety requirements relevant to the examination.

**Closed-Book Recall:** Since personal references are prohibited during the exam, candidates should regularly practice without books. Use the code references to learn the requirement, then test recall under realistic conditions.

**Timed Practice:** The current Master Plumber examination allows 360 minutes for 308 items. Practice maintaining a consistent pace while budgeting additional time for calculations, charts, diagrams, and system-design questions.

The Master Plumber license comes from the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. You apply to TSBPE, then take the written exam at Pearson VUE and, where required, the practical in Austin or Waco.
