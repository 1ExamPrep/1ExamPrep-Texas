### Build your “answer-finding” routine

Every open-book test rewards a routine. The goal is to create a consistent method for moving from question → keyword → section → answer.

- **Step 1: Identify the topic.** Is the question asking about a definition, a permit rule, a restriction, or a maintenance duty?
- **Step 2: Pull 2–3 keywords.** Use terms you can search for in headings and section titles (or that you know show up in definitions).
- **Step 3: Locate the rule.** Find the governing section, then read above and below for exceptions and conditions.
- **Step 4: Confirm the “best” answer.** Multiple-choice questions often include close distractors. Use the ordinance language to eliminate options.

### Study by weighted content areas

The outline weights are your roadmap. Here’s how to translate them into a week-to-week plan:

## How does the San Antonio Billboard Operator (975Y) exam work?

The San Antonio Billboard Operator (975Y) exam is set by a Texas city or by TDLR rather than by a statewide contractor board. The city or agency approves you, then the exam is scheduled with ICC or PSI.

- ICC exam 975 has 70 questions in 3 hours, is open book and costs $115.
- It is written to the San Antonio sign code and billboard ordinance, plus Chapter 10 building-related codes.
- Texas does not license general contractors at the state level; the Texas Residential Construction Commission was abolished in 2009.
- Cities set their own registration rules, and some, such as San Antonio, require an ICC exam for certain trades.
- San Antonio sign operators need a passing ICC exam, a $45,000 bond and $1,000,000 in general liability insurance.
- Texas code enforcement officers register with TDLR after the TEEX course and a closed-book PSI exam that costs $50.
