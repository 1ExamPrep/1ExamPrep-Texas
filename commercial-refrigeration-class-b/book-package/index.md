### Licensing Steps

1. **Meet the minimum age requirement.** Applicants must be at least 18 years old.

5. **Submit the Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor application.** TDLR reviews applicant eligibility before examination authorization.
6. **Receive examination eligibility.** After TDLR approves eligibility, PSI provides instructions for registering and scheduling the applicable examination.
7. **Prepare for the Commercial Refrigeration Class B examination.** Study the current codes, technical references, Texas licensing law, and refrigeration subjects covered by the examination blueprint.

10. **Complete the remaining licensing requirements.** Follow TDLR procedures for any additional documents or requirements that apply before the license is issued.

### State Requirements

The Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors program provides two contractor license classes: Class A and Class B. The license class determines the equipment capacity on which the contractor may perform authorized work.

A **Class B Commercial Refrigeration and Process Cooling or Heating** license authorizes qualifying refrigeration contracting involving a system, product, or individual piece of equipment with no more than **25 tons of cooling capacity** or **1.5 million Btu per hour of heating output**.

The Commercial Refrigeration endorsement applies to the use of mechanical or absorption equipment to control temperature or humidity in order to satisfy the intended use of a particular space. Process Cooling or Heating relates to control of temperature, humidity, or cleanliness for production requirements or proper operation of equipment.

The Class B license differs from Class A primarily in its capacity limitation. A Class A license permits work on equipment of any size within the authorized endorsement, while Class B remains subject to the applicable cooling and heating capacity limits.

The standard Texas experience pathway requires at least **48 months of practical air-conditioning and refrigeration experience during the preceding 72 months**. Applicants must document qualifying experience according to TDLR requirements.

Texas also recognizes qualifying experience through certain alternate pathways. Applicants should use the pathway that applies to their individual experience and licensing history when completing the contractor application.

Passing the examination is one step in the licensing process. Applicants must complete all applicable TDLR requirements before performing work as a licensed Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**HVACR Systems and Design:** With approximately 17 questions, this is the largest area of the Commercial Refrigeration Class B examination. Review complete refrigeration systems, equipment application, system configuration, operating conditions, load considerations, installation requirements, and commercial refrigeration design principles.

**Controls:** Approximately 11 questions focus on controls. Study thermostats, pressure controls, relays, contactors, solenoid valves, defrost controls, temperature controllers, safety controls, and electrical control sequences.

**Equipment Requirements:** Approximately 10 questions cover equipment requirements. Review proper installation, access, clearances, manufacturer requirements, machinery-space provisions, equipment ratings, and mechanical-code requirements.

**Piping:** Approximately nine questions involve piping. Study suction, discharge, and liquid lines, refrigerant piping practices, pressure considerations, oil return, insulation, piping support, fittings, valves, and installation procedures.

**Service:** Approximately nine questions cover service and troubleshooting. Review compressor problems, refrigerant charging, electrical diagnosis, airflow problems, system pressures, leak detection, evacuation, refrigerant recovery, and operating conditions.

**Basic Refrigeration Components:** Approximately eight questions test knowledge of compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, receivers, accumulators, filter-driers, sight glasses, and other common refrigeration components.

**Refrigeration Principles:** Approximately eight questions involve the refrigeration cycle and related physical principles. Review heat transfer, sensible heat, latent heat, saturation, pressure-temperature relationships, superheat, subcooling, compression, condensation, expansion, and evaporation.

**Safety:** Approximately six questions address safety. Study electrical hazards, refrigerant handling, high-pressure conditions, personal protective equipment, workplace procedures, and safe servicing practices.

**Air Distribution:** Approximately five questions involve air distribution. Review airflow, duct sizing, friction rate, velocity, static pressure, and basic supply and return concepts. The included Ductulator can support practice in this area.

**Testing:** Approximately five questions cover testing procedures. Review electrical measurements, pressure measurements, temperature measurements, superheat, subcooling, refrigerant testing, leak testing, and evaluation of refrigeration-system performance.

**Combustion Air:** Approximately three questions address combustion air. Review mechanical-code and fuel-gas requirements when fuel-burning equipment is involved in an installation.

**Commercial Refrigeration Equipment:** Become familiar with systems such as walk-in coolers, walk-in freezers, reach-in equipment, condensing units, evaporators, ice-making equipment, and related commercial refrigeration applications.

**Compressors:** Review compressor types, operation, lubrication, electrical requirements, failure conditions, capacity, and the role of the compressor within the refrigeration cycle.

**Metering Devices:** Understand thermostatic expansion valves, fixed metering devices, and other refrigerant-flow controls. Review how metering affects evaporator operation, superheat, and system performance.

**Defrost:** Study applicable defrost methods, controls, timers, termination methods, and system behavior during and after a defrost cycle.

**Superheat and Subcooling:** Be comfortable with the concepts and their use when evaluating system operation and refrigerant charge.

**Texas Laws and Rules:** Review Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1302 and Texas Administrative Code Chapter 75 so technical preparation is supported by an understanding of the responsibilities and limitations associated with the contractor license.

**Reference Navigation:** Practice identifying the nature of the question before opening a book. A refrigeration-theory question may lead to Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, a mechanical installation question to the IMC or UMC, a combustion question to the IFGC, and a contractor-law question to Texas statutes or administrative rules.
