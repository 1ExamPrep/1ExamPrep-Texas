About this Journeyman Industrial Electrician package

Prepare for the Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examination with the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition, the current electrical code reference used for Texas electrician examinations. Texas adopted the 2026 National Electrical Code effective September 1, 2026, making this edition the primary technical reference for candidates pursuing the Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician license through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

The Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician license is a specialized electrical credential for individuals performing electrical work exclusively at qualifying industrial businesses. The license applies to electrical work at facilities such as chemical plants, petrochemical plants, refineries, natural gas plants, natural gas treating plants, pipelines, and oil and gas exploration and production operations.

This is a limited Texas electrical license. A Journeyman Industrial Electrician may not use this credential to perform general residential or commercial electrical work, and the credential is not equivalent to the Texas Journeyman Electrician license. Experience gained solely under the Journeyman Industrial Electrician license cannot be applied toward the experience requirement for a Texas Master Electrician license.

The licensing examination is administered through PSI for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). The exam covers electrical definitions, theory, plans, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, electrical equipment, motors, generators, control devices, disconnecting means, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and electrical safety.

The 2026 NEC is the essential technical reference included in this package. Industrial electrical work requires candidates to move beyond basic code memorization and understand how NEC requirements interact within complex electrical systems. Candidates should be prepared to work with conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, transformers, motors, controllers, disconnects, raceways, hazardous locations, industrial equipment, and electrical safety.

Because the examination is open book, efficient NEC navigation is an important part of preparation. Candidates should practice identifying the subject of a question, moving directly to the applicable article or table, and applying the rule without spending unnecessary time searching through the code.

Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examination is administered by PSI after TDLR has reviewed the applicant's licensing application and qualifying experience.

Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examination is an open book test.

The current approved technical reference is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

Any soft-bound edition of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound NEC books are not permitted in the testing room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes made before the examination session. Permanent NEC index tabs manufactured or provided by the NEC publisher may be used.

Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or otherwise mark the NEC during the examination. Homemade, do-it-yourself, or non-NEC tabs are not permitted. Additional loose paper or pages attached to the book are also prohibited.

Industrial candidates should practice navigating the NEC sections most relevant to motors, hazardous locations, industrial wiring methods, transformers, services, grounding and bonding, feeders, disconnects, overcurrent protection, and electrical equipment. Knowing where to begin a code search can reduce the amount of time required for each question.

Licensing Steps

  1. Register and work as a licensed electrical apprentice. Qualifying experience must be accumulated while working within the Texas electrical licensing structure.
  2. Work under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician. The required on-the-job training must be performed under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas.
  3. Complete 8,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training. Texas requires Journeyman Industrial Electrician applicants to document the full experience requirement before applying.
  4. Obtain experience verification. Each Master Electrician who supervised qualifying work must complete the required experience-verification documentation.
  5. Submit the Journeyman Industrial Electrician application. The application and supporting documentation are submitted to TDLR for review.
  6. Receive examination authorization. After TDLR verifies the application and qualifying experience, the applicant is approved to test.
  7. Schedule the examination through PSI. Approved candidates use the PSI scheduling process for Texas electrician examinations.
  8. Prepare with the 2026 NEC. Review the official content outline and practice applying NEC requirements to industrial electrical situations.
  9. Take the Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examination. Complete the scored and non-scored portions within the allotted testing time.
  10. Earn the required passing score. A score of 70% or better is required.
  11. Complete the licensing process. After satisfying the application, experience, and examination requirements, complete any remaining TDLR steps associated with license issuance.

State Requirements

Texas requires a Journeyman Industrial Electrician applicant to complete 8,000 hours of on-the-job training as a licensed electrical apprentice under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.

Each Master Electrician who supervised qualifying work must verify the applicant's experience. TDLR may review and confirm submitted experience information as part of the licensing process.

The Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician license authorizes electrical work exclusively at a business that operates a chemical plant, petrochemical plant, refinery, natural gas plant, natural gas treating plant, pipeline, or oil and gas exploration and production operation.

The credential is a limited license. Journeyman Industrial Electricians may not perform general residential or commercial electrical work under this credential.

The Journeyman Industrial Electrician license is also not equivalent to the general Texas Journeyman Electrician license. Experience obtained while working as a Journeyman Industrial Electrician does not qualify toward the experience requirement for a Texas Master Electrician license.

The license is valid for one year and must be renewed annually.

Journeyman Industrial Electricians must complete four hours of continuing education during each license cycle before renewal. Required continuing education addresses the National Electrical Code, Texas electrician law, Texas electrician administrative rules, and electrical safety.

Texas adopted the 2026 National Electrical Code as the state electrical code effective September 1, 2026. Current Texas electrician examinations are referenced to the 2026 NEC.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions, Theory, and Plans: Seven scored items cover NEC terminology, electrical theory, and plan interpretation. Review Article 100 definitions along with voltage, current, resistance, power, transformers, three-phase systems, conductor relationships, and industrial electrical symbols.

Electrical Services and Service Equipment: Review Article 230 requirements for service conductors, service equipment, disconnects, ratings, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and industrial service arrangements.

Separately Derived Systems: Industrial facilities frequently use transformers and other separately derived systems. Study system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, grounding electrode connections, and related Article 250 requirements.

Electrical Feeders: Review feeder conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, load calculations, and industrial distribution systems.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: Six scored items address branch circuits. Study conductor sizing, continuous and noncontinuous loads, overcurrent protection, ampacity, adjustment factors, correction factors, and branch-circuit requirements.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: With 10 scored items, this is the largest examination category. Review raceways, conduit, cable, conductors, boxes, fittings, cable trays, supports, wet locations, underground installations, and protection from physical damage.

Industrial Raceways: Become familiar with rigid metal conduit, intermediate metal conduit, electrical metallic tubing, flexible metal conduit, liquidtight systems, and other wiring methods commonly encountered in industrial environments.

Conductor Ampacity: Study Article 310 tables, conductor insulation, temperature ratings, terminal limitations, adjustment factors, correction factors, parallel conductors, and conductor sizing for industrial loads.

Grounding and Bonding: Article 250 should be a major part of study. Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, grounding for transformers, service bonding, and effective ground-fault current paths.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Six scored items cover electrical equipment and devices. Review panelboards, switchboards, transformers, switches, receptacles, overcurrent devices, utilization equipment, and equipment installation requirements.

Motors and Generators: Seven scored items focus on motors and generators. Article 430 is especially important for industrial electricians. Review motor full-load current, conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, and disconnects.

Motor Calculations: Practice single-motor and multiple-motor calculations, conductor sizing, overload selection, overcurrent protection, and feeder requirements for groups of motors.

Motor Controllers: Understand controllers, contactors, starters, overload relays, control circuits, and disconnecting means. Industrial systems often require coordinated application of several NEC requirements.

Generators: Review generator conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, transfer equipment, separately derived system considerations, and installation requirements.

Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: Three scored items cover controls and disconnects. Review ratings, accessibility, location, grouping, lockable disconnects, controllers, and equipment isolation.

Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: Six items address specialized NEC requirements. Industrial candidates should pay particular attention to installations that may involve hazardous or classified locations.

Hazardous Locations: Chemical plants, refineries, natural gas facilities, and oil and gas operations often include areas where flammable gases, vapors, liquids, or combustible materials are present. Review classification concepts and applicable NEC wiring and equipment requirements.

Industrial Equipment: Study equipment installation, working clearances, equipment ratings, overcurrent protection, grounding, disconnects, and environmental considerations.

Electrical Safety: Seven scored items are devoted specifically to electrical safety. Candidates should understand safe work principles involving energized equipment, electrical hazards, equipment isolation, proper protection, grounding, and code-compliant installations.

Code Navigation: Practice moving rapidly between Articles 100, 110, 215, 230, 240, 250, 300, 310, 430, Chapter 5 requirements, and other sections relevant to industrial work.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 180 minutes for 60 scored items. Develop a steady pace of approximately three minutes per scored question while reserving additional time for calculations or complex code lookups.
