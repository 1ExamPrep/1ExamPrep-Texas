Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examination is administered through PSI after the applicant has been approved to test by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 60
  • Time for Scored Items: 180 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

The current examination content outline distributes the 60 scored items across the following subject areas:

  • Definitions, Theory, and Plans: 7 items

  • Electrical Feeders: 4 items

  • Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials: 10 items
  • Electrical Equipment and Devices: 6 items
  • Motors and Generators: 7 items
  • Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: 3 items

Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials is the largest individual examination category with 10 scored items. Definitions, Theory, and Plans; Motors and Generators; and Electrical Safety each account for seven items. Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors, Electrical Equipment and Devices, and Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions each contain six items.

This distribution shows why balanced preparation matters. Industrial candidates should spend substantial time on wiring methods and motors, but services, feeders, safety, controls, equipment, theory, conductors, and special installations also require careful review.

Unless an examination question specifically states otherwise, conductors are considered copper.

Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examination is an open book test.

The approved technical reference is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own acceptable NEC book to the examination site.

Any soft-bound edition of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound NEC copies are not permitted in the examination room.

The open-book format makes reference-navigation practice an important part of preparation. Candidates should become comfortable moving among services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, motor provisions, special occupancies, and general equipment requirements.

Industrial questions can require more than one NEC article. A motor installation may involve Article 430 as well as conductor, grounding, disconnecting, and special-location provisions. A transformer question may involve separately derived systems, overcurrent protection, conductor sizing, and grounding and bonding.

Regular use of the 2026 NEC during online exam prep can help candidates recognize these relationships and find supporting code requirements more efficiently during the examination.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the Journeyman Industrial Electrician license matches your intended work. This credential is limited to qualifying electrical work at designated industrial operations.
  2. Gain qualifying on-the-job electrical experience. Texas requires supervised electrical training before an applicant may qualify for the license.
  3. Complete 8,000 hours of qualifying training. Applicants must complete the required experience as a licensed electrical apprentice under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.
  4. Obtain experience verification. Each Master Electrician who supervised qualifying work must document the applicable experience.
  5. Submit the Journeyman Industrial Electrician application. The completed application and experience documentation are submitted to TDLR.
  6. Receive examination authorization. After the application has been reviewed and verified, the applicant is approved to test.
  7. Schedule the examination through PSI. Approved candidates follow the PSI scheduling process.
  8. Prepare using the 2026 NEC. Review wiring methods, motors, services, feeders, branch circuits, equipment, controls, special occupancies, and safety.
  9. Use online exam prep for structured study. Work through the examination subjects while practicing code navigation and application.
  10. Take the Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examination. Complete the scored and non-scored items within the allotted testing time.
  11. Earn the required passing score. A minimum score of 70% is required.
  12. Complete all remaining licensing requirements. Follow the applicable TDLR process for license issuance after satisfying the examination requirement.

State Requirements

A Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician is licensed to perform electrical work exclusively at a business operating a chemical plant, petrochemical plant, refinery, natural gas plant, natural gas treating plant, pipeline, or oil and gas exploration and production operation.

The license is a limited credential and is not equivalent to a Texas Journeyman Electrician license. Journeyman Industrial Electricians may not perform general residential or commercial electrical work under this credential.

Applicants must complete at least 8,000 hours of on-the-job training as a licensed electrical apprentice under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.

Each supervising Master Electrician must verify the qualifying experience. TDLR may contact the individuals involved to verify the information submitted with the application.

The limited industrial scope makes trade-specific preparation important. Candidates should understand the electrical systems commonly encountered in industrial facilities while also knowing how those systems are governed by general NEC requirements.

Journeyman Industrial Electrician experience may not be used toward the experience requirement for a Texas Master Electrician license.

Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually. Applicable continuing education requirements must also be completed for renewal.

The 2026 National Electrical Code became the Texas electrical code effective September 1, 2026, and current Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examinations reference this edition.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions, Theory, and Plans: Seven scored items cover electrical terminology, theory, and plan interpretation. Review Article 100 definitions along with voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, and common electrical symbols.

Electrical Services: Study service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, equipment ratings, overcurrent protection, grounding, and bonding. Industrial facilities can involve substantial service equipment, making a strong understanding of service requirements important.

Separately Derived Systems: Industrial facilities commonly use transformers. Review system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and related grounding and bonding provisions.

Electrical Feeders: Study feeder conductor sizing, load requirements, overcurrent protection, grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and feeder arrangements supplying industrial equipment.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: Six scored items cover branch circuits and conductors. Review conductor ampacity, continuous loads, circuit ratings, overcurrent protection, grounding, and equipment supply requirements.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials: With 10 scored items, this is the largest examination category. Industrial electricians should be comfortable with the Chapter 3 wiring methods used in demanding environments.

Raceways: Review rigid metal conduit, intermediate metal conduit, electrical metallic tubing, flexible raceways, fittings, support requirements, conductor fill, bends, physical protection, and environmental limitations.

Cable Systems: Study the permitted uses and limitations of cable wiring methods that may appear in industrial facilities. Understand support, protection, equipment connections, and environmental considerations.

Boxes, Cabinets, and Enclosures: Review pull boxes, junction boxes, cabinets, wireways, enclosure requirements, accessibility, environmental ratings, and conductor installation requirements.

Conductor Ampacity: Practice using Article 310 tables. Review conductor insulation types, temperature ratings, correction factors, adjustment factors, terminal limitations, and ampacity requirements.

Grounding and Bonding: Article 250 is important throughout the exam. Review grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, effective ground-fault current paths, services, feeders, and separately derived systems.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Six scored items involve equipment and devices. Study equipment ratings, listing and labeling, installation and use, working space, overcurrent protection, and environmental suitability.

Motors and Generators: Seven scored items make this one of the major exam categories. Article 430 deserves regular attention during preparation.

Motor Conductors: Review motor branch-circuit conductor sizing, feeder conductors supplying motors, full-load current tables, and the difference between conductor sizing and motor overload protection.

Motor Overload Protection: Study overload devices, motor nameplate information, NEC requirements, and the relationship between overload protection and short-circuit and ground-fault protection.

Motor Branch-Circuit Protection: Review branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, maximum device ratings, permitted protective devices, and applicable motor tables.

Motor Controllers and Disconnects: Study controllers, starters, contactors, disconnecting means, ratings, locations, visibility, accessibility, and installation requirements.

Generators: Review conductor protection, grounding and bonding, disconnecting means, transfer equipment, and other NEC requirements affecting generators.

Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means: Three scored items address controls and disconnects. Review switches, relays, contactors, controllers, disconnect ratings, and safe isolation requirements.

Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: Six scored items address specialized installations that can be particularly important in industrial facilities.

Hazardous Locations: Chemical plants, refineries, natural gas operations, and similar facilities may contain classified locations. Candidates should review the NEC requirements addressing areas where flammable gases, vapors, liquids, combustible dusts, or fibers may be present.

Equipment Suitability: Industrial electrical equipment must be suitable for the environment where it is installed. Review requirements involving location classification, enclosure types, wiring methods, temperature limitations, and listed equipment.

Electrical Safety: Seven scored items specifically address electrical safety. Candidates should review safe installation requirements, working clearances, guarding, equipment condition, access, protection from live parts, and related code provisions.

Article 110: General Requirements for Electrical Installations deserve careful review. Study installation and use, interrupting ratings, conductor terminations, working space, guarding, equipment identification, and access.

Overcurrent Protection: Review Article 240 and understand how overcurrent devices protect conductors and equipment. Industrial questions may require applying general protection rules alongside motor or equipment-specific provisions.

Code Cross-References: Industrial questions frequently involve more than one NEC article. Practice moving from a specialized article to related grounding, conductor, wiring-method, service, or overcurrent requirements.

Code Navigation: Use the 2026 NEC throughout online exam preparation. Practice identifying the primary subject first, moving to the likely NEC article, locating the relevant section or table, and confirming the requirement.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 180 minutes for 60 scored items. This averages approximately three minutes per scored question. Practice maintaining a consistent pace while reserving additional time for complex calculations and multi-article code questions.

Study by Exam Weight: Give strong attention to Wiring Methods and Materials, Motors and Generators, Electrical Safety, and Definitions, Theory, and Plans. Continue reviewing services, feeders, branch circuits, electrical equipment, controls, and special occupancies to maintain balanced exam coverage.
