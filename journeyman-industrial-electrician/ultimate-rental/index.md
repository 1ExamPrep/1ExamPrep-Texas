### State Requirements

The Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician license is a specialized electrical credential with a defined industrial scope. Applicants must document at least **8,000 hours of on-the-job training as a licensed electrical apprentice under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician**.

The license authorizes qualifying electrical work in the industrial facilities covered by the credential. These include qualifying chemical plants, petrochemical plants, refineries, natural gas plants, natural gas treating plants, pipelines, and oil and gas exploration or production operations.

The Journeyman Industrial Electrician credential is not the same as the general Texas Journeyman Electrician license. Its work authority is limited to the qualifying industrial facilities and activities established for the license.

This distinction is important during exam preparation. Candidates should understand general NEC rules while giving particular attention to code requirements commonly associated with industrial equipment, motors, generators, raceways, controls, disconnects, grounding and bonding, classified locations, and electrical safety.

Experience gained while working under the Journeyman Industrial Electrician license does not count toward the experience requirement for the Texas Master Electrician credential.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to annual renewal and applicable continuing education requirements.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Theory, and Plans:** Seven scored items cover electrical terminology, theory, and plan concepts. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, single-phase and three-phase systems, conductor terminology, equipment definitions, and basic plan interpretation.

**Electrical Services:** Study service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, equipment ratings, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and service installation requirements.

**Separately Derived Systems:** Review transformers, system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, and the Article 250 requirements that apply to separately derived systems.

**Electrical Feeders:** Review feeder conductor sizing, grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and feeder installation requirements.

**Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors:** Six scored items cover branch-circuit sizing and conductor requirements. Review conductor ampacity, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, equipment supply, temperature limitations, and adjustment or correction factors.

**Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials:** This is the largest examination category with 10 scored items. Review raceways, cables, conductors, boxes, enclosures, fittings, support requirements, environmental limitations, and protection from physical damage.

**Industrial Raceways:** Study permitted uses, securing and supporting, conductor fill, bends, fittings, wet or corrosive locations, grounding requirements, and protection from mechanical damage.

**Conductor Ampacity:** Practice using NEC ampacity tables and applying insulation ratings, terminal temperature limitations, adjustment factors, correction factors, and other requirements affecting conductor sizing.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Six scored items cover electrical equipment. Review equipment ratings, working clearances, panels, disconnects, grounding, overcurrent protection, enclosures, and equipment suitability.

**Motors and Generators:** Seven scored items make this a major examination category. Review motor full-load currents, conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, feeders supplying multiple motors, and generator requirements.

**Motor Calculations:** Practice finding the applicable NEC motor-table values and using them for conductors, overload devices, branch-circuit protective devices, and feeder calculations.

**Electrical Control Devices:** Study controllers, contactors, relays, switches, control equipment, ratings, and general installation requirements associated with industrial systems.

**Disconnecting Means:** Review location, accessibility, grouping, identification, ratings, and equipment-specific disconnect requirements.

**Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions:** Six scored items address specialized NEC requirements. Candidates should be prepared for installations requiring rules beyond the general wiring chapters.

**Hazardous Locations:** Industrial facilities may contain classified areas. Review hazardous-location concepts, wiring methods, equipment suitability, enclosures, sealing requirements, and the NEC structure used for classified locations.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 is important across numerous examination categories. Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, services, feeders, separately derived systems, and equipment bonding.

**Electrical Safety:** Seven scored items are assigned to electrical safety. Candidates should understand the safety principles and electrical installation requirements relevant to industrial work.

**Working Space and Guarding:** Review Article 110 requirements involving working clearances, accessibility, guarding, environmental suitability, equipment installation, identification, and workmanship.

**Overcurrent Protection:** Study how conductor ampacity, equipment ratings, branch circuits, feeders, motors, and protective devices work together.

**Industrial Equipment:** Practice applying NEC requirements to motors, transformers, motor-control equipment, distribution equipment, disconnects, generators, raceways, and other systems commonly encountered in industrial facilities.

**Code Navigation:** Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC throughout preparation. Practice identifying the main subject of a question first and moving directly to the appropriate article, table, or cross-reference.

**Cross-Reference Practice:** Industrial questions often involve several code provisions. Practice moving between equipment-specific articles and general rules for wiring methods, grounding, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, or disconnecting means.

**Timed Practice:** Candidates receive 180 minutes for 60 scored items. Practice maintaining an average pace of approximately three minutes per scored question while leaving additional time for calculations and detailed code searches.

**Study by Exam Weight:** Give strong attention to Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials, Definitions and Theory, Motors and Generators, Electrical Safety, Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors, Electrical Equipment, and Special Occupancies.

**Long-Term Preparation:** With **1 year of course access**, candidates can divide study into stages. Begin with NEC organization and electrical fundamentals, move into industrial systems and calculations, and use later sessions for timed practice, navigation drills, and reinforcement of weaker topics.
