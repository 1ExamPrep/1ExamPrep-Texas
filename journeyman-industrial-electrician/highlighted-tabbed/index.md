### Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The current approved technical reference is the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own acceptable NEC book to the testing center.

Soft-bound editions of the NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes prepared before the examination session. Permanent NEC index tabs that comply with the applicable testing rules are permitted.

Candidates may not add highlighting, underlining, notes, tabs, or other markings during the examination. Homemade or temporary tabs and added loose paper are not permitted.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Theory, and Plans:** Seven scored items cover NEC terminology, electrical theory, and plan interpretation. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, symbols, and basic electrical calculations.

**Electrical Services:** Study service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, ratings, grounding and bonding, and overcurrent protection. Industrial facilities may use complex service arrangements, making general service knowledge important.

**Separately Derived Systems:** Review transformer grounding and bonding, system bonding jumpers, grounding electrode conductors, and other Article 250 requirements that apply to separately derived systems.

**Electrical Feeders:** Study feeder conductor sizing, grounded conductors, grounding and bonding, load requirements, overcurrent protection, and feeder arrangements supplying industrial equipment.

**Branch Circuits and Conductors:** Six scored items involve branch circuits and conductors. Review conductor ampacity, circuit sizing, overcurrent protection, continuous loads, grounding, and equipment supply requirements.

**Wiring Methods and Materials:** With 10 scored items, this is the largest examination category. Candidates should be comfortable with Chapter 3 wiring methods and their permitted uses.

**Raceways:** Review rigid metal conduit, intermediate metal conduit, electrical metallic tubing, flexible raceways, industrial raceway applications, support requirements, bends, conductor fill, and protection from physical damage.

**Cable Systems:** Study the permitted uses and limitations of cable wiring methods that may appear in industrial facilities. Understand support, protection, environmental considerations, and equipment connections.

**Boxes, Cabinets, and Enclosures:** Review box fill, pull and junction boxes, cabinets, wireways, enclosures, accessibility, environmental ratings, and protection against physical or environmental conditions.

**Conductor Ampacity:** Practice using Article 310 tables. Study insulation types, temperature limitations, adjustment factors, correction factors, terminal ratings, and conductor sizing for industrial loads.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 is central to industrial electrical work. Review equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, bonding jumpers, fault-current paths, services, feeders, and separately derived systems.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Six scored items cover equipment installation and device requirements. Review ratings, working space, equipment identification, overcurrent protection, environmental suitability, and general installation requirements.

**Motors and Generators:** Seven scored items make this a major study category. Industrial electricians should be especially comfortable with Article 430.

**Motor Conductors:** Review motor branch-circuit conductors, feeder conductors supplying motors, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, and conductor sizing based on motor full-load current tables.

**Motor Controllers:** Study controllers, contactors, starters, overload devices, disconnecting means, and the relationship between control equipment and motor circuits.

**Motor Disconnects:** Review location, ratings, accessibility, visibility, grouping, and permitted disconnecting arrangements.

**Generators:** Study conductor protection, disconnects, grounding and bonding, transfer equipment, and the NEC provisions that apply to generator installations.

**Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means:** Three scored items address controls and disconnects. Review switches, contactors, controllers, disconnects, ratings, and installation requirements.

**Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions:** Six scored items address specialized NEC requirements that can be especially relevant in industrial environments.

**Hazardous Locations:** Industrial facilities may include areas where flammable gases, vapors, liquids, dusts, or fibers create special electrical hazards. Candidates should review the NEC articles governing classified locations and equipment suitability.

**Special Equipment:** Study industrial equipment and specialized systems covered by the NEC, including equipment with unique wiring, grounding, disconnect, or overcurrent requirements.

**Safety:** Seven scored items address safety. Candidates should understand safe electrical installation principles, guarding, working clearances, equipment condition, protection from live parts, and other code-related safety requirements.

**Article 110:** General Requirements for Electrical Installations are especially important. Review working space, dedicated equipment space, equipment ratings, interrupting ratings, installation and use, guarding, identification, and access.

**Highlighted and Tabbed NEC Navigation:** Practice with the prepared codebook under timed conditions. Industrial questions can involve multiple articles, so learn to recognize the main subject first and then follow cross-references efficiently.

**Time Management:** Candidates receive 180 minutes for 60 scored items, averaging approximately three minutes per scored question. Practice balancing direct code lookups with calculation and application questions that require more time.
