Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examination is administered through PSI after the applicant has been approved to test by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation.

  • Exam: Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician
  • Licensing Agency: Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Scored Items: 60
  • Time for Scored Items: 180 minutes
  • Non-Scored Items: 5
  • Additional Time for Non-Scored Items: 20 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Primary Reference: National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition

Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examination is an open book test.

The approved technical reference is the National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition. Candidates should become familiar with the code before exam day so they can locate information efficiently during the timed test.

Soft-bound NEC editions may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the testing room. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before the examination, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the testing session.

The included rental NEC is highlighted and tabbed to support code navigation and organized preparation. Candidates should use the book throughout the 6 months of course access to build familiarity with important articles, tables, definitions, and cross-references.

Open-book testing still requires strong subject recognition. A candidate who can quickly identify whether a question relates to services, feeders, wiring methods, motors, equipment, hazardous locations, grounding, controls, or safety can move more efficiently through the code.

Licensing Steps

  1. Gain qualifying industrial electrical experience. Texas requires documented on-the-job training for the Journeyman Industrial Electrician credential.
  2. Work under qualified supervision. Qualifying experience must be completed as a licensed electrical apprentice under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.
  3. Complete the required experience. Applicants must document at least 8,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training.
  4. Obtain experience verification. The Master Electrician or Master Electricians who supervised qualifying work must verify the applicable experience.
  5. Submit the Journeyman Industrial Electrician application. The application and supporting documentation are submitted to TDLR.
  6. Receive examination authorization. TDLR reviews the application before eligible applicants are approved to test.
  7. Schedule through PSI. Approved candidates follow the PSI process to schedule the state examination.
  8. Prepare with the 2026 NEC. Review industrial services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, equipment, motors, generators, controls, special occupancies, and safety.
  9. Use the highlighted and tabbed rental NEC. Practice moving efficiently between important articles and tables throughout preparation.
  10. Use the included online course. The package includes 6 months of course access for structured exam-focused study.
  11. Practice industrial code application. Connect NEC rules to the types of electrical equipment and installations encountered in industrial facilities.
  12. Take the Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician examination. Complete the scored and non-scored questions within the allotted testing period.
  13. Earn the required passing score. A minimum score of 70% is required.
  14. Complete all remaining licensing requirements. Follow the applicable TDLR process for issuance of the Journeyman Industrial Electrician license.

State Requirements

The Texas Journeyman Industrial Electrician license is a specialized electrical credential. Applicants must document at least 8,000 hours of on-the-job training as a licensed electrical apprentice under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.

The credential authorizes qualifying electrical work only within the industrial facilities covered by the license. These facilities include qualifying chemical plants, petrochemical plants, refineries, natural gas plants, natural gas treating plants, pipelines, and oil and gas exploration or production operations.

This is not a general Texas Journeyman Electrician license. The Journeyman Industrial Electrician credential does not authorize unrestricted residential or commercial electrical work outside the industrial scope established for the license.

The limited scope of the credential makes industrial-focused preparation especially important. Candidates should understand general NEC requirements while also studying the code provisions that frequently apply to industrial equipment, raceways, motors, generators, hazardous locations, grounding and bonding, disconnecting means, and safety.

Experience gained while working under the Journeyman Industrial Electrician license does not count toward the experience required for the Texas Master Electrician credential.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to annual renewal requirements and applicable continuing education requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

Definitions, Theory, and Plans: Review NEC definitions, electrical terminology, voltage, current, resistance, power, single-phase and three-phase systems, basic circuit relationships, and plan-reading concepts.

Electrical Services: Study service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, equipment ratings, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and separately derived systems.

Separately Derived Systems: Review transformers, grounding electrode conductors, system bonding jumpers, equipment grounding conductors, and other Article 250 requirements associated with separately derived systems.

Electrical Feeders: Study feeder conductor sizing, grounded conductors, overcurrent protection, equipment grounding conductors, load considerations, and feeder grounding and bonding.

Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors: Review conductor ampacity, continuous loads, branch-circuit sizing, overcurrent protection, equipment supply, conductor temperature limitations, and adjustment or correction factors.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials: This is the largest examination category with 10 scored items. Review raceways, cable systems, industrial wiring methods, conductors, boxes, enclosures, fittings, supports, environmental limitations, and physical protection.

Raceways: Study permitted uses, securing and supporting, conductor fill, bends, fittings, wet and corrosive locations, and protection from physical damage.

Conductors: Review conductor types, insulation ratings, ampacity, temperature limitations, conductor identification, parallel conductors, adjustment factors, and correction factors.

Electrical Equipment and Devices: Six scored items cover equipment installation requirements. Review working clearances, ratings, overcurrent protection, disconnects, grounding, enclosures, switches, panels, and equipment suitability.

Motors and Generators: Seven scored items make this a significant examination category. Study motor full-load current tables, conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, feeders supplying multiple motors, and generator requirements.

Motor Calculations: Practice locating the correct NEC motor current values and applying the appropriate rules for conductor sizing, overloads, protective devices, and feeders.

Electrical Control Devices: Review controllers, contactors, relays, switches, control circuits, and the requirements that apply to industrial control equipment.

Disconnecting Means: Study location, accessibility, ratings, grouping, identification, and equipment-specific disconnect requirements.

Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: Six scored items cover specialized NEC requirements. Industrial facilities may contain installations that require close attention to special rules beyond the general chapters of the NEC.

Hazardous Locations: Industrial electrical work may involve classified locations. Review hazardous-location concepts, equipment suitability, wiring methods, sealing, enclosures, and the code structure governing classified areas.

Grounding and Bonding: Article 250 is important throughout the exam. Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, separately derived systems, services, feeders, and equipment bonding.

Electrical Safety: Seven scored items are assigned to safety. Candidates should understand safe electrical work practices and the code requirements that help protect people and equipment from electrical hazards.

Working Space and Guarding: Review Article 110 requirements involving electrical equipment clearances, accessibility, guarding, environmental suitability, installation, labeling, and workmanship.

Overcurrent Protection: Study the relationship between conductor ampacity, equipment ratings, branch circuits, feeders, motors, and protective devices.

Industrial Equipment: Become comfortable applying NEC requirements to equipment commonly encountered in industrial environments, including motors, motor-control equipment, transformers, disconnects, distribution equipment, and related wiring systems.

Code Navigation: Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC during regular study sessions. Practice identifying the main subject of a question first, then move directly to the relevant article, table, or cross-reference.

Cross-Reference Practice: Industrial NEC questions often connect several requirements. Practice moving between equipment-specific articles and general rules involving wiring methods, grounding, overcurrent protection, or conductor sizing.

Timed Practice: Candidates receive 180 minutes for 60 scored items. Practice maintaining a steady average pace while allowing additional time for calculations and complex code searches.

Study by Exam Weight: Give strong attention to Wiring Methods and Materials, Definitions and Theory, Motors and Generators, Electrical Safety, Branch Circuit Calculations, Equipment, and Special Occupancies. Together, these categories make up most of the scored examination.
