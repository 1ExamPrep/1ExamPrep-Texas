### Exam Details

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) delivers electrician licensing exams through PSI. TDLR’s exam information explains that **starting March 11, 2025**, the **Journeyman Electrician exam** changed to consist of **two separate parts**: a **knowledge portion** and a **calculations portion**. TDLR also states a **score of 70% or better** is required to pass, and candidates may retake exams as needed (with additional fees).

PSI’s Texas Electrician Candidate Information Bulletin (updated 4/20/2026) lists the Journeyman Electrician written exam in two portions with these formats and timing:

- **NEC Knowledge Portion:** 59 items (including 3 non-scored items), **130 minutes**, **70% correct to pass**.
- **Calculations Portion:** 26 items (including 2 non-scored items), **110 minutes**, **70% correct to pass**.

The bulletin also states both portions are multiple choice, you must pass **each portion** with a minimum score of 70%, and time from one portion **does not roll over** to the next.

**Exam fee:** PSI’s bulletin lists the Journeyman Electrician examination fee as **$78 for both portions**, and if you fail one portion or both portions, the retake examination fee is **$78**.

For planning your prep, the bulletin provides a content outline for each portion. The NEC Knowledge portion emphasizes code understanding and application across major NEC-driven areas (like services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, equipment, special occupancies/conditions, and more). The Calculations portion emphasizes calculation-based topics (including load calculations, feeders and branch circuits, grounding/bonding conductors, and additional calculation-driven areas).

### Open Book Test

TDLR’s electrician exam information clearly states the examination is **OPEN BOOK** and that you will be allowed to reference the **National Electrical Code®, 2023 Edition**. PSI’s Candidate Information Bulletin confirms exams are open book and lists the permitted reference as the **National Electrical Code®, 2023 Edition**.

PSI’s bulletin also includes specific reference rules that matter for your test-day setup and your study routine:

- **What’s allowed:** any soft-bound edition of the NEC 2023 may be used; your NEC book may be highlighted/underlined and may contain notes *prior* to the exam session; NEC books may be tabbed with **permanent index tabs** manufactured and/or provided by the NEC book publisher.
- **What’s not allowed:** you may not write, highlight, underline, index, or mark the NEC during the exam session; you may not use homemade/DIY/non-NEC tabs; no additional paper (loose, taped, adhered, stapled, etc.) is allowed; loose-leaf/spiralbound/ring-bound NEC copies are not allowed; the NEC Handbook is not allowed.

### Licensing Steps

Journeyman licensing in Texas is managed by TDLR. While every applicant’s experience history is different, the typical path follows a clear sequence:

1. **Complete qualifying on-the-job training under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.**
TDLR states that to apply for a Journeyman Electrician license, you must have completed at least **7,000 hours** of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas. TDLR also explains that submitting at least 7,000 hours allows you to take the exam before reaching the full **8,000 hours required for licensure**.
2. **Collect signed experience verification.**
TDLR explains that your experience must be documented and signed by each supervising Master Electrician using an **Experience Verification Form**.
3. **Submit your initial application by mail with the required fee.**
TDLR states you must apply by mail for the initial license application and submit a completed Journeyman Electrician License Application Form with a **non-refundable $30 application fee**.
4. **Receive approval to test, then schedule your exam with PSI.**
TDLR explains that after your completed application has been received and verified, you’ll receive notification that you are approved to take the licensing exam, and PSI handles exam scheduling.
5. **Pass both written portions: NEC Knowledge and Calculations.**
PSI’s bulletin explains the Journeyman Electrician written exam is split into two portions and requires passing each portion with a minimum score of **70%**.
6. **Maintain your license.**
TDLR states electrician licenses are valid for **one year** from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Texas expects you to do more than recognize code language—you have to apply it. The most effective study plan trains the way the exam works: read the scenario, identify what the question is testing, locate the controlling NEC rule, and answer decisively. Because the Texas journeyman exam is split into two written portions, your prep should match that split.

### 1) Train for the NEC Knowledge Portion (navigation + application)

The NEC Knowledge portion is timed and built to test how well you can use the code as a reference. Strong candidates don’t “search everywhere.” They narrow down quickly:

- **Pull keywords first:** location, voltage, occupancy type, wiring method, equipment type, protection requirement, and any special conditions.
- **Go to the right neighborhood:** chapter, article, or section that controls that situation.
- **Read beyond the main rule:** exceptions and special conditions are often where the correct answer is decided.

With a tabbed codebook, your goal is speed to the right section, then accuracy in reading. Tabs are a tool—not a shortcut. When you practice the same lookups repeatedly, you stop wasting time and start confirming answers with confidence.

### 2) Build a reliable calculations workflow (for the Calculations Portion)

The Calculations portion separates the math-heavy questions so you can focus on problem-solving without bouncing between unrelated question types. The best approach is a consistent process you repeat every time:

- **Write the “ask” in plain language:** what does the question want—ampacity, overcurrent, conductor size, load, or another value?
- **List given information:** voltage, phase, conductor material assumptions, distance, load types, and any conditions.
- **Choose the method:** identify the correct NEC rule or calculation path that applies to the scenario.
- **Check reasonableness:** before you lock in the answer, make sure the result makes sense for the scenario.

Consistent practice helps reduce mistakes caused by rushing, misreading units, or skipping a key condition. When you train this workflow in a steady routine, the calculations portion becomes a place to earn points, not a place to lose time.

### 3) Build speed without sacrificing accuracy

Texas is timed, and the two-portion format means you need pacing for each section. Your practice sessions should include:

- **Timed code lookups** (not just untimed reading).
- **Timed calculation reps** with step-by-step checking.
- **Review of missed questions** by returning to the exact NEC location or calculation method and repeating it until it becomes familiar.

Speed comes from familiarity. Familiarity comes from repetition. And repetition comes from having the right tools and a plan you can actually follow.

### 4) Use your tabs the right way

PSI’s bulletin spells out tab rules for the NEC in the testing room, and that should shape how you study. The goal is to use tabs to reach the right area quickly, then use the NEC’s structure—headings, tables, exceptions, and cross-references—to locate the exact requirement. When your prep mirrors test-day rules, you remove surprises and build confidence with your materials.

## How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has two portions: 59 NEC knowledge items in 130 minutes and 26 calculation items in 110 minutes.
- The exam fee is $78 for both portions and the TDLR application fee is $30.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
- Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
