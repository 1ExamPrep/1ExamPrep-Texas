### Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The technical reference for the current examination is the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2026 Edition**. Candidates should become comfortable using the NEC efficiently before exam day.

Acceptable soft-bound NEC editions may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is not permitted as the examination reference.

The NEC may contain permitted highlighting, underlining, notes prepared before testing, and compliant permanent NEC index tabs. Candidates may not add new notes, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or other markings during the examination.

The NEC rental book included with this package is **highlighted and tabbed** for organized preparation. Using the prepared book throughout the **1 year of course access** can help candidates develop familiarity with commonly tested articles and tables.

An open-book exam still requires strong knowledge of the code structure. Candidates who recognize whether a question involves services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding, wiring methods, equipment, motors, or special installations can usually begin their reference search more efficiently.

### State Requirements

The standard Texas Journeyman Electrician licensing pathway requires a total of **8,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training** under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.

An applicant may become eligible to take the examination after documenting at least **7,000 qualifying hours**. The remaining hours must still be completed before the Journeyman Electrician license can be issued.

Qualifying experience must be documented and verified by the Master Electrician or Master Electricians who supervised the applicant.

Texas also recognizes approved education pathways that can affect examination eligibility. Candidates using an approved educational program must satisfy the requirements applicable to that pathway.

A licensed Texas Journeyman Electrician performs electrical work under the **general supervision of a Master Electrician**.

The Journeyman Electrician license is an individual credential. A Texas Electrical Contractor license is a separate business credential with separate requirements.

Texas electrician licenses are subject to annual renewal and applicable continuing education requirements.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Branch Circuits and Conductors:** This is one of the largest NEC Knowledge categories. Review conductor sizing, circuit ratings, continuous loads, overcurrent protection, required circuits, grounding, GFCI and AFCI requirements, and equipment supply rules.

**Electrical Wiring Methods and Materials:** Study raceways, cable systems, conductors, boxes, fittings, enclosures, securing and supporting, physical protection, and Chapter 3 installation requirements.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Review receptacles, switches, luminaires, appliances, HVAC equipment, panels, disconnects, working space, equipment ratings, and general installation rules.

**Electrical Services:** Study service conductors, service equipment, grounding and bonding, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, and separately derived systems.

**Electrical Feeders:** Review feeder loads, conductor sizing, grounded conductors, equipment grounding conductors, overcurrent protection, and related calculations.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 supports many exam subjects. Study grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, feeders, and separately derived systems.

**Conductor Ampacity:** Practice using NEC ampacity tables and applying terminal temperature limitations, adjustment factors, correction factors, insulation ratings, and other conductor requirements.

**Motors and Generators:** Study motor full-load current tables, conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, feeder requirements, and generator provisions.

**Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions:** Review specialized NEC requirements that apply to installations requiring rules beyond the general electrical chapters.

**Renewable Energy Technologies:** Review photovoltaic systems and electric vehicle charging equipment, including conductors, protection, disconnects, grounding, and equipment requirements.

**Electrical Calculations:** Practice service calculations, feeder calculations, branch-circuit calculations, conductor sizing, motor calculations, box fill, raceway fill, and other calculations relevant to the exam outline.

**Code Navigation:** Use the highlighted and tabbed 2026 NEC throughout preparation. Practice recognizing the subject first and then moving directly to the likely article, section, or table.

**Timed Practice:** The NEC Knowledge Portion provides 130 minutes for 59 total items, while the Calculations Portion provides 110 minutes for 26 total items. Practice each section under realistic time constraints.

**Study by Exam Weight:** Branch Circuits and Conductors, Wiring Methods and Materials, and Electrical Equipment and Devices each represent major portions of the NEC Knowledge exam. Give these areas regular attention while maintaining coverage of every tested category.

**Long-Term Preparation:** With **1 year of course access**, candidates can divide preparation into stages. Begin with general NEC familiarity, move into topic-specific study and calculations, and then use later study sessions for timed practice and reinforcement of weaker subjects.

## How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has two portions: 59 NEC knowledge items in 130 minutes and 26 calculation items in 110 minutes.
- The exam fee is $78 for both portions and the TDLR application fee is $30.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
- Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
