### Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Electrician examination is an **open book test**.

The approved examination reference is the **National Electrical Code, 2026 Edition**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own NEC book to the testing center.

Any soft-bound edition of the 2026 NEC may be used. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not allowed as the exam reference.

The NEC may be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes made before the examination session. Permanent index tabs manufactured or provided for use with the NEC may also be used.

Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or otherwise mark the NEC during the examination. Homemade tabs, non-NEC tabs, loose paper, taped-in sheets, stapled materials, or other additional paper are not permitted.

Open-book testing places a premium on navigation speed. Candidates should practice going directly to common articles instead of relying on a broad search for every question. Familiarity with Articles 100, 110, 210, 215, 220, 230, 240, 250, 300, 310, 314, 430, and other frequently used areas can make the examination more manageable.

### Licensing Steps

The Texas Journeyman Electrician license is administered through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. Candidates must complete the applicable experience and examination requirements before the license can be issued.

1. **Gain qualifying on-the-job electrical training.** Experience must be obtained under the supervision required by Texas law.
2. **Work under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.** Qualifying experience for the standard journeyman pathway is supervised by a licensed Master Electrician.
3. **Accumulate sufficient experience for examination eligibility.** Texas currently permits standard-pathway applicants to apply to take the examination after completing at least 7,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training.
4. **Complete the full experience requirement for licensure.** The standard Journeyman Electrician licensing pathway requires a total of 8,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training.
5. **Obtain experience verification.** Qualifying work must be documented using the applicable TDLR verification process.
6. **Submit the Journeyman Electrician application.** TDLR reviews applicant eligibility before examination authorization.
7. **Receive examination approval.** Approved candidates receive instructions for scheduling the examination through PSI.
8. **Prepare using the 2026 NEC.** Study both the NEC Knowledge and Calculations content outlines.
9. **Take both examination portions.** Complete the NEC Knowledge Portion and Calculations Portion within their separate time limits.
10. **Pass both portions.** A minimum score of 70% is required on each portion.
11. **Complete the remaining licensing requirements.** Finish the experience and licensing steps required by TDLR before license issuance.

### State Requirements

A Texas Journeyman Electrician is licensed to perform electrical work under the **general supervision of a Master Electrician**.

The standard licensing pathway requires a total of **8,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training** under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician. Texas currently allows applicants on the standard pathway to become eligible to take the examination after completing at least 7,000 hours, with the full experience requirement still applying before licensure is completed.

Texas also created an accelerated Journeyman Electrician Education Program pathway. Approved programs may provide qualifying credit toward the on-the-job training requirement and may affect examination eligibility for participants who satisfy the program rules.

Applicants must document qualifying experience through the forms and verification process required by TDLR. Experience should be accurately recorded and verified by the appropriate supervising Master Electrician.

Passing the examination does not by itself replace the experience requirement. Candidates must satisfy the applicable training, application, examination, and licensing requirements before receiving the Journeyman Electrician license.

Texas adopted the 2026 National Electrical Code as the state electrical code effective September 1, 2026, with a limited state exception affecting certain GFCI requirements for outdoor outlets serving HVAC equipment.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Definitions, Theory, and Plans:** Review Article 100 definitions, basic electrical theory, and plan-reading concepts. Candidates should understand voltage, current, resistance, power, conductor characteristics, symbols, and common electrical terminology.

**Services and Service Equipment:** Review Article 230 requirements for service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, service ratings, grounding, bonding, and temporary wiring. Service knowledge appears in both exam portions.

**Separately Derived Systems:** Study transformers and grounding and bonding requirements for separately derived systems, including system bonding jumpers and grounding electrode conductors.

**Feeders:** Review Article 215, feeder conductor sizing, grounded conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, and feeder load calculations.

**Branch Circuits:** Branch Circuits and Conductors account for 10 NEC Knowledge items. Study Article 210, required branch circuits, circuit ratings, receptacles, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, overcurrent protection, and conductor requirements.

**Load Calculations:** Practice Article 220 calculations involving lighting, receptacles, appliances, dwelling units, commercial loads, ranges, dryers, motors, heating, air conditioning, demand factors, feeders, and services.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Article 250 is a critical study area. Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, services, feeders, separately derived systems, and conductor sizing.

**Wiring Methods and Materials:** Approximately 10 NEC Knowledge items are devoted to this category. Study raceways, cable trays, boxes, enclosures, cabinets, panels, cables, cords, underground wiring, conductor support, and protection from physical damage.

**Boxes and Enclosures:** Practice box-fill calculations, conductor volume allowances, equipment grounding conductor allowances, and requirements for boxes and conduit bodies.

**Raceways:** Review raceway installation, conductor fill, support, bends, wet locations, underground systems, and Chapter 9 tables.

**Conductors:** Study Article 310 ampacity requirements, insulation types, temperature limitations, correction factors, adjustment factors, terminal limitations, and conductor sizing.

**Electrical Equipment and Devices:** Another 10 NEC Knowledge items cover receptacles, switches, luminaires, air-conditioning and refrigeration equipment, heating equipment, appliances, and workspace clearance.

**Motors and Generators:** Review Article 430 motor requirements, including full-load current, conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnecting means, and motor tables. Generator requirements should also be reviewed.

**Control Devices and Disconnecting Means:** Study equipment disconnect requirements, controller ratings, accessibility, location, grouping, and operation.

**Special Occupancies:** Prepare for Chapter 5 applications such as hazardous locations and other specialized occupancies.

**Pools and Similar Installations:** Review swimming pools, fountains, and similar electrical installations, including grounding, bonding, GFCI protection, wiring methods, and equipment locations.

**RVs, Mobile Homes, Park Trailers, and Marinas:** Study the special wiring and equipment requirements associated with these installations.

**Data Processing and Communications Equipment:** Review applicable requirements for communications and specialized electrical systems identified in the exam outline.

**Emergency Systems:** Become familiar with emergency and related special-condition requirements, transfer equipment, wiring, and system separation where applicable.

**Renewable Energy Technologies:** Review photovoltaic systems and electric vehicle charging equipment. These topics appear on both the NEC Knowledge and Calculations portions.

**Calculation Practice:** The separate Calculations Portion requires candidates to apply NEC rules numerically. Practice services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor sizing, grounding and bonding, raceways, boxes, electrical equipment, motors, and special installations.

**Time Management:** The NEC Knowledge Portion allows 130 minutes for 59 items, while the Calculations Portion allows 110 minutes for 26 items. Practice each type separately because unused time from one portion does not carry into the next.

## How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has two portions: 59 NEC knowledge items in 130 minutes and 26 calculation items in 110 minutes.
- The exam fee is $78 for both portions and the TDLR application fee is $30.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
- Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
