### Exam Details

The Texas Journeyman Electrician examination is administered by PSI for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. Applicants must first submit the required license application and experience documentation to TDLR and receive approval before scheduling the examination.

The current Texas Journeyman Electrician examination contains 80 scored questions. Candidates are allowed 240 minutes, or four hours, for the scored portion. The examination also includes five non-scored questions with an additional 20 minutes. A score of at least 70 percent is required to pass.

The examination content is divided among several major technical areas. Definitions, Calculations, Theory, and Plans account for six scored questions. Candidates should understand electrical terminology, fundamental theory, mathematical relationships, code definitions, and basic plan interpretation.

Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems account for nine scored questions. Preparation should include service calculations, service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, transformers, grounding, bonding, and separately derived systems.

Electrical Feeders account for three scored questions, while Branch Circuit Calculations and Conductors account for 15. Candidates should be comfortable with feeder loads, branch-circuit requirements, conductor ampacity, adjustment and correction factors, overcurrent protection, required circuits, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, and conductor sizing.

Electrical Wiring Methods and Electrical Materials represent the largest examination category with 18 scored questions. This makes raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, cabinets, enclosures, support requirements, underground installations, conductor installation, permitted wiring methods, and physical protection important areas of study.

Electrical Equipment and Devices account for 10 scored questions. Preparation can include receptacles, switches, luminaires, appliances, utilization equipment, heating and cooling equipment, panelboards, disconnecting means, and other equipment regulated by the NEC.

Motors and Generators account for five scored questions. Candidates should understand motor conductors, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnects, generators, and the calculations associated with these installations.

Electrical Control Devices and Disconnecting Means account for three scored questions. Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions account for nine, while Renewable Energy Technologies account for two.

Unless a test question specifically indicates otherwise, the current Texas examination instructs candidates to consider conductors as copper. This is an important testing convention when working through conductor-sizing and calculation questions.

The simulated practice exams included with this package help candidates apply these subject areas under exam-style conditions. Practice results can identify whether additional review is needed in calculations, wiring methods, services, branch circuits, motors, grounding, special occupancies, or code navigation.

### Open Book Test

The Texas Journeyman Electrician examination is an open-book examination. For examinations taken through August 31, 2026, candidates may reference the National Electrical Code, 2023 Edition, during the test.

Candidates are responsible for bringing their own compliant NEC book to the PSI testing center. The examination questions are based on the NEC edition adopted for the Texas testing program.

A soft-bound edition of the NEC is permitted. Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, and ring-bound copies are not permitted in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is also not permitted as a substitute for the approved code book.

The NEC book may be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes prepared before the examination. Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or otherwise mark the NEC after the examination session begins.

Permanent index tabs manufactured or provided for the NEC book are permitted under the current testing rules. Homemade, do-it-yourself, or non-NEC tabs are not permitted. Additional loose, taped, stapled, or adhered papers are also prohibited.

The NEC tabs included with this package provide candidates with an organized way to practice finding frequently tested subjects during preparation. Students should develop familiarity with Article 100 definitions, conductor tables, branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, equipment, and special occupancies.

An open-book exam still requires strong preparation. With 80 scored questions in four hours, candidates should know enough electrical theory and code structure to recognize the subject before attempting to locate the exact section. Efficient navigation can make a significant difference in time management.

Students should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, chapter structure, article numbering, tables, exceptions, informational notes, and cross-references. Reading an entire applicable section is important because exceptions and qualifying conditions may affect the final answer.

### Licensing Steps

Begin by gaining qualifying on-the-job electrical training under the supervision required by Texas law. A Texas Journeyman Electrician performs electrical work under the general supervision of a licensed Master Electrician.

Texas allows an applicant to become eligible to take the Journeyman Electrician examination after documenting at least 7,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas.

The 7,000-hour threshold allows the applicant to sit for the examination before completing the entire experience requirement. A full 8,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training is required before the Journeyman Electrician license can be issued.

Applicants must obtain experience verification from each Master Electrician who supervised the qualifying work. The required experience documentation must be completed and signed so TDLR can review the applicant’s eligibility.

Submit the Journeyman Electrician license application and required experience documentation to the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. TDLR reviews the application before approving the applicant to test.

After approval, schedule the Journeyman Electrician examination through PSI. Candidates should make certain that their personal information matches the information TDLR supplied to PSI.

Use the preparation period systematically. Begin with the NEC overview and organize the included 2023 NEC with compliant tabs. Become familiar with the major chapters and frequently tested articles before attempting full simulated exams.

Work through the Master Electrician Study Guide as an advanced supplemental resource and use the Calculations Study Guide regularly. Give particular attention to branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods, electrical equipment, services, and special installations because these subjects make up a substantial portion of the Texas examination.

Use flash cards for active recall between longer study sessions. Attend live instructor-led NEC training and use the recorded lessons to revisit difficult calculations, electrical theory, and code concepts.

Complete simulated practice exams under timed conditions. A four-hour target for 80 scored questions helps candidates develop realistic pacing for the official examination.

After earning the required passing score and completing the full 8,000 hours of qualifying experience, complete the remaining TDLR licensing requirements so the Journeyman Electrician license can be issued.

### State Requirements

Texas regulates electricians statewide through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. With limited statutory exemptions, individuals performing regulated electrical work in Texas must hold the appropriate electrical license.

A Texas Journeyman Electrician is authorized to perform electrical work under the general supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician. Electrical work must also be performed through the appropriate licensed electrical contractor structure when required by Texas law.

The standard experience requirement for Journeyman Electrician licensure is 8,000 hours of qualifying on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician. Examination eligibility may be granted after 7,000 qualifying hours have been documented.

Texas has also adopted an accelerated journeyman electrician education pathway for qualifying participants in an approved accelerated Journeyman Electrician Education Program. This newer pathway operates under separate TDLR rules and does not replace the standard experience route for applicants who are not participating in an approved program.

The 2023 National Electrical Code remains the Texas electrician examination reference through August 31, 2026. Effective September 1, 2026, Texas electrician licensing examinations will be referenced to the 2026 NEC.

Because this product is based on the 2023 NEC, candidates scheduled to test on or after September 1, 2026 should prepare with the 2026 NEC and the examination materials applicable to the new code cycle in addition to using this package for electrical theory, calculations, and general code-navigation development.

Texas electrician licenses are issued for one year and require annual renewal. Licensed electricians remain responsible for maintaining an active credential and complying with the continuing education and renewal requirements applicable to their license.

Municipalities may require registration of state-licensed electricians, but local registration does not replace the Texas Journeyman Electrician license. Electrical installations must also comply with the state-adopted electrical code and applicable permit and inspection requirements.

## How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has two portions: 59 NEC knowledge items in 130 minutes and 26 calculation items in 110 minutes.
- The exam fee is $78 for both portions and the TDLR application fee is $30.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
- Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
