### Open Book Test

You indicated your Georgia journeyman electrician exam is **open book**. In an open-book electrical exam environment, the NEC is not just a reference—it’s a tool you must use efficiently. Open book does not mean “easy.” It means you’re expected to:

- **Recognize the code topic fast:** wiring methods, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, box fill, raceways, motors, service calculations, special occupancies, and more.
- **Choose the fastest path to the answer:** sometimes that’s the index, sometimes it’s a chapter/article you already recognize, and sometimes it’s a table or definition.
- **Read precisely:** many missed questions happen because the test-taker stops reading before exceptions, notes, table footnotes, or specific conditions that change the correct answer.

This is where your **National Electrical Code 2023 Paperback with Tabs** becomes a major advantage. Tabs reduce friction and help you jump to the right NEC “neighborhood” quickly. They don’t replace the index—and they don’t replace understanding—but they can save valuable minutes by shortening the time it takes to get to the correct article area during timed practice and on exam day.

To get the most out of an open-book format, use this repeatable workflow while you study:

- **Step 1: Identify the keyword.** Translate the question into a code-search term (example: “box fill,” “ampacity,” “conduit fill,” “GFCI,” “bonding jumper,” “service sizing”).
- **Step 2: Find the location.** Use the index first if you’re unsure, or use tabs when you already recognize the article area.
- **Step 3: Confirm the rule.** Read the full requirement and scan for exceptions or conditions.
- **Step 4: Apply it to the scenario.** Make sure the situation in the question matches the conditions in the code section.
- **Step 5: Move on.** Don’t overthink once you’ve confirmed the rule.

The practice materials in this package are designed to train that process until it feels automatic.

## How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has two portions: 59 NEC knowledge items in 130 minutes and 26 calculation items in 110 minutes.
- The exam fee is $78 for both portions and the TDLR application fee is $30.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
- Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
