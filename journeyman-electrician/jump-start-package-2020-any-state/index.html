Open Book Test

You confirmed this is an open book testing approach. Open-book electrician exams reward one thing above all: code navigation skill. It’s not enough to own the NEC—you need to be able to use it efficiently while the clock is running.

Open-book success comes down to a repeatable process:

Licensing Steps

Journeyman licensing processes vary by jurisdiction, but most candidates move through a similar set of steps: complete required experience and/or education, submit documentation, schedule an exam, and pass within the required timeframe. If you’re preparing now, your priority is the portion you can control immediately: exam readiness.

A practical exam-prep-focused pathway looks like this:

  1. Confirm your jurisdiction’s journeyman exam requirements. Make sure you’re using the correct NEC edition and understand your testing format.
  2. Build a study schedule that includes practice exams. Reading alone isn’t enough—timed question practice is where performance improves.
  3. Train code navigation deliberately. Open-book doesn’t mean slow. Navigation speed is a performance skill.
  4. Drill calculations repeatedly. Calculations are often where candidates leak points. Repetition builds accuracy and confidence.
  5. Simulate the exam experience. Practice full-length sets so you learn pacing, focus, and endurance.
  6. Schedule your exam when your practice scores stabilize. Consistency matters more than one great practice run.

This Jump Start package supports each of those preparation steps with coordinated materials so your study sessions feel structured instead of scattered.

  • Study Guide (timed practice): Use the 12 open-book practice tests to develop a fast, repeatable code-lookup process. After each test, review missed questions by finding the correct NEC section and reading enough context to understand the rule—not just the answer.
  • Calculations Exams (math drills): Work through the 25-question calculation sets in short sessions, then build up to the 100-question final exam when you want a longer stamina check. Focus on writing clean steps and catching common mistakes early.
  • Online Success Kit (simulation + reinforcement): Use simulated exams to practice pacing and decision-making. The online format helps you repeat key practice sets and build comfort with exam-style pressure.

A simple weekly structure that works well for open-book journeyman prep is:

  1. 2–3 code navigation sessions: fast lookups by topic (build speed)
  2. 2 timed practice sessions: open-book tests (build rhythm and accuracy)
  3. 2 calculations sessions: short drills (build consistency)
  4. 1 simulation session: longer set or final exam-style practice (build stamina)

If you’re short on time, prioritize the two areas that typically create the biggest score swing: code navigation and calculations accuracy. Improving those even slightly can make your exam experience dramatically smoother.

How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

  • The PSI exam has two portions: 59 NEC knowledge items in 130 minutes and 26 calculation items in 110 minutes.
  • The exam fee is $78 for both portions and the TDLR application fee is $30.
  • TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
  • Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
  • The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
  • Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
