### Open Book Test

This package is designed for an **open book** testing environment where code lookups are part of the exam strategy. In Georgia, the journeyman reference materials may be used during the examination, while the bulletin notes that reference materials may not be used for the other plumbing exams.

The most effective open-book strategy is built around three skills:

- **Recognition:** identify what the question is really asking (definitions, sizing, materials, installation requirements, prohibited practices, or exceptions).
- **Location:** quickly determine where that topic lives in the code (chapter, section, table, or the index).
- **Confirmation:** verify the requirement in the exact code language, including any exceptions, footnotes, or related provisions.

The IPC is dense by design. Using it well is a learned skill. Tabs support the “location” step by helping you jump to the right chapter area faster, but the real advantage comes from consistent practice with the code until navigation becomes second nature.

Your study is most effective when you combine “know it” learning with “find it” practice. Journeyman plumbing exams are built around real-world plumbing knowledge and code-based decision-making, which means you’ll typically face questions that test your ability to:

- **Interpret code requirements correctly:** understand what is required, what is permitted, and what is prohibited.
- **Navigate drainage and venting rules:** trap and vent concepts, drainage system fundamentals, and code-driven installation requirements.
- **Work through water supply topics:** distribution concepts, fixture supply expectations, and code-driven provisions that impact installation.

To build speed for an open-book environment, practice with a simple routine:

- **Weak-area rotation:** focus on the chapters where you lose time—then repeat them until your search process speeds up.
- **Question-to-code mapping:** after each practice set, write down which IPC chapter/section answered each question. This builds your mental “map” of the book.

## How does the Journeyman Plumber exam work?

The Journeyman Plumber license comes from the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. You apply to TSBPE, then take the written exam at Pearson VUE and, where required, the practical in Austin or Waco.

- The written exam has 100 items in 120 minutes and costs $68.50 at Pearson VUE; the practical has 6 items in 240 minutes.
- TSBPE charges $40 to apply for the exam and $40 for the license.
- The Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners (TSBPE) licenses plumbers, not TDLR, and Pearson VUE gives the computer exams.
- The written exams are closed book: no books, papers or references are allowed in the room, so what you study is what you have.
- Journeyman and tradesman candidates also take a hands-on practical, held only in Austin and Waco.
- The current handbook points to the 2018 IPC with the 2018 IFGC, or the 2018 UPC; a move to the 2021 codes was proposed in July 2026.
