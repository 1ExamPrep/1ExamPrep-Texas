Exam Details

Journeyman plumber exams are designed to evaluate a candidate’s ability to work safely and compliantly under an adopted plumbing code. In an IPC-based test environment, questions commonly focus on how to interpret code language correctly and apply it to jobsite scenarios. You’ll typically see questions that require you to identify the correct code rule, confirm key details (including exceptions), and choose the best answer based on the code’s intent.

This package is built around the 2021 IPC and is intended for candidates preparing for journeyman-level testing that references IPC 2021 standards. The strongest prep approach is to combine concept learning with open-book practice, because many questions require more than finding a section—they require knowing which topic you’re in, what the code is trying to prevent, and how the rule applies to the scenario described.

Common journeyman-level focus areas when studying the IPC include:

  • General regulations and definitions: learning how code terms change the meaning of requirements.
  • Fixtures and fixture fittings: installation rules, connections, and fixture-related requirements.
  • Water supply and distribution: practical compliance questions that relate to supply systems and installation standards.
  • Sanitary drainage: how drainage systems are installed, protected, and kept functional.
  • Vents and venting principles: vent system logic, required provisions, and scenario-based application.
  • Tables and sizing references: learning to read and apply table-driven requirements without mistakes.

Open Book Test

You confirmed the exam is open book. That changes how you should study.

Open book rewards candidates who have a system. The goal is not to flip through pages hoping you land in the right spot. The goal is to build a repeatable process:

  • Identify the topic: determine whether the question is about drainage, venting, fixtures, water supply, definitions, or a table-driven requirement.
  • Choose the correct search method: go to the tabbed chapter area when you know the chapter, or go straight to the index when the question is phrased in trade language.
  • Confirm the exact requirement: read the rule carefully, then scan for exceptions, footnotes, and related provisions that change the answer.
  • Answer confidently and move on: avoid overthinking once you have confirmed the code requirement.

Licensing Steps

Journeyman plumber licensing is handled at the state level and typically requires an application process, eligibility review, and passing the licensing exam. In Georgia, plumbing licensure falls under the state construction licensing structure.

  1. Confirm the license level: Make sure you’re applying for the Journeyman Plumber credential (not a master-level classification).
  2. Complete the application process: Submit the required application for examination through the state’s licensing process.
  3. Receive approval to test: Once approved, you can schedule your exam according to the testing instructions provided.
  4. Prepare with the correct code and exam strategy: Build code comprehension, practice open-book navigation, and train for speed.
  5. Take the exam: Follow the exam-day rules for reference use, identification, and testing procedures.
  6. Finalize licensure steps after passing: Follow the state’s instructions for license issuance and any additional requirements.

A strong timeline is to begin study early enough to complete multiple rounds of practice: first learning the content, then building speed, then polishing accuracy on the chapters where you lose time.

Test Information and Study Materials

Most journeyman plumber candidates improve fastest when they stop studying “random pages” and start training like they’ll test. That means combining comprehension with navigation. Here’s how to use this package effectively in a real study plan:

  • Start with structure: Spend time learning the IPC layout—how chapters are organized, how sections are numbered, where tables are located, and how to use the index efficiently.
  • Build your topic map: Create a simple “where to look” map for yourself. For example, if a question mentions vents, you already know which IPC chapter area you’ll start in; if it’s a terminology question, you go straight to definitions and the index.
  • Practice code reading: Train yourself to read code language carefully. Pay attention to words like “shall,” “shall not,” “permitted,” and “prohibited.” Small words change answers.
  • Train exceptions and notes: Many candidates miss points not because they can’t find the rule, but because they stop reading before the exception, footnote, or note that changes the correct answer.
  • Get comfortable with tables: Table-based questions can be high-value on code exams because they test accuracy. Practice locating the right table and reading it correctly without rushing.

Use the Journeyman Plumber Exam Study Guide to reinforce concepts and build exam readiness. A good method is to do study-guide practice first (to learn how questions are framed), then immediately confirm every answer in the IPC. That “answer + verify” habit is what builds confidence for an open-book exam.

The Quick Reference Tabs become most valuable when you use them consistently from day one. They help reduce wasted motion and keep your study sessions focused on solving problems rather than searching for pages.

Simple practice routine that works well for open-book code exams:

  • Daily (10–20 minutes): Do navigation drills. Pick 10 terms or topics and locate the code section using the index, then confirm the rule by reading the section. Record the section numbers.
  • 3 times per week: Complete timed sets using your study guide. After each set, verify answers in the IPC and note which chapters cost you the most time.
  • Weekly review: Revisit your “slow chapters” and run dedicated drills until your lookups improve and your confidence increases.

How does the Journeyman Plumber exam work?

The Journeyman Plumber license comes from the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. You apply to TSBPE, then take the written exam at Pearson VUE and, where required, the practical in Austin or Waco.

  • The written exam has 100 items in 120 minutes and costs $68.50 at Pearson VUE; the practical has 6 items in 240 minutes.
  • TSBPE charges $40 to apply for the exam and $40 for the license.
  • The Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners (TSBPE) licenses plumbers, not TDLR, and Pearson VUE gives the computer exams.
  • The written exams are closed book: no books, papers or references are allowed in the room, so what you study is what you have.
  • Journeyman and tradesman candidates also take a hands-on practical, held only in Austin and Waco.
  • The current handbook points to the 2018 IPC with the 2018 IFGC, or the 2018 UPC; a move to the 2021 codes was proposed in July 2026.
