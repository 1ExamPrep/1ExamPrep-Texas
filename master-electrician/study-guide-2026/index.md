### Exam Details

Texas electrician licensing is overseen by the **Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR)**, and examinations are delivered through **PSI**. For the Master Electrician exam, the official Candidate Information Bulletin shows the exam is structured as **two separate portions**: an **NEC Knowledge** portion and a **Calculations** portion. Both portions are multiple choice, and you must pass **each portion** with a minimum score of **70%**.

- **NEC Knowledge Portion:** 75 items (including 5 non-scored items), 150 minutes, 70% correct to pass
- **Calculations Portion:** 33 items (including 3 non-scored items), 170 minutes, 70% correct to pass
- **Exam fee:** $78 for both portions (retake fee is also $78 if you fail a portion or both portions)

The content outline published by PSI highlights major areas that show up on the Master Electrician exam. On the NEC Knowledge side, this includes (among other areas) definitions/theory/plan reading, services and grounding/bonding, feeders and branch circuits, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, motors and generators, special occupancies/conditions, and renewable energy technologies. On the Calculations side, the outline emphasizes load calculations, grounding/bonding, transformers, feeders, branch circuit calculations and conductors, motors/generators, and related topics.

### Open Book Test

The Texas electrician examinations are **open book**. TDLR’s exam information page states the exam is open book and allows candidates to reference the **National Electrical Code (NEC), 2023 Edition**. PSI’s bulletin adds important details about what is and is not allowed in the testing room.

- You must bring your own **NEC 2023** to the test site (electronic notepads are provided).
- **Soft-bound** NEC editions are permitted.
- **Loose-leaf, spiralbound, or ring-bound** copies are **not** allowed.
- Your NEC may be **highlighted/underlined** and may contain **notes prior to the exam**, but you may **not** write or mark in it during the examination session.
- Tabs must be **permanent index tabs manufactured/provided by the NEC publisher**. Homemade or non-NEC tabs are not allowed.
- The **NEC Handbook** is **not** allowed in the exam room.

Because this is an open-book exam, a major part of passing is not just “knowing code”—it’s knowing *where* to find it fast, and how to interpret it correctly under time pressure. That’s why practice exams that force you to navigate and apply code rules are such a powerful way to study.

### Licensing Steps

Texas Master Electrician licensing begins with meeting TDLR’s experience requirements, submitting your application, receiving eligibility approval, and then scheduling your exam through PSI.

1. **Confirm experience eligibility.** TDLR requires Master Electrician applicants to have held a **Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years** and to document **12,000 hours of on-the-job training** under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.
2. **Collect verification.** TDLR requires experience to be documented and signed by supervisors using the required verification method/forms.
3. **Apply by mail with the application fee.** TDLR’s Master Electrician application page states that the **initial license application must be submitted by mail** and includes a **non-refundable $45 application fee**.
4. **Receive approval to test.** After your application is received and verified, TDLR notifies you that you are approved to sit for the licensing exam.
5. **Schedule with PSI.** Once eligible, PSI provides scheduling instructions; you can schedule online or by phone.
6. **Pass both exam portions.** You must pass the NEC Knowledge portion and the Calculations portion (minimum 70% each).
7. **Maintain your license.** TDLR notes electrician licenses are valid for **one year** and must be renewed annually.

### State Requirements

TDLR defines Master Electrician work broadly as installing, maintaining, or extending electrical wiring systems and associated equipment, including service entrance conductors. The state also notes that—aside from certain exemptions—anyone performing electrical work in Texas must be licensed.

For Master Electrician applicants, the key state requirements emphasized by TDLR include:

- **Journeyman held for at least two years** (Journeyman Industrial experience may not be used to qualify for the Master Electrician license)
- **12,000 hours** of supervised on-the-job training under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician
- **Mail-in initial application** with the required fee
- **TDLR approval** before scheduling and taking the licensing exam

### Test Information and Study Materials

Knowing the rules of the testing room can save your exam day from unnecessary stress. PSI’s Candidate Information Bulletin outlines common testing policies and what to expect at the computer-based exam site.

- **Two-portion structure:** Time does not roll over from one portion to the next, so pacing matters for each portion.
- **Non-scored items:** The exam includes non-scored questions, and time to answer them is included in the allotted time.
- **Calculator policy:** PSI allows only non-programmable, silent, battery-operated calculators with no paper tape printing and no alphabetic keyboard.
- **Identification:** You must present a valid government-issued ID, and your name must match what TDLR has on file.
- **Score reporting:** Scores are provided immediately after completion (on screen and via a printed score report).

## How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has two portions: 75 NEC knowledge items in 150 minutes and 33 calculation items in 170 minutes.
- The exam fee is $78 for both portions and the TDLR application fee is $45.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
- Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
