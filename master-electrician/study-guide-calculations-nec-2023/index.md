### Exam Details

Texas electrical licensing is regulated by the **Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR)**. TDLR requires eligibility approval before scheduling the licensing exam, and the exam is administered through **PSI**. The PSI Candidate Information Bulletin for Texas Electricians (updated April 20, 2026) explains that the Master Electrician written exam is split into **two portions**: **NEC Knowledge** and **Calculations**. You must pass **each portion** with at least **70%**. The time for one portion does not roll over to the other portion.

- **Exam structure:** Two portions (NEC Knowledge and Calculations)
- **Minimum passing score:** 70% on *each* portion
- **Master NEC Knowledge portion:** 75 items (including 5 non-scored items), 150 minutes
- **Master Calculations portion:** 33 items (including 3 non-scored items), 170 minutes
- **Exam fee:** $78 for both portions (and $78 to retake one or both portions, as described in the PSI bulletin)

TDLR also states that exam scores are provided at the test center and transmitted to TDLR electronically, and that candidates may retake the exam as many times as needed (with additional exam fees per attempt).

### Open Book Test

Texas makes it clear: the electrician examination is **OPEN BOOK**. TDLR states candidates may reference the **National Electrical Code®, 2023 Edition** and are responsible for bringing their own NEC to the test site. TDLR also notes that **soft-bound** editions are allowed, while **loose-leaf, spiralbound, or ring-bound** copies are not allowed.

The PSI Candidate Information Bulletin adds important details about what is and isn’t allowed with your NEC:

- **Allowed:** Any soft-bound NEC 2023 may be used; the NEC may be highlighted, underlined, and/or contain notes *prior* to the exam.
- **Allowed tabs:** Permanent index tabs manufactured and/or provided by the NEC book publisher.
- **Not allowed:** Writing, highlighting, underlining, indexing, or marking the NEC during the exam.
- **Not allowed:** Homemade/DIY/non-NEC tabs; any additional paper attached to the book; the NEC Handbook.

That “open-book” label doesn’t make the exam easy—it changes what the exam rewards. The top skill is **fast, accurate navigation**:

- **Find the topic quickly** using the index and the NEC’s structure
- **Confirm the exact condition** (many wrong answers are “almost right” but fail under one detail)
- **Use tables correctly** (right table, right column, right assumptions)
- **Move steadily** (no time sinks, no page-flipping panic)

### Licensing Steps

### State Requirements

TDLR publishes clear eligibility requirements for Master Electrician applicants. To apply for a Texas Master Electrician license, TDLR states you must:

- **Hold a Texas Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years** (TDLR also notes Journeyman Industrial Electrician experience may not be used to qualify for the Master Electrician license).
- **Show completion of 12,000 hours of on-the-job training** under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas.

TDLR explains that experience must be documented and signed by each supervising Master Electrician on an **Experience Verification Form**, and that TDLR may contact individuals to verify experience. TDLR also states that applicants must apply by mail for the initial license application and submit a completed Master Electrician License Application Form with a **non-refundable $45 application fee**. Electrician licenses are valid for **one year** from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually (renewals may be completed online).

These requirements tell you something important about the Master level in Texas: the state expects depth of hands-on experience *and* exam-ready competency. Your study routine should match that expectation—especially on service work, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, and special conditions.

### Test Information and Study Materials

The PSI Candidate Information Bulletin doesn’t just confirm the two-portion format—it also lists a detailed content outline for each portion. Use that outline to focus your prep where it counts.

**Master NEC Knowledge portion (75 items / 150 minutes):** The bulletin outlines topics including:

- **Definitions, Theory, and Plans** (including code definitions, electrical theory, and plan reading)
- **Electrical Services, Service Equipment, and Separately Derived Systems** (load knowledge, fault current, service equipment, temporary wiring, grounding and bonding, transformers)
- **Electrical Feeders** (feeder conductors, feeder grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection)
- **Branch Circuit Conductors** (GFCI/AFCI protection, branch circuit OCPD, conductors, grounding and bonding conductors)
- **Wiring Methods and Materials** (raceways, boxes/enclosures, cabinets/panels, cables/cords, underground wiring)
- **Electrical Equipment and Devices** (receptacles/switches, luminaires, HVAC equipment, appliances, workspace clearance)
- **Motors and Generators**
- **Control Devices and Disconnecting Means**
- **Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions** (including pools/fountains, RVs/mobile homes/marinas, data processing/communications equipment, emergency systems)
- **Renewable Energy Technologies** (including photovoltaics and electric vehicle chargers)

**Master Calculations portion (33 items / 170 minutes):** The bulletin outlines calculation coverage across many of the same categories, including services and separately derived systems, feeders, branch circuits, motors/generators, and more.

**How to study with this combo (a proven, electrician-friendly routine):1) Train your open-book workflow (NEC practice days)**
Open-book success comes from disciplined navigation. Practice with your NEC paperback in front of you:

- **Index first:** Build the habit of going to the index instead of flipping through chapters.
- **Use NEC language:** Search using the terms the code uses (this saves huge amounts of time).
- **Read “one level deeper”:** If the section lists conditions, exceptions, or references, slow down long enough to confirm them.
- **Table discipline:** Most table mistakes are column mistakes. Practice staying calm and verifying the correct column and assumptions.

**2) Build a repeatable calculation process (calculation practice days)**
Calculation questions become manageable when the setup becomes consistent. Use the Electrician Calculations Study Guide to train a standard method:

- **Translate the question:** What is the final output—amperes, conductor size, OCPD, load, or selection?
- **Collect conditions:** Voltage, phase, continuous load, distance, equipment type, and any special constraints.
- **Select your path:** Identify the correct code rule/table logic that applies to the problem type.
- **Compute cleanly:** Do the math without shortcuts that cause mistakes.
- **Verify the result:** Quick reasonableness check and a final code/table confirmation.

**3) Use a weekly schedule that’s realistic**

- **3 sessions/week:** NEC Knowledge practice (timed lookups + mixed questions)
- **2 sessions/week:** Calculations drills (repeat the same calculation types until setup speed improves)
- **1 session/week:** Missed-question review only (this is where scores climb fastest)

That last step is the difference-maker. Most people keep doing what they’re already good at. The fastest improvement comes from reworking what you missed until those weak topics become dependable.

## How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has two portions: 75 NEC knowledge items in 150 minutes and 33 calculation items in 170 minutes.
- The exam fee is $78 for both portions and the TDLR application fee is $45.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
- Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
