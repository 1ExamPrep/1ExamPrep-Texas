Exam Details

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) contracts with PSI to deliver electrician examinations. The Texas Master Electrician written exam is organized into two separate portions: NEC Knowledge and Calculations. Each portion is multiple-choice, and you must pass each portion with a minimum score of 70%.

  • NEC Knowledge Portion: 75 items (including 5 non-scored items) with 150 minutes to complete
  • Calculations Portion: 33 items (including 3 non-scored items) with 170 minutes to complete
  • Passing requirement: 70% correct to pass each portion
  • Exam fee: $78 for both portions (retake fee is $78 if you fail one portion or both)

The “two-portion” setup matters for your study plan. Even if you feel confident reading code, the calculations portion rewards a different skill set: steady math, correct assumptions, clean steps, and the ability to avoid small errors that cost points. Your prep should train both skills—code navigation and calculation execution—so neither portion becomes a surprise.

The exam content outline highlights major topic areas that commonly appear at the master level. While specific questions vary, your practice should routinely touch the kinds of work you see on real projects: services and equipment, grounding and bonding, feeders and branch circuits, wiring methods, devices and equipment, motors and generators, special occupancies and conditions, and more. Calculations practice often includes load and sizing work that depends on choosing the correct code references and applying them correctly.

Open Book Test

The examinations are open book. The allowed reference is the National Electrical Code (NEC), 2023 Edition, and the Texas Candidate Information Bulletin includes clear rules about what is allowed and what is not allowed in the exam room. The exam also states that effective October 1, 2023, examinations are referenced to the 2023 NEC Code Book.

What is allowed:

  • You are responsible for bringing your own NEC 2023 to the exam site.
  • Soft-bound editions are allowed.
  • Your NEC book may be highlighted, underlined, and/or contain notes prior to the exam session.
  • NEC books may be tabbed with permanent index tabs manufactured and/or provided by the NEC publisher.

What is not allowed:

  • You may not write, highlight, underline, index, or mark your NEC book during the examination session.
  • Homemade/DIY/non-NEC tabs are not permitted.
  • Loose-leaf, spiralbound, or ring-bound copies of the NEC book are not allowed in the examination room.
  • Additional paper (loose, taped, adhered, stapled, etc.) is not allowed in the exam room.
  • The NEC Handbook is not allowed in the examination room.

Open book does not mean easy—it means the exam rewards the electrician who can find the right rule quickly and interpret it accurately. A strong prep strategy focuses on NEC navigation: knowing where definitions live, how chapters are organized, how to use the index effectively, and how to move between articles, tables, and exceptions without losing time.

Licensing Steps

Texas Master Electrician licensing is handled through TDLR. In general, the process includes meeting experience requirements, submitting your application, receiving approval to test, scheduling through PSI, passing the exam portions, and then keeping your license current through renewal.

  1. Meet the experience requirements. TDLR’s Master Electrician requirements include holding a Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years and documenting 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.
  2. Document your experience. Experience must be verified as required by TDLR. Make sure your documentation is complete and matches how the state expects hours and supervision to be reported.
  3. Submit your initial application by mail. TDLR states you must apply by mail for the initial Master Electrician license application, and it includes a non-refundable $45 application fee.
  4. Receive eligibility approval. Once TDLR approves your eligibility, you receive instructions to schedule your exam.
  5. Schedule your exam with PSI. You’ll select a test center, date, and time through PSI’s scheduling process.
  6. Pass both portions of the exam. You must pass NEC Knowledge and Calculations at the required score level.
  7. Renew to keep your license active. TDLR notes electrician licenses are valid for one year and must be renewed annually.

Because the exam is open book and code-referenced, your documentation and eligibility steps are only one side of the process. The other side is building exam performance: timed problem solving, code lookup skills, and consistent accuracy across a wide range of NEC topics.

State Requirements

TDLR regulates electricians in Texas, and state guidance emphasizes that anyone who performs—or offers to perform—non-exempt electrical work in Texas must be licensed and perform that work through a licensed electrical contractor.

For Master Electrician applicants, the key state-level requirements and expectations typically include:

  • Experience and supervision: documented on-the-job hours under appropriate supervision, consistent with TDLR requirements
  • Journeyman status: meeting the required time holding the Journeyman Electrician license before applying
  • Application method: initial application submitted by mail with the stated fee
  • Exam approval and scheduling: eligibility approval before scheduling through PSI
  • Ongoing maintenance: annual renewal to keep licensing current

Master licensure reflects a higher level of responsibility, including planning, oversight, and code compliance decisions that affect safety and project outcomes. That’s why the exam looks beyond basic definitions and tests whether you can apply NEC requirements in real-world scenarios.

Test Information and Study Materials

Good preparation includes knowing what to expect at the test center and training yourself to work within the exam structure. The Texas Candidate Information Bulletin explains that both portions are multiple choice, and time does not roll over from one portion to the next. That means your pacing for NEC Knowledge is separate from your pacing for Calculations.

Build your study plan around two outcomes:

  • NEC Knowledge performance: locate the correct rule quickly, interpret it correctly, and avoid overthinking exceptions unless the question specifically directs you to consider them.
  • Calculation consistency: solve methodically, check units, confirm assumptions, and avoid “rushing errors” that turn correct knowledge into wrong answers.

How to train with 12 practice exams + 2 full finals:

  • Start with diagnostic practice. Take one practice exam early to identify weak areas. Don’t guess what to study—use your results to decide.
  • Create a miss-tracker. For every missed question, note the topic and the NEC area involved. Over time you’ll see patterns (grounding, motor rules, conductor sizing, special occupancies, etc.).
  • Practice code navigation on purpose. When you review, do a “second lookup” even if you got the question right. The goal is to shorten the time it takes you to confirm an answer.
  • Use timed sessions. Set a timer and work in blocks that match exam pressure. Timing practice helps you avoid getting stuck and teaches you when to move on.
  • Separate your study by portion. Some days should be code-heavy; other days should be calculation-focused. This mirrors the two-portion exam and prevents one skill from dragging down your score.
  • Save the finals for later. Treat the full final exams like dress rehearsals. Use your NEC book exactly as you plan to use it on test day.

Open-book success tips that matter for Texas:

  • Get comfortable with the index. The index can be faster than flipping chapters if you know how to search terms effectively.
  • Know where tables live. Many time-consuming questions involve tables. Practice locating them quickly and reading them correctly.
  • Reduce “hunt time.” The exam is timed. Every minute you spend searching is a minute you’re not answering questions.
  • Stay compliant. Use only NEC publisher-provided permanent index tabs. Avoid anything that risks non-compliance on exam day.

Most electricians don’t fail because they “don’t know electrical work.” They struggle because test-day pressure exposes gaps in navigation, pacing, or calculation accuracy. Practice exams help you train those exam-specific skills so your knowledge shows up when it counts.

FAQ Section

Is the Texas Master Electrician exam open book?

Yes. Texas electrician examinations are open book, and the allowed reference is the National Electrical Code (NEC), 2023 Edition, under the exam room rules described in the Texas Candidate Information Bulletin.

What NEC edition is used for the Texas exams?

The Candidate Information Bulletin states that, effective October 1, 2023, examinations are referenced to the 2023 NEC Code Book, and the allowed reference material is the National Electrical Code (NEC), 2023 Edition.

How is the Texas Master Electrician exam structured?

The Master Electrician written examination consists of two portions: NEC Knowledge and Calculations. Each portion is multiple choice, and you must pass each portion with a minimum score of 70%.

How many questions are on the Texas Master Electrician exam?

The NEC Knowledge portion has 75 items (including 5 non-scored items). The Calculations portion has 33 items (including 3 non-scored items).

How much time do I get for each portion?

You have 150 minutes for the NEC Knowledge portion and 170 minutes for the Calculations portion. Time does not roll over from one portion to the next.

What score do I need to pass?

You must pass each portion with a minimum score of 70%.

What NEC book formats are allowed in the exam room?

The Candidate Information Bulletin allows soft-bound NEC editions. It does not allow loose-leaf, spiralbound, or ring-bound copies.

Can I tab and highlight my NEC book?

Yes. The bulletin states your NEC book may be highlighted, underlined, and/or contain notes prior to the exam session. Tabs must be permanent index tabs manufactured and/or provided by the NEC publisher, and homemade/DIY tabs are not allowed.

Is the NEC Handbook allowed in the exam room?

No. The Candidate Information Bulletin states the NEC Handbook is not allowed in the examination room.

Do I have to pass both portions?

Yes. You must pass both portions—NEC Knowledge and Calculations—at the minimum required score.

How should I use the 2 full final exams?

Save them for late in your prep. Take them under timed conditions and use your NEC book exactly as you plan to use it on exam day. Then review results carefully to tighten up your final weak areas.

How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

  • The PSI exam has two portions: 75 NEC knowledge items in 150 minutes and 33 calculation items in 170 minutes.
  • The exam fee is $78 for both portions and the TDLR application fee is $45.
  • TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
  • Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
  • The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
  • Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
