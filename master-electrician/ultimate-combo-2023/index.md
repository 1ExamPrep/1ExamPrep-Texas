### Exam Details

The Texas Master Electrician written examination consists of two portions: NEC Knowledge and Calculations. Both portions are multiple choice, and each portion requires a minimum score of 70% to pass. Time from one portion does not roll over into the other portion, so candidates should practice managing each section independently.

The Texas Master Electrician NEC Knowledge portion includes 75 items, including 5 non-scored items. Candidates are allowed 150 minutes for this portion, and the passing score is 70%. The NEC Knowledge portion includes definitions, theory, plans, electrical services, service equipment, separately derived systems, electrical feeders, branch circuit conductors, wiring methods, wiring materials, electrical equipment, devices, motors, generators, control devices, disconnecting means, special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, and renewable energy technologies.

The Texas Master Electrician Calculations portion includes 33 items, including 3 non-scored items. Candidates are allowed 170 minutes for this portion, and the passing score is 70%. The Calculations portion includes calculations and theory, electrical services, service equipment, separately derived systems, feeder calculations, branch circuit calculations, conductors, wiring methods, electrical equipment, motors, generators, control devices, special occupancies, special conditions, and renewable energy technologies.

The exam bulletin explains that non-scored questions are used to help develop future examinations. These questions do not count toward the candidate’s score, and time to answer them is included in the time allowed. Candidates should answer every question carefully and avoid trying to identify which questions may be non-scored.

### Open Book Test

The Texas Master Electrician examination is open book. Candidates are allowed to reference the National Electrical Code, 2023 Edition during the examination. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own NEC book to the exam site, and electronic notepads are provided.

Texas exam rules allow any soft-bound edition of the National Electrical Code for the examination. Loose-leaf, spiralbound, and ring-bound copies of the NEC are not allowed in the examination room. The NEC Handbook is not allowed. Candidates should prepare their approved NEC book before exam day and make sure it meets the testing rules.

NEC books may be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes before the examination session. NEC books may also be tabbed with permanent index tabs manufactured or provided by the NEC book publisher. Candidates are not allowed to write, highlight, underline, index, or mark their NEC book during the exam session. Homemade, DIY, or non-NEC tabs are not permitted, and additional loose, taped, adhered, or stapled paper is not allowed in the examination room.

Open book does not mean easy. The exam still requires code familiarity, speed, accuracy, and clear understanding of electrical concepts. Candidates who are not comfortable using the NEC can lose valuable time searching for answers. ### Licensing Steps

The Texas Master Electrician licensing process begins with meeting the state’s experience qualifications. To apply for a Texas Master Electrician license, a candidate must have held a Journeyman Electrician license for at least 2 years. Journeyman Industrial Electrician experience may not be used to qualify for a Master Electrician license.

The candidate must also be able to show 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas. The experience must be documented and signed by the supervising Master Electrician on the required experience verification form. TDLR may contact individuals to verify the submitted experience.

After the completed application has been received and verified, the candidate is notified that they are approved to take the licensing exam. PSI administers the examination for TDLR. Candidates then schedule the Texas Master Electrician exam and complete the required portions.

After passing the required exam portions, the candidate continues through the licensing process with TDLR. Electrician licenses in Texas are valid for one year from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually. Candidates should keep their contact information current with TDLR and follow all state instructions for application, examination, licensure, and renewal.

### State Requirements

Texas electrical licensing is regulated by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. With limited exemptions, anyone who performs electrical work in Texas must be licensed. A Texas Master Electrician is licensed to install, maintain, or extend electrical wiring systems and the appurtenances, apparatus, or equipment used in connection with the use of electrical energy in, on, outside, or attached to a building, residence, structure, property, or premises, including service entrance conductors.

For the Texas Master Electrician license, the main qualification requirements include holding a Journeyman Electrician license for at least 2 years and documenting 12,000 hours of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas licensed Master Electrician. Candidates who have held a municipal or regional master electrician license may be able to verify their own on-the-job training for the years they held that license.

Texas electrician licenses are renewed annually. Electricians must complete continuing education for renewal. The continuing education requirement for electricians is 4 hours and includes National Electrical Code content as adopted by TDLR, state law and rules that regulate electrical work, and electrical safety. License holders are responsible for completing renewal requirements on time and following current TDLR rules.

## How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has two portions: 75 NEC knowledge items in 150 minutes and 33 calculation items in 170 minutes.
- The exam fee is $78 for both portions and the TDLR application fee is $45.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
- Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
