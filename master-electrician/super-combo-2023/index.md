### Exam Details

Texas electrician licensing is regulated by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR), and examinations are delivered through PSI. The Master Electrician written exam is structured as two separate portions: **NEC Knowledge** and **Calculations**. Both portions are multiple-choice, and you must pass **each portion** with a minimum score of **70%**. The exam is open book and is based on the **National Electrical Code (NEC), 2023 Edition**.

Texas Master Electrician exam structure (two portions):

- **NEC Knowledge Portion:** 75 items (including 5 non-scored items), 150 minutes, 70% correct to pass.
- **Calculations Portion:** 33 items (including 3 non-scored items), 170 minutes, 70% correct to pass.

Important pacing note: the time you finish in one portion does **not** roll over into the next portion. Treat each portion like its own timed event and train accordingly.

Exam fee: the Master Electrician examination fee is **$78** for both portions. Retake fees are also listed as **$78**.

Content emphasis is not random. PSI’s published outline shows the NEC Knowledge portion is weighted toward trade-relevant NEC areas, and the Calculations portion focuses heavily on service and system calculations, branch circuit and feeder calculations, and motor/generator work. ### Open Book Test

The Texas electrician examination is **OPEN BOOK**. You are allowed to reference the **National Electrical Code (NEC), 2023 Edition** during the exam, and candidates are responsible for bringing their own NEC book to the examination site.

Texas reference rules matter because open-book performance is largely a navigation skill:

- **Allowed:** Any soft-bound edition of the NEC may be used. Your NEC may be highlighted, underlined, and/or contain notes **before** the exam session.
- **Tabs:** NEC books may be tabbed with **permanent index tabs** manufactured and/or provided by the NEC publisher.
- **Not allowed during testing:** You may not write, highlight, underline, index, or mark your NEC during the exam session.
- **Binding rules:** Loose-leaf, spiral-bound, or ring-bound NEC copies are not allowed in the examination room.

This is why a tabbed NEC can be such a practical advantage: it supports a faster, cleaner workflow without relying on last-minute “page flipping.” The best way to use open-book is to practice an exam-ready routine:

- **Recognize the question type** (definition, wiring method, protection, equipment rule, grounding/bonding, motors, special occupancies, calculations, etc.).
- **Go directly to the likely NEC area** using tabs and familiarity with the Code’s layout.
- **Confirm the exact rule language** and apply it to the scenario being tested.
- **Answer and move**—avoid rereading entire sections when the question only needs one key requirement.

Open-book doesn’t reward slow reading. It rewards fast verification. This bundle is designed to help you train that skill until it becomes automatic.

### Licensing Steps

In Texas, Master Electrician licensing is managed through TDLR. While every applicant’s background is a little different, the overall path is straightforward: qualify, apply, get approved to test, pass the exam portions, and complete licensing requirements.

1. **Confirm you meet the Master Electrician qualifications.**
Texas requires Master applicants to hold a Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years and complete a required amount of on-the-job training under a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.
2. **Gather verification documents.**
You’ll need verification from each supervising Master Electrician who oversaw your on-the-job training. Texas uses an Experience Verification Form for supervisors to document this experience.
3. **Submit your Master Electrician application and fee to TDLR.**
Texas requires the initial Master Electrician license application to be submitted by mail with the non-refundable application fee.
4. **Receive exam approval notification.**
After TDLR receives and verifies your application, you’ll be notified that you are approved to take the licensing exam.
5. **Schedule both exam portions with PSI and pass each portion.**
The Master exam consists of NEC Knowledge and Calculations. You must pass both portions at the required score.
6. **Maintain and renew your license.**
Texas electrician licenses are valid for one year from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually (renewals can be completed online).

### State Requirements

TDLR outlines the qualifications required to apply for a Texas Master Electrician license. To apply, you must meet both of the following experience requirements:

- **Hold a Texas Journeyman Electrician license for at least two years.**
- **Complete 12,000 hours of on-the-job training** under the supervision of a Master Electrician licensed in Texas, with experience verified by each supervising Master Electrician.

TDLR also notes a restriction that matters for many applicants: **Journeyman Industrial Electrician experience may not be used to qualify** for a Master Electrician license.

Application and licensing details published by TDLR include:

- **Initial application method:** Apply by mail for the initial Master Electrician license application.
- **Application fee:** Non-refundable $45 application fee.
- **License term:** Electrician licenses are valid for one year and must be renewed annually; renewals may be completed online.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Texas Master Electrician testing is split into two portions for a reason: code knowledge and calculations are both essential, but they require different study tactics. The most effective prep uses both, consistently.

**1) Train NEC Knowledge like a skill, not a subject.**
The NEC Knowledge portion is multiple-choice and timed. The goal is to move from question to correct NEC location quickly. When you practice with your study guide, don’t stop at “right or wrong.” Always do a quick confirmation in the NEC so you learn where the answer lives and how the Code phrases it. Over time, your navigation becomes faster and your confidence gets steadier.

**2) Train calculations with a repeatable set-up.**
The calculations portion is where many candidates lose points due to small mistakes—units, misread values, skipped steps, or rushed arithmetic. The Calculations Study Guide is built to strengthen the process:

- **Write what you know** (given values, units, conditions).
- **Choose the right rule and method** before you touch the calculator.
- **Set it up cleanly** so your steps are easy to follow.
- **Sanity-check the result** before selecting the answer.

**3) Use flash cards to keep daily momentum.**
Flash cards are where consistency becomes easy. Ten minutes a day can reinforce definitions, core concepts, and “high-frequency” topics that show up often in Master-level testing. They’re also ideal for the final week when you’re tightening recall and reducing mental clutter.

**4) Practice in timed blocks that match the exam portions.**
Because time doesn’t roll over between portions, it helps to practice in realistic time windows. A simple weekly rhythm many working electricians can maintain:

- **3 days/week:** Master Study Guide + NEC lookups (60–90 minutes).
- **2 days/week:** Calculations Study Guide practice (45–75 minutes).
- **Daily:** Flash cards (10–15 minutes).
- **1 day/week:** Mixed session: short NEC set + short calculations set under time.

This keeps your prep balanced so you don’t become strong in one portion and shaky in the other.
