### Exam Details

Texas electrician examinations are delivered through **PSI** for the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). The **Texas Master Electrician** written exam is structured as **two separate portions**:

- **NEC Knowledge Portion**
- **Calculations Portion**

Both portions are multiple choice, and you must pass **each portion** with a minimum score of **70%**. Time from one portion does not roll over to the next portion, so it’s important to be ready for the pace of each section.

**Texas Master Electrician — NEC Knowledge Portion**

- **Number of items:** 75 items (including 5 non-scored items)
- **Time allowed:** 150 minutes
- **Passing requirement:** 70% correct to pass

**Texas Master Electrician — Calculations Portion**

- **Number of items:** 33 items (including 3 non-scored items)
- **Time allowed:** 170 minutes
- **Passing requirement:** 70% correct to pass

The published content outline for the Master exam spans major NEC knowledge areas such as definitions and theory, services and equipment, feeders, branch circuit conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, motors and generators, control devices and disconnecting means, special occupancies/conditions, and renewable energy technologies. The calculations portion emphasizes load calculations, grounding and bonding calculations, conductor and overcurrent topics, motors/generators calculations, and other math-driven applications tied to code rules.

Exam fees are set through the testing process and can vary by exam type. For the Master Electrician exam, the published fee for both portions is listed as **$78**, with the same amount shown for retake fees when a portion (or both portions) must be retaken.

### Open Book Test

Texas electrician examinations are published as **open book**, with the **National Electrical Code®, 2023 Edition** listed as the allowed reference material. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own NEC book to the examination site, and electronic notepads are provided at the testing center.

Because open-book does not mean “easy,” the exam rewards the ability to **navigate correctly**—fast. The code is still the code. The clock still moves. What changes is that your best strategy becomes: find the rule, confirm the exact wording, apply it, and move on.

**Published reference rules for the NEC book include:**

- **Soft-bound NEC editions are permitted** for the exam.
- **Loose-leaf, spiralbound, or ring-bound copies** of the NEC book are not allowed.
- Your NEC book may be **highlighted, underlined, and/or contain notes** prior to the exam session.
- NEC books may be tabbed with **permanent index tabs manufactured and/or provided by the NEC book publisher**.
- Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or mark the NEC book during the exam session.
- Homemade/DIY/non-NEC tabs are not permitted, and additional paper (loose, taped, adhered, stapled, etc.) is not allowed.
- The NEC Handbook is not allowed in the examination room.

The simplest way to study for an open-book code exam is to build your system early. Don’t wait until you’re “done studying” to start using your codebook like a tool. Use it daily while you practice questions, and learn the fastest routes to the sections that show up repeatedly.

### Licensing Steps

Licensing decisions and requirements are handled by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). While individual situations can vary, the Texas Master Electrician application path generally follows this structure:

1. **Hold a Texas Journeyman Electrician license** for the required period.
2. **Document qualifying on-the-job training** under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.
3. **Submit the Master Electrician license application** and application fee to TDLR (initial applications are submitted by mail).
4. **Receive approval to test** after TDLR reviews and verifies your application and experience documentation.
5. **Schedule your exam with PSI** and complete the Master Electrician exam (NEC Knowledge portion and Calculations portion).
6. **Receive your license** once exam and application requirements are satisfied.

Many candidates find it helpful to separate “license paperwork” from “exam performance” in their prep plan: handle experience verification and application logistics early, then keep your study time focused on code navigation and calculations.

### State Requirements

To apply for a Texas **Master Electrician** license, TDLR publishes experience qualifications that include both time as a licensed Journeyman Electrician and supervised on-the-job training.

**Published requirements include:**

- **Journeyman license requirement:** You must have held a Texas Journeyman Electrician license for at least **two years**.
- **On-the-job training requirement:** You must be able to show completion of **12,000 hours** of on-the-job training under the supervision of a Texas-licensed Master Electrician.
- **Experience verification:** Experience must be documented and signed by each supervisor on the required experience verification form(s).

TDLR also publishes application details, including that the initial Master Electrician license application is submitted by mail with a **non-refundable $45 application fee**. Electrician licenses are valid for **one year** from the date of issuance and must be renewed annually.

These requirements are important for planning because they affect when you can test. If you’re lining up your timeline, it helps to confirm you have your experience verification contacts in place so you can avoid delays on the front end.

## How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a TDLR license exam given by PSI. TDLR approves your application first, then you schedule with PSI and have one year from the application date to pass.

- The PSI exam has two portions: 75 NEC knowledge items in 150 minutes and 33 calculation items in 170 minutes.
- The exam fee is $78 for both portions and the TDLR application fee is $45.
- TDLR licenses Texas electricians, and PSI gives every exam with a 70 percent passing score on each portion.
- Since September 1, 2026, the exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, the edition Texas adopted on July 28, 2026.
- The exams are open book: bring a soft-bound NEC, highlighted and noted before the exam, with only the publisher's permanent index tabs. Homemade and other tabs are not allowed.
- Licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E.
