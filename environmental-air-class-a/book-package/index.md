### Open Book Test

The Texas Environmental Air Class A Contractor examination is an **open book test**.

PSI allows candidates to bring approved references from the official examination reference list. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own materials to the examination center.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and contain notes made **before** the examination session. Permanent index tabs are permitted. Temporary tabs, removable Post-it-style notes, homemade tabs, and loose additional paper are not permitted.

Candidates may not write, highlight, underline, index, or otherwise mark their reference materials during the examination session. This makes preparation of the books before exam day especially important.

Open-book testing does not mean every answer can be found through a simple keyword search. Some questions evaluate practical HVAC concepts, calculations, diagnostic knowledge, and common industry principles. Candidates should combine technical understanding with fast reference navigation.

For code questions, candidates should use the code edition identified for the examination. Effective September 1, 2026, Texas references the **2024 International Mechanical Code**, **2024 International Fuel Gas Code**, and **2024 Uniform Mechanical Code** for these examinations.

### State Requirements

Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor licensing is regulated by TDLR under the Texas Occupations Code and Texas Administrative Code.

The **Class A** license permits work on equipment of any size within the license holder's authorized endorsement. This distinguishes Class A from Class B, which has equipment-capacity limitations.

The **Environmental Air Conditioning** endorsement covers air treatment for human comfort, including control of temperature, humidity, cleanliness, ventilation, and circulation.

Applicants must establish qualifying practical experience before TDLR authorizes them to take the contractor examination. The standard experience pathway requires at least 48 months of practical experience under the supervision of a licensed air-conditioning and refrigeration contractor during the preceding 72 months.

A separate pathway is available for applicants who have held a technician certification for the preceding 12 months and have accumulated at least 36 months of practical experience during the preceding 48 months.

Applicants must complete the licensing requirements, including passing the appropriate examination, within the application eligibility period established by TDLR. After passing the examination, the applicant must satisfy the applicable insurance requirement before the contractor license is completed.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Air Distribution:** With 18 questions, air distribution is the largest examination category. Study airflow, duct installation, duct sizing, filtration, return and supply grilles, and static pressure. Manual D and a duct-sizing calculator are particularly useful for this area.

**Basic Refrigeration Components:** Review compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, and refrigerants. Understand what each component does and how it affects refrigeration-system operation.

**Combustion Air and Venting:** Use the mechanical and fuel-gas codes to review combustion-air requirements, appliance venting, vent assemblies, and safe operation of fuel-burning equipment.

**Controls:** Review general HVAC controls, pressure and safety controls, temperature controls, and how control systems interact with heating, cooling, and refrigeration equipment.

**Equipment Requirements and Design:** This 14-question category includes sensible and latent capacities, heat loads, HVAC codes, and voltage. Manual J and Manual S are important study resources for load calculations and equipment selection.

**HVAC Systems:** Study equipment components, installation requirements, refrigeration systems, and the interaction between individual components in complete heating and cooling systems.

**Piping:** Review condensate systems, fittings, pipe sizing, pipe types, support, and valves. Know where code requirements differ depending on system type and installation conditions.

**Refrigeration Principles:** Understand heat transfer, basic physics, and system pressures. Candidates should be comfortable applying refrigeration theory to practical service and diagnostic questions.

**Service and Testing:** These categories together account for 24 questions. Review electrical diagnosis, refrigerant recovery, superheat, subcooling, airflow measurement, combustion testing, electrical testing, and gas-system testing.

**Texas Laws and Rules:** Review Chapter 1302 of the Texas Occupations Code and Chapter 75 of the Texas Administrative Code. Know the licensing structure, contractor responsibilities, and regulatory provisions relevant to air-conditioning and refrigeration work.

**Reference Navigation:** Practice deciding which book controls a question before searching. Code questions should lead to the appropriate 2024 mechanical or fuel-gas reference, while design calculations may require Manual J, Manual D, Manual S, or the duct-sizing tool.
