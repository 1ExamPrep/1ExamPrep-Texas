Texas Contractor License: 15 Questions People Ask, Answered

Texas has no state general contractor license, but electricians, plumbers and A/C contractors are licensed by TDLR or TSBPE. Here are straight answers to the 15 questions Texans search most about getting licensed. Each answer is short, plain and checked against the official board and exam sources.

texas contractor licenseelectricalplumbingacr

Key takeaways

  • Texas has no statewide general contractor license; trades are licensed instead.
  • Electricians and A/C contractors are licensed by TDLR and test at PSI; plumbers are licensed by TSBPE and test at Pearson VUE.
  • Electrical and A/C exams are open book; Texas plumbing written exams are closed book.
  • An Electrical Contractor license costs $110 and needs a Master Electrician plus $300,000 per occurrence liability insurance.

Updated November 27, 2026 · prices checked against the 1 Exam Prep store

Texas does not license general contractors at the state level, so most builders need no state license at all. Electricians, plumbers and air conditioning contractors do: TDLR licenses electrical and A/C work and tests at PSI, while the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners (TSBPE) licenses plumbers. The answers below cover the 15 questions Texans search most.

Does Texas require a contractor license?

Only for certain trades. Texas has no statewide license for general contractors, remodelers or handymen, so a builder can bid most residential and commercial work without a state card. The trades that carry risk to life and property are licensed: electrical work through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR), plumbing through TSBPE, and air conditioning and refrigeration (ACR) through TDLR. Many cities add their own registration or permit rules on top, so a contractor with no state license may still need to register with the city before pulling permits. Check the building department in each city where you work.

Does Texas have a general contractor license?

No. Texas created a Texas Residential Construction Commission in 2003, but it was abolished in 2009, and nothing replaced it at the state level. Today a general contractor in Texas forms a business, carries insurance and registers with cities as needed. Some cities, such as San Antonio, require an ICC exam for certain trades, and contractors who also work in other states sometimes take the NASCLA Commercial General Building exam, which several neighboring states accept. If your work includes wiring, plumbing or HVAC, the person doing it needs the trade license even when the business itself does not.

Which contractor license do I need in Texas?

Match the license to the work you do with your own hands or supervise:

  • Electrical work: a TDLR license, from Residential Wireman or Journeyman Electrician up to Master Electrician. A business doing electrical work also needs a TDLR Electrical Contractor license.
  • Plumbing: a TSBPE license, from Tradesman Plumber-Limited to Journeyman and Master Plumber.
  • Air conditioning and refrigeration: a TDLR ACR contractor license in Class A or Class B.
  • Signs, code enforcement or city trades: check the city or TDLR program that covers that work.

How do I become a licensed electrician in Texas?

You build hours, apply to TDLR and pass a PSI exam. TDLR lets journeyman candidates apply and test after 7,000 hours of on-the-job training and issues the license at 8,000 hours. A Master Electrician needs 12,000 hours and at least 2 years holding a journeyman license. A Residential Wireman needs 4,000 hours. Every portion of the PSI exam needs 70 percent to pass, and you have one year from the application date to pass. Since September 1, 2026, Texas electrical exams are written to the 2026 National Electrical Code, so buy the 2026 edition before you study.

How do I get a Texas electrical contractor license?

Texas has no separate Master Electrical Contractor exam. An Electrical Contractor license from TDLR needs a licensed Master Electrician on the payroll, called the master of record. The license costs $110 a year and needs general liability insurance of $300,000 per occurrence and $600,000 aggregate. There is no business and law exam for it in Texas; the master's PSI exam is the only test. Most new contractors also form an LLC or corporation, which costs $300 at the Texas Secretary of State for a certificate of formation.

How long does it take to get a contractor license in Texas?

Most of the time is experience, not paperwork. A journeyman electrician needs 7,000 hours before testing, which is roughly 3.5 years of full-time work. An ACR contractor needs 48 months of practical experience in the last 72 months. Once you qualify, TDLR or TSBPE reviews the application, then you schedule the exam. Plan on a few weeks for approval and 4 to 8 weeks of focused study before the test date.

How hard is it to get a contractor license in Texas?

The paperwork is simple; the exams are the hard part. The electrical exams are long: the master exam runs 80 scored items in 240 minutes, about 3 minutes per question with the code book open. Candidates who fail usually know the trade but run out of time looking things up. Tabbing and highlighting the 2026 NEC before the exam, and practicing timed lookups, is what moves scores past 70 percent.

Is the Texas electrician exam open book?

Yes. You bring your own soft-bound 2026 NEC. Highlighting, underlining and notes made before the exam are allowed, but PSI only permits the publisher's permanent index tabs; homemade and third-party tabs are not allowed. That rule is why many candidates buy the NEC with factory tabs already applied rather than tabbing it themselves.

Do plumbers need a license in Texas?

Yes. Plumbers are licensed by TSBPE, not TDLR. A Journeyman Plumber needs 8,000 hours in the trade and a 48-hour approved course, then passes a written exam at Pearson VUE and a hands-on practical held only in Austin and Waco. Unlike electrical exams, the Texas plumbing written exams are closed book: no books or papers are allowed in the room, so study guides and practice exams are how you prepare.

Does Texas require a license for HVAC work?

Yes. TDLR licenses air conditioning and refrigeration contractors in two classes. Class B covers systems up to 25 tons of cooling and 1.5 million BTU per hour of heating; Class A has no limit. A contractor needs 48 months of practical experience in the last 72 months, pays a $115 application fee and passes an open-book PSI exam. Since September 1, 2026, the ACR exams reference the 2024 IMC, UMC and IFGC.

Does Texas require a contractor license for roofing?

Texas has no state roofing license. Roofers, like general contractors, register with cities where required and carry the insurance their clients and cities ask for. Some roofers choose a voluntary industry credential to stand out, but it is not required by the state. If a roofing job includes electrical or plumbing work, such as a rooftop unit, that part still needs a licensed trade.

When do you need a contractor license in Texas?

You need a state license whenever you perform or supervise electrical, plumbing or air conditioning work for others. You also need it to run a company that sells that work: an Electrical Contractor license or an ACR contractor license is issued to the business. Cities decide the rest. Before your first job in a new city, ask its building department whether you must register, show insurance or pass a local exam.

How long is a contractor license good for in Texas?

Texas electrical licenses renew every year with 4 hours of continuing education covering the NEC, Texas electrician law and rules, and NFPA 70E. ACR contractors complete 8 hours of continuing education each year, including 1 hour of Texas law and rules. Plumbing licenses renew through TSBPE on its own schedule. Let a license lapse too long and you may have to test again, so put the renewal date on your calendar.

Should contractors be licensed and insured?

Yes, where the law requires it, and insured in every case. An Electrical Contractor license needs $300,000 per occurrence liability coverage, and an ACR contractor license needs $300,000 per occurrence for Class A and $100,000 for Class B. Even trades with no state license, such as general building and roofing, are usually asked for proof of insurance by cities, lenders and commercial clients.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to a few more questions people ask about the exam and license.

Can I take the Texas electrician exam before I finish my hours?

Journeyman candidates can apply and test after 7,000 hours, but TDLR issues the license only at 8,000 hours.

Which NEC edition is on the Texas exam?

The 2026 NEC, since September 1, 2026. Older editions are no longer the exam reference.

Where are the Texas plumbing practical exams held?

Only in Austin and Waco, after the written exam at Pearson VUE.

Who gives the Texas plumbing exams?

Plumbing licenses come from the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners, and the written exams are given by Pearson VUE.

Are Texas plumbing exams open book?

No. Texas plumbing written exams are closed book, unlike the TDLR electrical and A/C exams.

What insurance does a Texas electrical contractor need?

An Electrical Contractor license needs a Master Electrician on staff and general liability insurance of at least $300,000 per occurrence.
